# 16

<!-- Tamil source: scan 67 / printed 65 -->

However many incidents we cite, however many speakers we describe as having planted the flag of fame—those who grow as speakers grow by shaping a distinct style of their own.

This article has been written with just one good intention—that it may be of some use to the success of their platform speaking!

As soon as I began writing this short series of articles, my dear friend Kanniyur Farooq, out of great enthusiasm, compiled and wrote down the views expressed by various foreign scholars about the art of speaking and sent them to me.

I would like to mention a few of them here:

“Write down exactly what you wish to say at a meeting and imagine that you must send it by telegram to Australia at your own expense. If you had to pay five rupees for each<!-- Tamil source: scan 68 / printed 66 --> word, would you not remove the unnecessary words? The necessary words that remain are the words fit for you to speak.”

“Smit”

“Only those without deep richness of thought keep needlessly lengthening a speech.”

“Montesquieu”

“The purpose of the art of speaking is not merely to state the truth—the important purpose is also to persuade the people.”

“Macaulay”

“On the speaking platform, do not speak of opponents with insulting, degrading and low accusations! Do not keep enlarging a small matter.”

“Disraeli”

“If you keep thinking constantly about the subject you have chosen to speak on, as a hen constantly sits brooding over its eggs, new ideas will leap out just as chicks emerge from the hen's eggs.”

“Brown”

“Those who wish to speak with polish and precision must often listen to the speeches of excellent speakers. They must read plenty of books and the thoughts of great people. If they cannot, it is better not to speak on the stage.”

“Chesterfield”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 69 / printed 67 -->

“If a man of eloquence who speaks wonderfully does not speak the truth, there can be no worse man than he.”

“Carlyle”

“The art of speaking should possess the special quality of being able to calm both the fury of the earth and the anger of a strong-armed warrior.”

“Shelley”

“Emotional expression is the basis of the flavour of words. The ears of one who knows nothing are much sharper than his eyes!”

“Shakespeare”

My friend A. Swaminathan has compiled and sent me a few maxims on the excellence of eloquence. They are as follows:

“The tongue is three inches long; yet it can kill a man six feet tall.”

Japanese proverb

“A woman's sword is her tongue. She never lets it become blunt.”

Chinese proverb

“Guard your tongue! You will guard your friend.”

Indian maxim

“A man is caught by his own tongue.”

Russian maxim

<!-- Tamil source: scan 70 / printed 68 -->

“The tongue has no bones. Yet it breaks bones.”

Albanian maxim

The views of great scholars and the maxims of many countries have stressed both the greatness of eloquence and how properly it must be used.

“Speak the word, knowing that there is no other word
capable of defeating that word.”

Valluvar states categorically that we must know that no other word can defeat our word—and, after examining and knowing this, expand our idea only with the word we have carefully chosen.

That is why he praises “the possession of the excellence called tongue-excellence”; he celebrates eloquence itself as a kind of wealth.
