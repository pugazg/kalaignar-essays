# 11

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 46; folio suppressed/inferred -->

“A speech must present its ideas clearly, in beautiful words, without violating logic, with experience and examples, so that it penetrates the listener's heart, arouses feeling, and makes the listener feel, ‘What is being said is true.’ That alone is excellent speech. That is also the grammar of speech.”

Professor A. Ki. Paranthamanar would put it beautifully in this way.

“Speech is an art; it possesses immense power; it has the strength to perform a threefold function. It is knowledge that turns speech into art. Platform speech enriches the country; refines the voter; reforms the Legislature; creates good government.”

Tamil-thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka. poured forth these words.

We must not forget that Thiru. Vi. Ka. was stressing not that all these things automatically happen through platform speech, but that platform speech must be of such a quality that it can bring them about.

If there is a music performance in the evening, the musician checks everything once in the morning with his troupe, either at home or at the lodge where they are staying.

If there is a play at night, even if the scenes have been performed many times in many places, the actors rehearse their important lines aloud in the morning<!-- Tamil source: scan 49 / printed 47 --> or evening.

In the same way, if speakers arrange the ideas they are going to present, think them through once or twice before going to the stage, and only then mount it, they can quickly reach the summit of success as good speakers.

Even someone who has acquired a fair degree of speaking ability may, in haste and anger, lose composure, become flustered before thousands of people, forget what he came to say, stumble, say what ought not to be said, and regret it later.

Many years ago a D.M.K. district conference was held in Nagercoil. Today's D.M.K. Deputy General Secretary Nanjilar and my friend John took the lead in conducting that conference. A speaker from Thanjai district spoke there. He is not in the Kazhagam now. I hear he is in another party.

To illustrate the poverty prevailing in the country, he used to collect and recount many incidents. At many meetings he had spoken, in exactly the sentence structure I have written here, of “eight respected comrades from Dindigul who died after eating kathaalai tubers.” But at the Nagercoil conference that day he said, “Eight comrades from Dindigul died after eating respected kathaalai tubers.” The word “respected” had jumped from the comrades<!-- Tamil source: scan 50 / printed 48 --> to the kathaalai tubers! That is what comes of speaking in haste!

Another speaker at the same conference! A man with poetic flair! Thinking he was mocking a literary speaker from the Congress Party, he himself became the object of ridicule at that meeting.

“That Congress literary speaker said Rama broke Janaka's bow at Ayodhyapuri in order to marry Sita! Poor fellow! That literary tiger does not even know whether Janaka's capital was Ayodhyapuri or Hastinapura!”

He mocked him like this! From the stage, Anna, I, Professor, Navalar, Sampath and Nanjilar all burst out laughing. By then our friend Aasai Thambi interrupted: “Why, man! Janaka's capital was Mithilapuri, Ayya!” Did that speaker leave it there? “Forgive me! In my memory of the Ramayana, I said it wrongly,” he told the people! The conference pavilion shook with laughter!

To string pearls of words along the thread of memory, a speaker needs great composure.

The great English scholar Addison was a celebrated figure in the eighteenth century. It is said that once, when he tried to speak, he stood there choking out “I conceive, conceive, conceive” three times! “Conceive” can mean “I think.” It can also mean “I am pregnant”!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 51 / printed 49 -->

While Addison was struggling like this, someone in front is said to have risen and joked, “Addison conceived three times! But he never gave birth to the child.”

Disraeli, the great English speaker, is said to have trembled with fear the first time he spoke in Parliament! “I would not fear to lead an army toward the battlefield; but when I first began to speak in Parliament, I trembled greatly,” he is said to have remarked. Beginning as a speaker with such a sense of defeat, he later steadied his mind, drove away fear, and planted the banner of fame as a great speaker.
