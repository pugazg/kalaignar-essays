# The Chapter of Driving Away the Tiger

<!-- Tamil source: scan 4 -->

★

A newspaper report appeared recently, describing an incident said to have taken place in a village forest in Kanwar district.

A tiger lying hidden in the bushes is said to have chased a bounding calf.

The mother cow saw it, they say. She turned on the tiger and chased it. With her horns, she tried to check the tiger's charge.

“The Cow That Chased the Tiger” is no joke! It really happened.

Now, cows that chase tigers can be seen all over the world!

Even a weakling sometimes settles the score. Even a coward becomes a hero.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 5 / Roman II -->

Weeping and weeping, the tears dry up. Wailing and wailing, the throat too closes up.

Only then does the helpless man trust the strength of his own arm. The result is this strange sight: a cow confronting a tiger!

Countries worn down to nothing but sinew and skin have ripped the arrogant apart with their own claws until their fury was spent.

The tiger has fallen; the cow has won; the calf has been saved.

This is a liberation uprising. The indifferent look of the oppressed is the herald that fixes the date for a liberation uprising.

Poor man—coward—simpleton—whatever sort he may be; once he has been crushed and crushed by suffering, the situation is that of the tiger and the cow.

If a tiger is driven away merely for harbouring venom against a cow, what if the fury of an elephant

<!-- Tamil source: scan 6 / Roman III -->

is roused?

If tiny little nations themselves have broken the arrogance of dominant powers, what will happen if a great nation—Dravida land—is pressed down by a dominant power?

The question mark itself must give the answer.

To save its calf, the cow has spat fire.

Small nations have risen roaring to defend their rights.

And here is the great nation—the proud Dravida—the fertile land that flourished—the fortress of heroes—the soil where warriors roamed—seeing its rights destroyed, it has surged up in fury.

In the saga of ideals called the Liberation Uprising, “The Chapter of Driving Away the Tiger” will be the opening chapter.

A liberation uprising does not concern itself with the outcome; nor does it proceed

<!-- Tamil source: scan 7 / Roman IV -->

by calculating victory and defeat.

Those who have been trampled will rise seething. Beside those who have risen in fury, those who do their duty will stand in ranks.

This is the chapter that follows “The Chapter of Driving Away the Tiger.”

It is in this chapter that letters have been dipped in blood and written, in many places. In many countries.
