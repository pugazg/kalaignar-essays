# Liberation Uprising

<!-- Tamil source: scan 8 -->

Liberation uprising! How much awakening there is in the very title. A cluster of feeling-laden words that gives warriors a quickening pulse and the discerning a sweetness; the very sound of it pours out a new vigour. Liberation uprising is no mere garland of letters; countless lives have been dedicated to that phrase! The heaps of severed sinews are immense! The number of crushed bones is beyond measure!

Liberation is the sweet harvest yielded by corpses sown in a mire of blood! The labour of cultivation undertaken for it is the uprising!

For the most part, liberation has never been won without uprising; anywhere; at any time!

In the nation—in the home—in the ways of life—uprisings have blossomed for liberation. A worm fallen into the fire writhes for liberation even knowing

<!-- Tamil source: scan 9 -->

that it will die! The insect caught in the frog's mouth, the frog trapped between the snake's fangs, the snake caught in Garuda's claws, Garuda caught in the wolf's gaze, the wolf fallen into the archer's sights—every one of them conducts a struggle for liberation, even while knowing for certain that death awaits!

Not only in everyday life; even the condition of the gods is the same. When we look at pictures concerning the gods, the shadow of a liberation uprising can be plainly seen spread across them. The bandicoot suffers, unable to bear Ganesha's swollen belly and enormous head. In a picture depicting Murugan's sacred form, the snake hanging from the peacock's leg conducts an uprising for liberation. The peacock, unable to carry Valli and Deivanai together, conducts a liberation uprising. Valli and Deivanai, unable to endure the strong embrace of Murugan's twelve arms, conduct a liberation uprising. Murugan—poor fellow—conducts an uprising to gain liberation from his devotees.

Thus, everywhere and without exception, the shadow of liberation uprising can be seen.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 10 -->

It may bear different names in different places; the name may vary according to language and place, but in essence it is liberation uprising all the same.

“In the prison called the world, I turn the oil-press called family. Is there no liberation from this?” cries the Vedantin.

He dreams that if he is liberated from this world there is another world where he may live in delight. Since that dream is mere fantasy, we do not approve of his liberation verse. We object to it as well.

The cat wages a struggle for the liberation of the caged parrot. Not out of affection for the parrot; out of eagerness to satisfy its hunger! So we do not welcome that terrifying cry of liberation. Among us, the parrot's liberation and the cat's hunger cannot become problems to be attended to at the same time.

Both the innocent prisoner and the murderer-prisoner desire liberation. Here, even the judgement of libera

<!-- Tamil source: scan 11 -->

tion may be delivered differently. What matters here is whose liberation is necessary; we cannot be bewitched merely by the beauty of the word “liberation.”

It is after thinking through all these questions that we place before ourselves the liberation uprisings drawn by world history and try to learn from them. I do not mean the lessons they directly preach; I mean the lessons we prepare for ourselves by probing and examining them.

Liberation uprising was born along with humankind. To put it in the manner of sages, man conducted an uprising for liberation even while crawling as an infant in the womb. That is not what I wish to speak of. What I mean is that whenever man changed—from one man, to two, to three, to many, to a society, to a nation—he had to fight for liberation in one form or another. Of those struggles, what I wish chiefly to explain here are struggles of nations! And in that explanation, we must pay attention not only to stories of two nations clashing, but also to the victories in which destructive gangs that arose within a nation were

<!-- Tamil source: scan 12 -->

defeated.

America—the treasure dug up by Columbus! A new country! Those who settled there were Englishmen. The chance blossomed for America to prosper through them and for them to prosper through America. At once Britain ordered them to pay taxes to it. They refused. They tried to free themselves from the authoritative tone that said they must pay tax. They resisted. They won. This too was a liberation uprising!

In the world divided into the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere, the Western Hemisphere has for the most part kept the eastern region—the East—in slavery; and still does. In days gone by, and in some regions even now, the East has continued to live enslaved, caught in the grip of imperialism, as colonies, and fallen into the intoxication of the Commonwealth. It continues so even now.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 13 -->

But in all those regions today, the desire for liberation can be seen at stages both ripened and budding.

Especially in Southeast Asia, on lands where the brutal feet of imperialism had been planted, we see scenes of blood-ablution being performed to wash away those marks of shame. Is it not clear that this is the sign of a growing desire for liberation?

The echo of these uprisings has not stopped at the fortifications of their respective countries. Piercing those fortifications, it has cast the flame of freedom into far-off lands. The very thought of the sparks thus kindled makes the heart rejoice. Even though many have been turned into graves—men bereft of their beloveds—widows—parents bereft of their darlings—orphans—helpless women—the homeless—beggars—every nation that carries in its heart the rich phrase “liberation uprising” and wages war is a spark that will burn away the life of evildoers. There is no doubt of it; not an atom of doubt.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 14 -->

Hunting grounds have become battlefields. They have become camps of warriors sounding the drum of liberation. Here is Indo China! Against French imperialism, the people of Indo China are waging guerrilla war. This country, with an area of 287,000 square miles and a population of 24 million—comprising Cochin China, Annam, Tonkin, Cambodia and Laos—is conducting a liberation uprising under the leader Dr. Ho Chi Minh against being caught in France's grip. French domination ties the puppet king Bao Dai and a few other dolls to its strings and tries to dam the thirst for liberation. From 1945 until now, the French eagle has spent 110,000 crore francs waging war in Indo China! For 1952 it has drawn up plans to spend 43,000 crore. It is calculated that this is 135 times the amount spent in 1945 alone.

Showing money—corpses—French imperialism makes the false calculation that it can crush Indo China's liberation up

<!-- Tamil source: scan 15 -->

rising. The young bulls who have tied the vow-band saying, “We care not even if we shed our lives,” stand before the world stage as messengers of liberation uprising! In Indo China!

The war of liberation that took place in Indonesia cannot be forgotten. Here the liberation uprising flared in the task of driving out the Dutchmen who had grown fat by taking sugar, petrol, rubber, tin and other goods for themselves! Indonesia has a largely Muslim population and three thousand small islands. On islands such as Java and Sumatra, the people waged a war of freedom and made a people's republic blossom. Dr. Sukarno became its leader. A new life budded through liberation uprising!

Madagascar is a land on the African coast. A place so far away that one might think the sounds of revolutions—uprisings—conches of freedom could never be heard there. Do you think a liberation uprising could take place there? It has taken place, comrades; it has taken place. The Malagasy people of Madagascar waged a war against French

<!-- Tamil source: scan 16 -->

colonial imperialism! Yes, a liberation uprising! What does this show? The domination of the arrogant—the swollen-headed pride of the conceited—can no longer endure anywhere; it must be smashed to dust and dust, does it not!

Libya, freed from colonialism, and Tunisia, rising to free itself—who can deny that all these are stirring lines in the song of liberation? The liberation uprising taking place in Tunisia for the distinct yet sweet life of the Arab people is being watched by the peoples of the world. It is winning the sympathy of progressive forces.

Big country or small country.... without distinction, the drum has sounded for cutting the fetters. Even in countries that have won liberation, we can see some oppressed peoples fighting for their liberation. We can hear cries for rights.

Burma, separated from India in 1937, then under Japanese domination for three years from 1942, later passing into British hands, and then

<!-- Tamil source: scan 17 -->

becoming an independent republic in 1948, now has the hill people called the Karens conducting an uprising demanding a separate land. Right or wrong, I mention this only to remind you of the many ways and places in which the desire for liberation springs forth.

Malaya is a country bound within the British colony, with the Sultan's rule kept as a puppet regime. There too the feeling of liberation has begun to flame among the people. In the struggle against reactionary forces, many have taken part even while accepting terrible punishments that embrace the scaffold of death.

In the American colony of the Philippines too we can see a liberation uprising!

The voice raised by the people of Ceylon demanding release from the Commonwealth has not failed to reach our ears. When Elizabeth, now Queen, had planned a tour while still bearing the title of Princess, the Ceylon government decided to give her a welcome. We cannot forget the uprising the people conducted in opposition to it.

Ireland, part of the British Isles, gained independence in 1937. In 1949 it removed even the nominal authority of the British

<!-- Tamil source: scan 18 -->

king and withdrew from the list of Commonwealth countries. How was this freedom won!.... Was it not the reward given by liberation uprising!....

The very name Iran brings oil wells to mind. The spark that fell into that oil became the fire in the hearts of the Iranian people; the drum of ideals sounded in the capital, Tehran. What else can we call the loud voice of the people's army resisting British-American intrigues but a liberation uprising? In whose country is whose marketplace? The heart of Persia thunders the question so.

In Sudan, over which Egypt—the land embraced by the Nile—and Britain exercise joint domination, we see a thirst for freedom arising.

Iceland, floating in the Atlantic, had been a ceremonial hall where the court of the Danish king was held until 1944. A referendum was conducted, and it planted the flag as an independent republic. Was that not the result of liberation uprising!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 19 -->

Thus, in every nook and corner of the world, wars of liberation have taken place in which the feet of brutes pressed into people's backs were hacked away at the knee.

It is no surprise that the French Revolution and the Russian Revolution, glittering in red letters on the scroll of history, appear as marvellous news that comforts and inspires an age of renaissance—and as the growl of thunder to religious fanatics.

A dog and a man fight over a piece of meat. The lords rage at the sight—not out of anger born of compassion for the man.... but from the fury that the man is snatching the dog's food! They send in cavalry. The leaping horses trample down the ribs of those who had gone there, rip open hungry bellies, make eyes bulge, and race on in terror. The French people met on the battlefield of liberation all those who greeted that sight with triumphant smiles and mad laughter. To the one who cried “Hunger!” they showed the Bastille prison. Against those who showed the Bastille, the war of liberation raised armies. And quickly! Very quickly!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 20 -->

The terrifying sway of Rasputin's beard, the grotesque furrows rising on his face, the horror flashing in his eyes—the Tsar regarded these as the counsel of an invaluable sage. The liberation uprising of the Russian people shattered into pieces the Tsar, the storm of his authority—the hurricane of dictatorship—the deceivers who clung to his tail—the arrogant—the palace-dwellers—the lords—the reactionary forces. There is never a time when that uprising is not spoken of on the world stage. The capitalist faints; the brute turns his back; even the fool lifts his head; even the coward crows when he hears the story of the Russian Revolution.

After the October Revolution in Russia and after the First World War ended, rays of awakening began to spread in China.

Against feudalism, imperialism, military frenzy and intoxication with power, workers rose in China.

It was only in 1921 that the Communist Party arose in China. At first, it became necessary for them to form a united front with the Kuomintang Party. In 1925, the libera

<!-- Tamil source: scan 21 -->

tion-uprising fighters who numbered two thousand became, by 1927, a force of three million. From 1937 to 1945 China and Japan were at war. In that war China gained liberation from Japan. It wore the garland of victory.

Chiang Kai-shek was the leader. Yes; the Kuomintang Party ruled. In 1946 the Kuomintang army was twice as large as the People's Liberation Army.

The Kuomintang Party ruled as a dictatorial party. The Liberation Army and the Kuomintang army came into conflict. The army of liberation uprising raised a loud cry for democracy free of dictatorship. That cry was ignored by the Kuomintang Party.

“We are the great party!”—that arrogance; “We are the ruling party!”—that conceit.

“We alone are the foremost party!”—with that stubborn obstinacy, Chiang Kai-shek moved as the pampered child of the capitalist class, doing as he pleased.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 22 -->

The Kuomintang leader thought America's affection and sidelong favour alone were enough. And so, without realizing it, he became cruel. The sea of people boiled over. Not ordinarily! Staking life itself!

In October 1949, the liberation uprising in China saw victory. A new life appeared. A new moon rose. Fulfilment overflowed! Chiang Kai-shek had to flee to Formosa. Mao Tse-tung became leader of the people. Yes; a messenger of renaissance. This is history known to everyone. But what lesson does this history give?

The Kuomintang Party fell!

Why?

Because the people disliked the party's name?

A fine name; Kuomintang Party means New Life Party. Would people say they disliked the name?

A delightful name.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 23 -->

A charming name of renaissance.

A word full of awakening.

A tasty word.

A string of letters that steals the heart.

New Life!

How could it fail to please! The people would have welcomed it with smiles curling on their faces and joy swelling in their hearts.

Yet they tried to destroy that party. Why? The man who had been its leader was no ordinary man!

Sun Yat-sen!

The lamp of China. The artist of reform. The star that showed the good path. He was once the leader of the New Life Party! The party in which he walked was ruined. The grove where the noble man wandered became a desert. A storm entered the garden where the good man had sat. The Kuomintang toppled. The war of liberation won.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 24 -->

Give your mind a little work! Draw back here the eyes that you had sent wandering through foreign lands. Turn here, for a moment, the gaze that had leapt into the pages of neighbouring countries.

The neighbouring country is China—and close by is Hindustan, called our ancient land!

In China the ruling party was the Kuomintang Party.

Here the ruling party is the Congress Party.

Kuomintang is a beautiful name.

Congress too is a beautiful name!

The leader of the Kuomintang was Sun Yat-sen.

The leader of the Congress was Mahatma Gandhi!

Chiang Kai-shek called himself Sun Yat-sen's “heir.”

The one who calls himself Gandhiji's “heir” is..........??

I do not wish to say!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 25 -->

The place Chiang Kai-shek went was Formosa.

Here! Poor thing; let no Formosa arise here.

When our eyes ranged across foreign lands, we saw liberation uprisings conducted against colonial imperialism, against the capitalist class, against exploiters living in luxury, against reactionary forces.

A kind of uprising like that is taking place in this subcontinent. That uprising is treated with contempt by the arrogant.

The paean “We want Pakistan” was sung—mocked by ears that would not listen—threatened with “Wretched people! Do not seek ruin”—and in the end the Muslims freed themselves from the Indian subcontinent and established a land of their own. Unquenchable zeal—unflagging uprising—unblocked struggle—on Islamic soil began to sound the sweet strain of the incomparable victory these had won.

Following that—no, comrades; it was we who began it—we trumpet that Dravida must be

<!-- Tamil source: scan 26 -->

separated from the North.

For this liberation uprising we have given not merely explanations; we have given the lives of many warriors.

The grave of Thalamuthu Natarajan. Tears shed at the grave.

The great warrior Udaiyarpalayam Velayutham, left swinging from a tree.

Fire scattered in Madurai.

Majeed's corpse.

The play of the bees.

Gunfire at Kundrathur.

A flood of blood in the streets of Kumbakonam.

A brave woman, pregnant, driven into the forest.

The red blood made to flow by the Madras cavalry unleashed upon warriors of principle.

The petty act of shaving heads.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 27 -->

Those struck by prohibitory orders.

Those scarred by baton blows.

Such ear-splitting tidings that make tears fall have found a place in the Dravida liberation uprising.

This is not caused by a thrill of desire merely to conduct an uprising.

We ask for a nation, demand separation, on the strength of the sentences of history: we once lived; we have fallen. Those who ruled are dead. Those born to rule ask for a country. This is called wrong. There are many who look upon a great question in a perverse—strange—and painful—yet playful manner.

“We must live joined to the Central Government. Only then can we remain a strong country.”

This is the argument of some.

The reasons we have shown for why we cannot live joined to the Central Government are beyond count.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 28 -->

We have made them themselves speak. Some among them said what we say, in another style. They still do.

When the Central Government growls that, though nine lakh tons of food are needed, it can give only six lakh, will not those here understand the meaning of our liberation uprising!

At the Sashtiapthapoorthi charitable-trust fund function of Alladi Ayyar, Ivor Jennings, Vice-Chancellor of the University of Ceylon—praised in The Hindu's review as a scholar who delivers lucid judgement—has said:

“That there are provisions in the Constitution of India that exercise excessive authority over provincial governments,”

“That a country with many kinds of divergent minds would be like sitting upon thorns,”

and “That under the Constitution of India, powers have been entrusted to the Hindus of the North.”

If he has said this, would he not then have thought

<!-- Tamil source: scan 29 -->

about the true form of the Dravida liberation uprising? Even those of little understanding will grasp it if they put their anger aside for a moment and begin to think, embracing “permission.”

When one hears that conches taken from the Madras seashore go to the North, are made into bangles, and are sold in the market streets of Dravida—and realizes that more than six lakh conches are sent away like this every year—could it fail to become clear that the Dravida separation uprising has meaning?

At Alwaye in the princely state of Travancore there is a chemical factory that makes fertilizer. Because it is small, they have opened a new and large chemical-fertilizer factory at Sindri in Bihar. Much of the gypsum—the lime-bearing material—for the ammonium sulphate made there comes from Trichy.

Yet, without any thought of expanding the small Alwaye factory, they conduct an inauguration for a new one at Sindri. Is that all?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 30 -->

There, industrial empires—here, people like roadside dry leaves. Tata, Bata and Birla sit astride the neck of the South and ride it. With money and office, with the evil course and treacherous intent of wearing Dravida down, they strengthen their domination; meanwhile the stainless tradition that would not bow to the enemy even at the mouth of death lies singing surrender at the sacred feet of the scorpion-tongued men who declare that Madras must not raise its head without Delhi's seal.

Though they collect taxes in crores, we are told to mingle merrily with the jackals who have no desire to revive a withering Dravida; men of poor understanding say so! Perverse debaters join them! The cruel too lend support! Is this cheerful news to those of us in the liberation uprising? No. Yet can we give up, fearing mischief-makers and Vibhishanas? Indo China stands before our eyes, reminding us of our duty. Indonesia, with a jasmine smile, tells us that effort will turn into achievement. China, which toppled the Kuomintang and planted its flag, pats us on the back: “Do not falter—march swiftly! Like a tiger! Like a storm!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 31 -->

Taking the victorious nations as guides, even remembering the valour of a nation that may have fallen in a liberation uprising, the liberation uprising here continues.

Today Indo China! Tomorrow it may become Indonesia!

Trying to stop a surging flood of waves with walls of a sand-house is children's work. A game!

If grown men themselves engage in it, then it is no longer called children's work; it becomes the work of frogs.

Many have entered precisely upon the work of building sand walls.

“Dravida is not even a Tamil word; therefore there must be no Dravida Nadu! Dravida is a word found in no book, anywhere; therefore there must be no Dravida Nadu!”

Such an objection rises—a sand-house!

In Manonmaniam Sundaram Pillai's poem, the honeyed word Dravida stands majestically.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 32 -->

At the shore temples of Mamallapuram, the words “carved in the Dravidian style” laugh at the short-sighted.

On the statue of U. V. Saminatha Ayyar standing at the entrance of the Government College by the Madras beach, the phrase “Dravida Vidyabhushanam” cries aloud.

In Tagore's national anthem, the line “Dravida Utkala Vanga” calls Dravida to mind!

Political performers who act as though they do not see what they see and do not hear what they hear jump and leap, their faces swelling, asking, “What is this Dravida?”

One can only feel pity when thinking of these Vibhishanas who would bring down the liberation uprising!

“If we separate from the Indian Union, can we survive? Will we not become a small country?”

Here comes the next sand-wall!

A small country, they say; it cannot live, say those whose minds are unclear!

Ireland, with thirty lakh people, may live separately, it seems. Five crore Dravidians may not live separately.

I give you another list. Look at countries that live separately! Tell me whether Dravida's demand for a land is unjust!!

People in the lakhs may live under governments of their own; ten thousand people may have a country of their own and live. Five crore people, it seems, must become orphans. And that is not all: Luxembourg, with an area of 999 square miles and two lakh people, lives separately.

We see another curiosity. Monaco, with an area of four square miles and a population of twenty thousand, remains separate on the Mediterranean shore!

A curiosity greater than curiosity: Vatican City in Italy, with a thousand people, lives separately under the Pope's rule. We, more than five crore people, are apparently not allowed to live. We must remain hirelings of the North.

The Jews, who wandered as nomads for two thousand years without a place to stay in the world, have obtained a separate country. Under the Balfour Declaration during the war of 1914–18, they began settling in Palestine; later those driven out by Hitler too came running, and finally in 1948 obtained in Palestine itself a separate country called Israel. A country for those who wan

<!-- Tamil source: scan 35 -->

dered as nomads; for those who once ruled a country, a condition lower than that of nomads!

Iceland, whose occupations are merely fishing and sheep-rearing, can establish an independent republic. But Dravida—the granary of rice; Dravida, whose pearls spread their glow through the streets of Egypt; Dravida that produces pepper; Dravida that yields gold; Dravida that gives rubber; Dravida that spreads the fragrance of sandalwood forests; Dravida whose shores pour forth conches;

Dravida, which has the five-hundred-mile-long Cauvery and a coastline 1,750 miles long; Dravida, which after Bombay has the greatest electrical facilities; Dravida, which holds fifty crore tons of coal beneath the earth in the Cuddalore-Vriddhachalam region; Dravida, which has locked away thirty crore tons of iron in Salem and Trichy districts; Dravida, which possesses right there chromite for separating iron and limestone; Dravida, which says there is manganese in Vizag; Dravida, which embraces Salem, the Nilgiris, Coimbatore and Malayalam, which yield mica;

<!-- Tamil source: scan 36 -->

Dravida, which yields aluminium in the Shevaroy Hills, the Nilgiris, the Palani Hills and Andhra; Dravida, which joyously gives thorium for making the atom bomb mixed into its sands—

it seems unfit to establish an independent republic! An island may live; Dravida may not!

They will approve Libya's liberation uprising. They will applaud Tunisia's war. Why, they will even support the language uprising taking place in Dacca.

But they will mock us shamelessly. New kinds of creatures!

In Burma the Karens demand a separate area. Here, the hill people of the northeastern frontier—the Nagas—ask for Nagastan. These two lakh people have readied themselves to wage a nonviolent struggle for Nagastan.

But we are treated with contempt by our own people. We are subjected to the slander of Hanumans. We are struck by the arrows of words from Sugrivas.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 37 -->

Just as Chiang Kai-shek thought: a great party—the first party—the only party—the one leadership—no other party should exist—there are many in the Congress who, from arrogant thought and conceited frenzy, hold the senseless doctrine that Congress alone is the party.

All those many have certainly been defeated in the South! Yes; in the election!

Among the lessons taught by the general election is this too: the South does not wish to remain joined to the North.

In the general election of the Indian Union, south of the Vindhyas, Congress has been defeated. What does this mean? Does it not mean that the South does not want Delhi! Campaigning against Congress rule took place throughout the country. Yet in the North Congress itself has won.

Some who opposed Congress have indeed won, but most among them are reactionary forces. What does this result explain?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 38 -->

The North is a land of prosperity. The South is a forest of poverty. Those who made this forest are the people of Delhi. Therefore the South has delivered its verdict that it wants no connection with them. Dravida does not wish to remain in the grip of the reactionary capitalist gang. This is what the election makes clear.

The northern circle wishes to behave as America's faithful child. Delhi domination. If one pays attention to the counsel scattered by those who parade into the Indian Union from America, this truth becomes clear. The American ambassador Bowles says foreign capital is absolutely essential to the prosperity of the Indian Union! The words poured out to reporters by Mr. Black, who came and went, are strange; dangerous too!

To a question carrying the idea, “Would not foreign money circulating here increase the Communist danger?” he answers in effect, “It is precisely to eliminate the Communist danger that I say that money must come here!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 39 -->

Mrs. Roosevelt, everywhere she spoke here, pointed anxiously to how Communism was to be eliminated.

In this manner—though its form is different—American invasions are taking place in the country with Delhi's support.

Russia comes forward to teach many kinds of training that would increase industrial development. At the industrial exhibition held recently in Bombay, Russia's achievements were praised by many. But this sacred jewel-land of Bharat is not ready to make use of them! Yes, Bharat Mata gone witless! Do not all these acts of Delhi appear as signs of some terrifying future?

But among us there are indeed some who have mastered the art of pretending not to know even what they know clearly!

“Do you know why Congress lost in the South?”

“They did not like Delhi's leadership.” That is us!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 40 -->

“The anti-national propaganda carried on by the Dravidian movement through cinema, drama, stage, school and book is the reason for Congress's defeat.”

That is them—poor fellows, broken by defeat.

The very pen that spilled ink declaring that the Dravidian movement could not live, that stabbing and slashing had broken out inside the fort, that it had split in two and the two would clash and destroy each other—that same pen now sheds tears and says that the Dravidian movement's opposition propaganda, its anti-national propaganda, was the reason for Congress's defeat!

To demand Dravida, it seems, is anti-national propaganda. Let that pass. But do those who say Congress was defeated by propaganda really speak truthfully?

Turn on the radio in the morning: Vande Mataram. In the afternoon: Jai Hind. In the evening: the song “Bharata Manikkodi.”

Go to a cinema for entertainment and there are

<!-- Tamil source: scan 41 -->

pictures. The South's warrior Ravana is burned at the North's Rama Navami; Nehru watches that celebration with delight; at Holi, many kinds of colours are poured on his face and Nehruji dances; Munshi plants a sapling, Prasad waters it, and the goat at Azad's house eats it—how many, how many such scenes in the newsreels!

Newspapers in splendid colours; posters that dazzle every street at election time.

A great party with propaganda power of this kind has, as far as the South is concerned, turned into the Kuomintang Party.

They say the cause is the propaganda of the opposition movement. Let them call it the echo of the liberation uprising; we will accept it. We are not people with the base desire merely to run an opposition movement. It was they who, under that name, made Draupadi dance in Kannagi pandals—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 42 -->

who passed resolutions solely to destroy good, solely to wipe out the teardrops, solely that no one should associate with us.

Under the name of an opposition conference, Vibhishanas arose to oppose the liberation uprising.

In Ravana's eyes even Rama is a little good. Vibhishana is the traitor. Traitors in the line of that traitor called us traitors. They say so even now.

We, it seems, are traitors to the nation. We, it seems, are traitors to the country. Asking for our country called Dravida, it seems, is treachery. The shouting of traitors!

From that age to this, we who shattered and crushed the selfish frenzy that wandered in madness in the name of caste, in the name of religion, in the name of God—thinking itself lofty—and who opened a way for Dravidian society to live, have been called traitors.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 43 -->

I predict, friends, that even the Tamil dictionary will undergo a change! The future dictionary is going to say: traitor means a pure man.

# There and Here
