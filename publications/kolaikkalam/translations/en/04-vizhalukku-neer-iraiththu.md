# Watering the Weeds...

---

<!-- Tamil source: scan 23 / printed numeral not visible -->

“Ah! What beauty!........A lovely pavilion that would scatter even a sculptor's imagination........From it, wonders of colour.... Oh! They carried my eyes away!

On an emerald cot lying there, she was like a statue of gold!

Her cool glance came first; bud-like teeth showed as though through a cracked pot; with a little smile, ‘Madana, come!’ she called in a cuckoo's voice, like the music of a veena, stealing my heart....That manner of calling....that sidelong glance....it simply bewitched me!

Was my state any different from that of a drunken man!....My heart was all excitement, throbbing......My feet hurriedly stepped forward!

‘Go close to her, touch that glass-like cheek with my hand...?’ one man said; and as the man beside him, bubbling with interest, showed all thirty-two teeth and asked, “Then what?”, the voice

<!-- Tamil source: scan 24 / printed 24 -->

choked, sorrow mingled with pity, pain swelled, and the man who had described the maiden and the mansion replied, “Then what! I suddenly woke up!” The condition of today's Congress comrades has come to resemble that ‘pitiful’ state........

Congress friends—true workers—once raised and thundered the song “Even if the sky crashes down upon the crown of the head” with fury, and the line “No fear” in a voice filled with pride and resonance....Their ‘pitch of valour’ has dropped from four-and-a-half kattai to half a kattai! It has fallen, and keeps falling! Why?

“Do not fall prey to foreign imperialist exploitation!” “Come running to cast off the English grip!” “Down with the demon of office and the traitor to his brother!”—with freedom as their one aim, Congress friends once raised a mighty cry. Their condition now resembles that of a man who, after seeing a youthful woman in a dream and losing himself in the shine of her body, her elegant coiffure and her heart-stealing form, opens his eyes and is stunned to find that nothing is there!

They thought that when their leaders said, ‘Once self-rule comes, good-rule will come,’ truly they and the country could live for ever in comfort without any kind of harm! But.......!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 25 / printed 25 -->

The troubles of life have not flown away; nor has the panic of hunger, hunger. Not only that! After freedom is said to have arrived, when hunger, starvation, suffering, want, poverty, distress and all the other afflictions are seen everywhere, it is no wonder that in one corner of the hearts of friends who truly dreamed of a good life there arises the thought, “Was it for this that we suffered so much?” It could hardly be otherwise!

And in a country that claimed its duty was to abolish the suffering of the poor—among them men of ideals, heroes of sacrifice, people who gave body, wealth and soul for the country—and its ideal was to abolish caste difference and Chandala-ness, they are bound to grieve when they see the Congress itself as a punctured cart, a rotten beam.

Instead of having in their hearts the thought, “We came to rule the country; we came to make the people's lives good lives,” office-mania—and the great greed to secure the platform of office even if everything must be given away for it—has become the very ideal of life of those called Congress leaders!

From the chapter of Prakasam's fall to the chapter of the attempt to bring Omandurar's rule to an end, within a year or two the splits inside a single party—and not only splits, but great quarrels

<!-- Tamil source: scan 26 / printed 26 -->

and fights—have grown so far that the chapters of the Congress Purana have turned into a farce!

“People like Sri Vaidyanatha Iyer because of class feeling, and some others because of their longing for ministerial office..........” writes none other than Chokkalingam, editor of ‘Dinasaari’; while ‘Bharata Devi’ sings a satirical song calling the method of choosing those who are to run the Legislative Assembly an ‘annual auction’. Just as people watching a good drama overflow with eagerness and ask, “What is the next scene?”—just as a man eager to see a circus trick bends again and again to look when the basket is covered: “Has the mango sprouted? What mango?”—the situation has come to this: “What is today's news?” “Who is the minister now, man?”—or rather, people treat it as a spectacle.

When he sees this rotten condition, this condition that makes the street-corners laugh, in the office-hunt of those called leaders, the heart of the Congress worker who has toiled and toiled to exhaustion in the name of an ‘ideal’ is bound to ache. He is bound to lament, ‘We poured water on weeds.’

When the very organisation of the party is the lid of the capitalist sewer, the drum of imperialism, are not ‘scenes that make the street-corners laugh’ bound to arise?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 27 / printed 27 -->

We who understood Marx's doctrine—“When a party gains political domination, if the foundation of that party is unsound, it will be surrounded by capitalists and self-seekers and become their slave”—were indeed expecting this ‘scene that makes the street-corners laugh’!

If the Congress workers who lower their heads, lower their pitch and suffer when they see this would put their ‘Congress devotion’ aside for a while and put thought to work, they would see clearly that Congress is an instrument of dalliance for the money-bags, a flower-garden that relieves the torments of Vadapathimangalam and Naadi Muthus, and a quarrel-house for Vaidyanatha Iyers.

Then, truly, their hearts will begin to turn the words “We will not rest from pouring water on weeds” into a song of pain.
