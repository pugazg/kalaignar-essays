# Raise the Heroic Cry!

---

<!-- Tamil source: scan 34 / printed numeral not visible -->

In the Indian political world—especially in the circles frequented by those seated on the throne of power—the cry that a powerful ‘Central Government!’ is needed has been rising for some time!

“Provincial feeling is a poisonous doctrine that will increase difference, division and separation.”

If that doctrine spreads in India, it will pave the way for Indian society to be shattered to pieces instead of becoming a powerful organisation.

Therefore, patriots! Men of sacrifice! Sons of Bharat Mata! Do not foster provincial feeling! Even if it grows, suppress it!

In this fashion Pandit Jawaharlal, praised as a political scholar and as the Premier of India, spoke in Coimbatore and in Delhi!

Our dear Achariyar, for his part, preaches that this feeling is not wanted now.

The sun of freedom rose in August! Not many years have passed! Yet already the broad-minded (!) are reading out a Viveka (!) Chintamani that this separatist mentality must not exist!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 35 / printed 35 -->

The economic condition seems about to collapse. Wherever one looks, there is only the cry of hunger! Starvation! Famine! Only sobs of suffering!

If everyone is to have food and clothing, abandon provincial feeling and labour for a powerful Central Government—Shanmugam Chettiar, who, from Vasishta's mouth, has received the rishi (!) titles ‘egalitarian Tamilian, clever Finance Minister’, keeps insisting on this again and again!

Why are so many people, with such great concern, girding their loins to save the Central Government?

For what reason, even after losing Bharat Mata in the east and the west, do they still render service to Bharat Mata?

Freedom has come! To what extent is the freedom that came useful to the people?

If we ask how far cooperation between the Central Government and the provincial governments has gained strength, at least some measure of the truth becomes clear to us!

An agitation demanding an Andhra province! Karnataka is being demanded! Kerala and Tamil Nadu likewise!

Bengal demands some parts of Bihar! In Bombay, the demand that certain parts be made Maharashtra is growing steadily!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 36 / printed 36 -->

The people of Assam province are dissatisfied with the Central Government! They lament that the Central Government inflicts cruelty greater than even a stepmother's cruelty!

It is in this very situation—at the very time when provincial separatist feeling is rising—that we demand a united Dravidian land! We speak of the formation of a Dravidian country; we state what its constituent parts are!

There is a vast difference between the separation of the Dravidian country that we demand and the agitation for provincial separation!

In support of our policy, many professors, many politicians and economic experts are themselves stressing the necessity of a South Indian Federation.

## Only the name changes; the idea is the same

In the present situation, a South Indian Federation—that is, the establishment of Dravidian land as a separate country—is possible! Not merely possible, but necessary too.

Only then will there be a way—and the means—for the many rare and great resources in South India to benefit South Indians themselves, the Dravidians themselves.

Many raw materials lie idle! Rivers and canals remain undeveloped, not adapted in ways suited to electric power,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 37 / printed 37 -->

and the growth of food production too has stagnated.

To develop and enrich all this—our wealth wasting away, unattended, under the Central Government's stepmotherly care—we need a separate Dravidian country (South Indian Federation)! We need it urgently, necessarily!

At this very time when we demand Dravidian land, those who have come to rule are speaking of linguistic provincial division!

It is indeed welcome news. But the question is: what permanent benefit are we going to obtain from that effort?

If linguistic provinces are formed, political gamblers may think that separatist feeling can be curbed to some extent; having thought so, they may even swell with pride that they can establish a powerful Central Government!

In this situation another sudden attack arises from the United Provinces.

“The Central Government exercises excessive domination over provincial financial matters. As a result, the provinces do not receive the finance they need—not even enough to protect themselves. India's political arrangement has been drawn up so that the Central Government collects in the provinces many lucrative taxes; this

<!-- Tamil source: scan 38 / printed 38 -->

is neither just nor proper; all the provinces must join together and set about doing what is necessary”—that is the sudden attack!

The United Provinces Government has made its position clear by calling all the provinces to its aid!

As things stand, this news may even have frightened the Brihaspatis who demand a powerful Central Government! For if the provinces unite and threaten the Central Government, it can no longer remain powerful or autocratic.

And that is not all. If there is a weak united India, Hindi or Hindustani cannot be brought in as the national language—as the Rashtra Bhasha. Why?

The provinces that have dared to resist on financial matters may also raise a dispute over language—the thought may arise!

Further, in many parts of India we can see a kind of dissatisfaction growing against the Central Government!

We know that the Central Government hobnobs with the leaders of Bombay province, and we know the injustice by which government posts have been given to a particular few!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 39 / printed 39 -->

We see an unavoidable split arising between the Central Government and the provincial governments; even in places where it has not arisen, we see the split beginning!

Seeing, in so many ways, those at the Centre trying to strengthen themselves and the provinces striving to obtain their respective rights, we—the Dravidians of the South—must have no difference of opinion that our country must become a separate country; if there is such a difference, we must wipe it out!

Those who belittle our agitation, our struggle, must either be people who do not know us well, or people who know us and yet have descended into mischief.

Those who supported Kabar Khan when he raised the cry that Pathanistan was needed in Pakistan; the nationalist comrades in Lower Bengal who stated emphatically that Bengali must be the language of government—what reason have they for opposing our demand?

Is it the apprehension that their glittering life will fade?

Is it anxiety that their autocracy will diminish—that it will be cut down? Is it grief?

Is it fear that their intoxication with power will clear—will certainly clear—must clear? Is it weariness?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 40 / printed 40 -->

Or is it the imperialist mentality that wants South India—Dravidian land—which has the means to live prosperously for ever, economically and in every other way, kept as a slave to the North, as a forest for the North's exploitation?

Why? Why should they deny the blossoming of our life?

Why should they block the path by which our life can prosper?

Should not the friends who boast of having driven out the white man who enslaved the Indian land and ruled it in a lust for power know this?

Knowing that domination, arrogance, police, armies and press power are not permanent, why this obstinacy?

To smash the arrogant man's obstinacy; to make our province (Dravidian country) live in complete freedom; to keep unwanted Hindi from entering; to enjoy our own wealth; to break the iron capitalist doctrine of the North that Birla or Bajaj or Tata or Dalmia nurture under the cloak of nationalism; to make way for equality; to obtain a joyous Dravidian land—raise the heroic cry: ‘Kaamaarkkung kudiyallom!’ ‘Dravidian land for the Dravidians!’
