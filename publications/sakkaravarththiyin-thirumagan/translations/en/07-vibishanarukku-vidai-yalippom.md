# Let Us Answer Vibhishana!

> **Translator identification/source note — not part of Kalaignar's text:** As established for this publication, Kalaignar's `ஆச்சாரியார்` is rendered **Achariyar** and refers to C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) only in translator/editorial identification. Where Kalaignar himself writes `இராஜாஜி`, the body correspondingly says **Rajaji**. The article uses `சிவஞானம்` and the mocking plural `சிவஞானங்கள்`; these are preserved as **Sivagnanam / Sivagnanams** without adding an external identification. A few unexplained source-specific forms are kept source-bearing rather than silently normalised: `தண்ட கண்ட` → **thanda-kanda**, `இனித்தவாயன்` → **Inithavayan**, and `ஆர்குஸிஸ்` → **Argusis**.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 43 / printed 41 -->

In recent times the Ramayana question has once again raised its head in the country. At a time when Ramayana story-discourses, plays and films had run their course and been forgotten, they have begun Ramayana propaganda again with a new twist. We can, to some extent, mark the beginning of this newly vigorous phase: we may reckon it from the time Achariyar was driven out of ministerial office.

Through government he tried to secure advancement for his own community and for the Bharata, Ramayana and Purana instruments that protect that community; determined to wipe out the very foundations of the Dravidian people without leaving a trace, the embodiment of deceit—the outer limit of intrigue—Achariyar set forth, drawing out the *muppuri*, the three-stranded sacred thread. Realising that what he intended would not be fulfilled completely, he has now taken up a new effort. The result of that new effort—the echo of that conspiracy—is the new lustre now being given to the Ramayana!

Antiquity—superstition—the thanda-kanda doctrine of God—a culture opposed to our culture: the world of reason that longs to level all these to the ground and create a new society spreading the light of rationalism; the soldiers of that intellectual world; those who offer sympathy and support to those fighters—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 44 / printed 42 -->

we say this is a time when every one of them must work with vigilance. Because there is no spear with which to strike us down—

**there is not much difference between the degraded fellow who sets out thinking, “If I cannot get a spear, at least I can take a stick, stab the eye and blind him,” and Achariyar, who, because authority slipped from his hand, dreams that at least with an epic he can make the Dravidian people blind! Certainly not!**

That is why the Kakapattar lineage leaps with delight to conduct “ear-ornament ceremonies,” “holy sacred-thread ceremonies,” “wedding festivities” and all the rest for the Bharata and Ramayana which, after their cough worsened at Erode, have reached the stage when the bier must be tied and the final journey begun! There is meaning in the desire and enthusiasm of that lineage—we admit it.

That lineage takes care that its people, its society, and that society's hereditary entitlement to a grand life without labour should all go on flourishing more and more with each passing day! A fox! It crouches in the bush with its tongue hanging out! Why? To live! A heron waits on the bank—why? To fill its belly! The fox and the heron wait in search of food—that concerns their hunger. But if a deer and a fish respectively were to become friends of the fox and the heron and make their own kind prey for them—could the others of their own kind refrain from anger? Forgive me, friends! Among deer and fish, such traitors cannot be found even in imagination! **I said it only as an example; that is all! Only among human beings can we find them. From Vibhishana of Ravana's age to the Sivagnanams of Rajaji's age, it is in human society alone that such people can be found!**

It is because the number of Vibhishanas has grown like this from age to age that this people still suffers, unable to obtain its rights—its own rightful claim to rule!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 45 / printed 43 -->

There may be some who spring up in fury because we call the Sivagnanams Vibhishanas! True enough; to those who compose songs praising Vibhishana as a great Alvar, a devotee of Rama, one who knew the path of righteousness and so on, there should be no reason to get angry if they themselves are called Vibhishana, should there? That is why we say it: they will joyfully accept the title Vibhishana! They will not rage! Their hearts will grow cool! If they do become angry—then they themselves have admitted that the name Vibhishana is stained! Therefore we may freely call Rama devotees—the carriers of the Ramayana—Vibhishanas. Valmiki, Kambar and the tail-bearers will nod in approval that it is a fitting name!

Comrade Sivagnanam has written an article in which the complete form of distilled Vibhishana-ness stands revealed—taking up the brief for the Ramayana, Rama, Kambar and the sage Valmiki! The more one reads it, the more pity rises.

We have heard the saying: as the king goes, so go the subjects. But the spectacle of Achariyar going one way and his armies of followers taking the same road into contradictory conduct—why, the more one thinks of it, the more it provokes laughter!

When Achariyar began writing *Chakravarthi's Son*, he stated firmly that “Rama is not God—not an incarnation.” Then, as he went on, he wrote—and continues to write—contradictory articles saying that Rama too is an incarnation. He repeatedly refers to Rama as an incarnation of Mahavishnu—as one born of God. Finally, even in the 3 October issue of *Kalki*, he laid a foundation for “incarnation” by writing: “In the Rama incarnation, the course of human nature and, together with it, an explanation of ordinary dharma proceed side by side.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 46 / printed 44 -->

But read below what Comrade Sivagnanam, setting out to attack Periyar Ramasamy, wrote in his article—and see in what way it stands against his guru Achariyar.

“It is a truth known even to religious believers that God does not take on a human body and be born in this earthly world.”

Disciple Sivagnanam thus supplies yet another contradiction to Guru Achariyar's contradictory argument. Sivagnanam also argues that “Rama was a man who lived as one ought to live—and they revered him as a god dwelling in heaven.” Having set out to find strength for Achariyar, he goes back to Achariyar's original path and, without even realising it, stumbles—poor man!

“E.V.Ra. laments, asking: ‘The Ramayana says that Dasaratha, king of Ayodhya, lived after marrying sixty thousand wives. Is this tolerable? Will womankind approve it?’ Until the period when the Ramayana was composed, the principle of one man for one woman had been followed in domestic life. But the corresponding principle of one woman for one man was not accepted. Yes, women's chastity was insisted upon, but men's chastity was not. Against this cruelty, the Ramayana teaches male chastity.

“Father Dasaratha married sixty thousand wives—the son Rama, however, was one who observed the vow of one wife. What a revolutionary change in the ethics of domestic life!”

All of the above was written by Comrade Sivagnanam himself! The life Rama and Sita lived is supposed to be the ethics of domestic life! A revolutionary change, it seems!

Because some fellow said something, Rama took his wife into the forest under a charge against her chastity, abandoned her there, and went on ruling the country—he, it seems, is the model of the ethics of domestic life!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 47 / printed 45 -->

She, who gave birth to a son named Lava in the forest, and later to a son named Kusha too by the grace of a rishi, and who spent her days in the forest without wishing to return and live with her husband—is supposed to be the holy woman who brought a revolutionary change into women's lives! Sivagnanam laments on, because the intoxication of Vibhishana-ness has gone to his head!

Younger brother Lakshmana, it seems, left his beautiful wife Urmila in the palace, and though married lived as a celibate in the forest, standing guard while his elder brother and sister-in-law lay asleep! Had Rama or Sita worried about him, could they have had the heart to revel and then close their eyes in sleep while Lakshmana stood guard? Is this the ethic of domestic life? A code even Inithavayan cannot accept! A ruinous ethic! Let us ask Achariyar himself whether Sivagnanam's argument is right!

“Sivagnanam says that the Ramayana was written precisely to preach the vow of one wife. If so, Achariyar! You called Rama an incarnation of Vishnu. If it is true that this Vishnu set out to preach the vow of one wife to the world, if it is right that he took the Rama incarnation for that very purpose, then why does that same Vishnu embrace two—Bhudevi and Sridevi? In the Krishna incarnation taken by that Vishnu, why does he sport with countless Gopis? Why is he the beloved of Bhama, Rukmini, Radha and others?” Sivagnanam's article serves only to make questions like these rise up towards Achariyar.

Sivagnanam argues that through the Ramayana chastity is upheld and the vow of one wife is protected. Through the Bharata—through the history of the woman who was wife to five men—what lesson will he say is being taught to the world? Who knows? What lesson will he say is obtained through the story of the god Kumara, who married two wives? We do not know! Achariyar has written—and Valmiki has written—that Ahalya knowingly united with Indra.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 46 -->

The Ramayana says that Rama freed that erring wife of the ascetic sage from her curse and stood worshipping her. And Sivagnanam says that this Ramayana has as its hero Rama, who is supposed to uphold the condition of chastity!

**Alas! Alas! Shame! Shame!**

“Rama was a northerner; therefore to make a fuss saying that Tamils must not worship him is nothing but a demonic frenzy of colour-hatred, not a struggle along the path of reason.”

Comrade Sivagnanam is the one who has hurled this golden(?) thought too! A human being acquires divine power and becomes worthy of worship—Rama became such a man—so what if he was a northerner? What is wrong in worshipping him?

Sivagnanam asks in this way. Sivagnanam, who refuses to give Potti Sriramulu's sacrifice the honour due to it because he was from the Telugu country, looks at us and says we are dancing a “demonic frenzy of colour-hatred”! Fortunately, through this at least, he admits the truth that there are different “colours”!

Sivagnanam, who angrily asks what is wrong in worshipping the northern Rama, will he shamefacedly admit that there are no northerners who worship Kannagi, who is revered as divine? Can the Sivagnanams deny that Aryan Sita has set foot in such a way that Kannagi's fame cannot spread properly even within Tamil Nadu?

Sivagnanam says that refusing to worship the northern Rama is colour-hatred. Is he then ready, without colour-hatred, to worship Western deities such as Apollo and Argusis?

Finally, Sivagnanam says that it was not Kambar who made Rama a god—that even Ilango Adigal, who wrote *Silappathikaram* earlier, referred to Rama as God.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 49 / printed 47 -->

What does it matter who said it first? Rama is not God; the Ramayana is not an epic of noble culture; people must not believe it and lose their reason—that is the argument of those with intelligence!

The Sivagnanams must remember this! The way Ilango Adigal refers to Rama and the way Kambar refers to him are different!

Kambar himself turns into an Alvar and worships and sings of Rama as God. Ilango Adigal, on the other hand, speaks of the minds of the people of that time, their conditions of life and their beliefs through the Kuravai song sung by the author. The Rama story existed even before Ilango Adigal composed *Silappathikaram*—but he did not wish to write a Ramayana like Kambar. He composed a work for the glory of Kannagi, a Tamil woman. Kambar, as though no historical story from Tamil Nadu were available to him, coated Valmiki's northern-language work in Tamil wax and earned blame—by making Rama a god and worshipping him!

Valmiki had no desire to write any history of Tamil Nadu! But Kambar wrote a northern history!

The Sivagnanams think that, in order to stress the principle of the “vow of one wife” and set out the rule of chastity, the Ramayana is more worthy than *Silappathikaram*. This is an idea born of the hereditary habit of carrying kavadi for the North! The Vibhishana doctrine that arose in Ravana's age! When will this doctrine disappear? When will the country attain well-being?

Is it only an empty dream that Rama devotees should gain clarity? Time alone must give the answer!
