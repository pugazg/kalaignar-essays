# Body and Feeling

> **Translator identification note — not part of Kalaignar's text:** As established for this publication, Kalaignar's recurring `ஆச்சாரியார்` refers to C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji). The translated body preserves Kalaignar's chosen label as **Acharya**.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 16 / printed 14 -->

“For twelve years—in bliss—Rama and Sita enjoyed pleasure in Ayodhya. Now, Bhagavan and Ishvari are going to experience directly, in bodies of flesh, all the troubles, sorrows, anger and passion that people in the world undergo.”

Do you understand, friends! The meaning-laden words of the leader of the priests! Acharya wants, right now, to draw a curtain over all the wrongs that Rama, the character in the *Ramayana*, is going to commit in the stages to come. In the previous issue (August 154), the genius Acharya did not stop with calling Rama, Sita and the others characters; he distinguished the two by saying that Sita's beauty was like the beauty of the Sita of the Deva-world, and presented Sita as a human woman. Chakravarthi, who began by writing categorically that Rama was only a prince—not God—now says that Bhagavan and Ishvari are going to experience directly, in bodies of flesh, all the troubles, sorrows, anger and passion suffered by people! Can Bhagavan drive his own wife into the forest? Can Bhagavan hide himself and kill a warrior? It is because he fears that such questions will arise hereafter that Acharya is trying to make Bhagavan subject to human nature. Even as he says this, look at the amusing contradiction that arises. Watch closely, friends, watch closely.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 17 / printed 15 -->

“For twelve years Rama and Sita lived in bliss and enjoyed pleasure in Ayodhya.”

Acharya himself has written it. Shall we too, for once, describe how and in what fashion that blissful pair born into royalty might have sung their love-song? That is not what I came to say! Do you know from what point in time Acharya says Bhagavan and Ishvari are going to experience troubles, anger, passion—everything that belongs to human nature? Read it again! “For twelve years Rama and Sita lived in bliss and enjoyed pleasure in Ayodhya. Now, Bhagavan and Ishvari are going to experience, in bodies of flesh, all the troubles, sorrows, anger and passion suffered by people in the world.”

The word “now” in this must be noticed. These two, who had been happy for twelve years, are apparently only from now on going to experience in bodies of flesh all the feelings of the people of the world.

If so, during those twelve years after Sita's marriage—and even before that, during Rama's boyhood—did feelings arising in the body never arise in them? Did they never become angry? Was it not in anger that he bent the Kodanda to kill Tataka? Can anyone say that Rama lived in bliss with his wife Sita for twelve years without passion arising? Will Rama himself say it? Will Lady Sita herself say, “I felt no passion”!

Then why must the Brahmin luminary Acharya say that only from now on Bhagavan and Ishvari are going to experience, in bodies of flesh, human feelings such as anger and passion? If so, were the feelings Rama and Sita experienced until now experienced in divine bodies? An explanation is needed—O great Vedic worthy, an explanation is needed!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 18 / printed 16 -->

“Whichever avatar I take, my divine play proceeds only with the body and feeling proper to that avatar—that is the explanation Bhagavan himself gave.”

Acharya writes like this in the August 8 issue: “Bhagavan took the Vamana avatar. We have even seen descriptions and pictures of that form. A very, very short form, a tiny form, a body below even human proportions—we have read that such a tiny form is the Vamana avatar. Do you know what it did? To destroy the might of Emperor Mahabali—it asked for three paces of land and, so the story goes, measured the whole world in two strides! Acharya says that whatever avatar Bhagavan takes, he conducts his divine play only with the body and feeling proper to that avatar. Look at this too!

<!-- The Tamil source opens the quotation above but has no visible closing quotation mark before the following paragraph. -->

Body and feeling, both, are apparently suited to the avatar taken—Bhagavan's own explanation, it seems. The lord of the Brahmin clan repeats it. Fine! Let us accept it! “Then how small must the feet of that tiny body called the Vamana avatar have been? With those tiny feet, how did Vamana-avatar measure the world?” Will people with sense not ask? Should not the genius Acharya answer them?

Bhagavan, it seems, receives the body and feeling suited to the avatar. He took the Varaha avatar—lifted up the earth and brought it back—received a pig-like body—fine! But the Purana is written to say that this pig acquired enough feeling to enjoy pleasure with Bhuma Devi. Is that the feeling suited to the avatar? We wish, with all humility, to ask Acharya.

## Coronation

Dasaratha announces in the assembly that a suitable day should be fixed for Rama's coronation. Then, for some reason, another thought comes to him—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 19 / printed 17 -->

and he decides that Rama's coronation should be held the very next day, under the Pushya star, and tells Rama so. Look at Acharya's explanation of why Dasaratha fixed the day in such haste.

“It appears that the promise Dasaratha gave Kekaya (Kaikeyi's father) when he married Kaikeyi came back to his mind and troubled him inwardly. Bharata is a very good man; he has great love and devotion for his elder brother. Therefore he will not pay heed to the condition attached to Kaikeyi's marriage. Even so, one must not trust the human mind. Dasaratha feared that even a good man's judgment may sometimes change.”

Do you understand, comrades! Dasaratha had promised that Bharata would be crowned. That was the condition imposed by King Kekaya, Kaikeyi's father, when Dasaratha wished to marry Kaikeyi, the woman who had helped him in battle! “Since you already have an elder wife, her son may come to the throne. If you promise that the crown will go to the son of my daughter Kaikeyi, you may marry her”—that was King Kekaya's condition. The very King Dasaratha who accepted it now begins, in frantic haste, to arrange the coronation of the elder son Rama while Bharata is away from town.

“Bharata is not in town. There is no time now to send word and bring him. It is not proper to hold up the affair waiting for his arrival.”

Dasaratha says this to Rama—Acharya has written it so. Comrades! Look where the plot first takes shape. Not with Kaikeyi; the plot takes shape with Dasaratha. Acharya, without even realising it himself, is forced to admit this.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 20 / printed 18 -->

“Rama had no desire to assume royal authority immediately. He regarded it only as a duty. If the king told him to accept it, fine; if not, he had no need of it—that was his view.”

This Rama—this man of rare virtue—look at how he joins the plot with his father. Acharya himself writes; read.

“Bharata, who regards his elder brother as his guru, is a dharmatma, a man with a pure heart and the power to restrain his desires. Even so, Dasaratha told Rama that a human being's mind does not always remain the same... and informed Vasishta that there was no need to wait for Bharata.

<!-- The Tamil source opens the quotation above but has no visible closing quotation mark before the following paragraph. -->

Rama too agreed to have himself crowned at the coronation ceremony without his brother Bharata. If Bharata came, it seems, he would remind Dasaratha of the promise made to Bharata's grandfather, King Kekaya, when Dasaratha married Bharata's mother, and would stop the coronation. Fearing that, in the conspiracy to crown Rama, sage Vasishta, the lord called Emperor Dasaratha, Bhagavan Ramachandramurti—all these people take part.

Dasaratha, it seems, was terrified that “some obstacle might arise”! Would he not be terrified? Of course he would! Would Dasaratha, who conspired to forget the condition and crown Rama before Bharata could return from out of town, not be terrified? That Mahavishnu should have been born as the son of such a Dasaratha—that is the wonder! Four sons born to divide Vishnu into shares! And a conspiracy among those very four! Sri Rama, incarnated in the larger share, becomes an accomplice to his father's conspiracy; he too, together with his wife, attends to all the preparations for the coronation!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 21 / printed 19 -->

The article Acharya has written in this issue has, without his even realising it, revealed one truth.

Dasaratha promised King Kekaya and married Kaikeyi. Now he has thrown that promise to the winds, plotted together with Rama, and planned to make Rama himself the one entitled to the crown. And Rama too is an accomplice—that is the truth!
