# Dasaratha Raja in the Grip of Kama-Raja!

> **Translator identification note — not part of Kalaignar's text:** As established for this publication, Kalaignar's recurring `ஆச்சாரியார்` refers to C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji). The translated body preserves Kalaignar's chosen label as **Achariyar**. The title and closing line preserve **Kama-Raja** for `காமராஜன்`, keeping the Tamil wordplay around `காமம்` (desire/lust) and `ராஜன்` (king), as well as its name-like form, rather than flattening it into a single explanatory English phrase.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 26 / printed 24 -->

After taking leave of his father, Rama immediately went to the inner apartments of Kausalya Devi. He went to tell his own mother the matter and receive her blessing. But the news had already reached Kausalya. The city was floating in a sea of joy. The women were filled with uncontainable excitement; men, women and children were all in boundless jubilation. People from the surrounding regions had already begun arriving in Ayodhya to witness the grand coronation festival.

Rama and Sita meditated upon Narayana in their dwelling, undertook the rites for the next day's coronation, spread grass on the floor and lay down there that night.

All that appears above is what Achariyar recorded in the issue dated 8 August 1954.

People from neighbouring towns had already reached Ayodhya after hearing the news of the coronation—but Kaikeyi alone had not been told. Kaikeyi hears the news through Manthara and is delighted. Then Manthara reminds her of the promise Dasaratha had given Kekaya and puts into her mind the idea of crowning Bharata.

We had already shown, from Achariyar's own writing, that Dasaratha's speaking to Rama and the two of them beginning the arrangements before Bharata returned, and Kaikeyi's not being informed,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 27 / printed 25 -->

were themselves echoes of the conspiracy plan.

Only now does Dasaratha come to the residence of Kaikeyi, whose mind has been turned by Manthara. This time Achariyar describes it in the issue dated 22 August 1954!

“After completing all the affairs in the assembly and fully issuing every order for what had to be done for the grand coronation festival, Dasaratha, pleased that there was now nothing more to worry about, set out towards the inner apartments of his beloved wife, Kaikeyi Devi.

The festival would begin at daybreak; until then, with the thought that he could immerse himself in pleasure to his heart's content, pure-hearted Dasaratha entered the inner apartments.”

Pure-hearted Dasaratha, it seems, comes to Kaikeyi's apartments to immerse himself in pleasure to his heart's content—and that too until dawn! What sort of man is he? Pure-hearted, it seems! Not only that. Achariyar makes him tell us, in his own mouth, what condition he is in. Read.

“Now my body has grown weak because of old age. Following the path of my forefathers, I too wish to spend the remainder of my life in the forest.”

Achariyar wrote on 8 August 1954 that this was the reason Dasaratha said he was going to crown Rama.

Old age of the body—weakness—the age at which he ought to spend the rest of his life in the forest—and with all this, this very Dasaratha, it seems, enters Kaikeyi's inner apartments in a frenzy of “let me immerse myself in pleasure to my heart's content until dawn”! Not we; the old Achariyar himself says so! And Achariyar goes on to tell us the wonder of how the old Dasaratha's speed increased. Listen!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 28 / printed 26 -->

“In the palace, perfumes and special drinks were all kept, as usual, in their proper places. His physical urge intensified.”

What special drinks there were! How exactly that physical urge intensified—perhaps Achariyar alone knows!

“Kaikeyi was the wife specially set apart for pleasure. The moment he entered her inner apartments, a surge of passion would fill the king's whole heart.”

Have you seen how the old ex-minister has glorified the worthiness of this old king—old age, weakness, the stage for forest retirement and all? The moment he enters the inner apartments, passion surges, it seems! Ah, old men! Old men!!

He has gone inside. Then—

“Kaikeyi did not even look up when she saw the king. The poor king could make nothing of it. How could he? There was not the slightest taint in his heart.”

Pure-hearted—poor innocent—without taint: this is how Achariyar paints Dasaratha's portrait.

Having sworn to King Kekaya, “I will marry your young daughter and will not betray her—I will crown the son born to her,” this Dasaratha breaks that pledge and enters into a cunning plot to crown Rama. And this Dasaratha, it seems, is a taintless, pure-hearted innocent! Achariyar says so! An innocent indeed, Achariyar. An innocent indeed! King Kekaya should be the one to say: “Ah—sinner!” The leader of the Puranic crowd that proclaims to the world Harischandra's refusal to break his word and his devotion to truth now praises, to his heart's content, a deceitful king who broke his word; he describes him—shame, shame, shame mixed with pain!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 29 / printed 27 -->

Look further at the contradiction in the picture Achariyar has drawn of this good king!

Dasaratha, it seems, ruled the kingdom tirelessly as though it were a child he had begotten. Such a Dasaratha? Listen, through Achariyar himself, to the words he speaks on seeing Kaikeyi's anger.

“Kaikeyi! Ask whatever you want! Whom should I punish? I shall punish them. Whom should I release from punishment? Even if he were a murderer, I would release him at once!

Do you not know the absolute authority I possess? What should be given to whom? In which country, what should be done to whom? Tell me! I shall do it immediately.” Thus, caught in lustful infatuation, he pleaded and begged!

Achariyar wrote this in the issue dated 22 August. This, it seems, is the good man—the pure-hearted man—the taintless man—the man who regarded the people of the kingdom as his children and laboured tirelessly for the kingdom! That is what he wrote on 8 August.

If old men who praise old Dasarathas like this come to the seat of power—if they had remained entrenched on that seat of power—I am frightened even to imagine what would have become of the country! What power Kama-Raja has! How he has made the old Raja babble on in every possible way—poor man!
