# In the Heavenly Realm

<!-- Tamil source: scan 14 / printed numeral not visible -->

The heavenly realm. The sun is setting. A beautiful flower garden. In one corner of the garden, a few devas were talking over personal matters; celestial maidens circled about plucking flowers, dancing like whirling bees.

Such grace in the way they chatted with one another! Menaka looked at Rambha's navaratna necklace and asked, “What's this, girl?” Rambha said, “Indra gave it to me.....” “Oh-ho, a bedchamber gift?” the others teased...... And so each of them introduced her ornaments...... “This is a gift from the Moon; this is a present from the Sun; this is a reward from Manmathan.”

In one part of the garden, the Moon and Tara were playing flower-ball, using the Asvathi star as the ball!

Rolling the earthly world itself around, Agni Bhagavan, Vayu and the others were deeply engag—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 15 / printed 14 -->

ed in a game of football.

Parvati Devi had taken the snake lying on Paramasivan's shoulder and was using it as a skipping rope.

Brahma and Saraswati were absorbed in the pleasure of reading a newly published novel.

Mahavishnu, pretending to play blind man's buff with Lakshmi, had blindfolded her and left her wandering about, while he and Urvashi had gone off beside a waterfall to enjoy themselves.

Murugan, having set the peacock and the rooster fighting, was immersed in delight with Valli and Deivayanai beside him.

Vinayagar sucked up water with his trunk and sprayed it over the celestial women for amusement.

Paramasivan, realising that no one was paying attention to him, was on his way to the “quarters” of the rishis' wives in Darukavana.

Such was the garden revel in the heavenly realm!

Ignoring all this, a group sat on the grass. From each person's shoulder hung a yellow

<!-- Tamil source: scan 16 / printed 15 -->

strip of cloth. In blue letters it was marked ‘Immigrant’. One look at their faces made it clear that they were earth-dwellers. Yes...... earth-dwellers indeed!

Kannappa Nayanar, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, Nandanar, Siruthondar ..... and all the others were there. Their talk did not become a great debate. Each had a word to say. It was about the way each of them had come to the heavenly realm!

“Only after I gouged out both my eyes did I receive God's grace.”

**Kannappa Nayanar.**

“I cut my child to pieces and cooked him as curry. My wife and I pounded his head into chutney and served the meal. Only after that did Sivaperuman take pity and make me his own.”

**Siruthondar.**

<!-- Tamil source: scan 17 / printed 16 -->

“In the Dikshitars' dream, Deena Dayaparan said, ‘Plunge into the fire, burn away all pollution, and come with a golden body.’ Only after I made that sacrifice did I receive the vision of the sacred feet of the Lord of Tillai.”

**Nandanar.**

“The mighty Lord told me to strip away all the flesh from my body, drain away the blood, become a skeleton—like a ghost—and walk upside down! Only after I underwent that suffering did Kailasapathi bestow his gracious glance.”

**Karaikkal Ammaiyar.**

“I weeded grass in temples. I carried the palanquin of Tirugnana Sambandar, the Brahmin. Only after doing all that did I receive permission to come to the realm of the immortals!”

**Appar.**

<!-- Tamil source: scan 18 / printed 17 -->

‘Honour is precious to a human being. Pawning that honour, I sent my wife into the bedchamber with Paramasivan. It was as the reward for that sacrifice that Paraman granted me this abode in the celestial sphere.’

**Iyarpagai Nayanar.**

“I rubbed my elbow in place of a sandalwood block and performed worship of Bhagavan. The fruit of that is this heavenly realm for me.”

**Murthi Nayanar.**

“Ah! Do you know how many people I killed to come to this realm? I hacked to death with an axe all those who reviled Siva. Only after I showed all those corpses to the crescent-wearing Mad One did I obtain this blessed state.”

**Eripatha Nayanar.**

“Because, during the famine, my mother, my father,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 19 / printed 18 -->

and my beloved wife ate the paddy that had been stored for Siva..... out of devotion I cut them down with a curved blade and killed them; I killed my dear child as well, because the child must have drunk the milk of the mother who ate the paddy. Only after that, forgiving my offence, the Red-Matted One wearing konrai appeared before me.”

**Kotpuli Nayanar**

Thus the devotees recounted their sacrifices and swelled with pride. They heaved deep sighs.

“There is no difference between you and the August martyrs, fellows!” A sudden voice rang out. Kannappar, who had opened his mouth to speak, stopped. Everyone turned and looked.

A sacred-thread-wearing Ayyar stood there with an arm around the shoulder of a woman whose youth had faded. All those jewels among devotees stared at his mouth. The Ayyar spoke.

“All of you are boast—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 20 / printed 19 -->

ing that you made great, great sacrifices to come to the realm of moksha; when I think of it, I laugh. The Congress man who set fire to telegraph wires and post offices and uprooted railway tracks is called a martyr! Aachariyar, who said it was wrong to cut telegraph wires, proclaimed that the August struggle was wrong, and stood opposed to it—he too is called a martyr!

“Why are you staring? What I am talking about now is a matter of Tamil Nadu politics!

“You say you suffered unspeakable hardships to come to the heavenly realm; look here—I have come to Sivalokam without the slightest hardship.

(The devotees were stunned with amazement.)

‘Do not be astonished, friends! Here, the woman leaning against me is my mother.’

<!-- Tamil source: scan 21 / printed 20 -->

(Kotpuli Nayanar interrupts.)

‘Your mother?’

“Yes, my mother! The mother who carried me for ten months and gave birth to me. The mother who laid me in a beautiful cradle and sang lullabies! I have taken this mother as my wife. Not only here—even in the earthly world this mother was my beloved woman. She put me in the cradle and rocked me. I gave her pleasure in bed. You will say, ‘Making one's mother into a great wife—is it not...?’ As an even greater, greater sin than this, I immediately killed my father when he found us on the bed.

“Are you asking, ‘The gates of heaven opened for you?’ Yes; heaven gave way easily for me. I cried, ‘Aragara Mahadeva!’ As I held Mother in my arms, the Father, mounted upon the bull, showered grace. I came happily to the land of heaven.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 22 / printed 21 -->

The Nayanmars were struck speechless.

Karaikkal Ammaiyar asked, “Is this true?”

“Still you doubt? If you have doubts, look at the ‘Mahapathagam Theertha Padalam’ in the *Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam*,” said the Ayyar.

“What is the reason that you, a sinner, alone obtained life in the heavenly realm so easily?” asked Siruthondar.

“This is the reason,” said the Ayyar, pulling out his sacred thread and showing it.

“Not only me. Bhagavan himself took Sundaramurthi Nayanar to a courtesan's house.... He made Sundaramurthi enter the temple with betel from the courtesan's house.... He granted Sundaramurthi heavenly life with ease. This sacred thread is the reason for that,” the Ayyar concluded, and the Ayyar and the mother went away.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 23 / printed 22 -->

“Then for Aachaariyar to become a martyr by rejecting the August struggle too......” Kannappar began, but before he could finish........

“Yes, the sacred thread is the reason,” replied Eripatha Nayanar.

“How much we all suffered, what immense sacrifices we made, to reach the sacred feet of this Maheswaran.... Yet this Brahmin, who committed a great sin, has come to Kailasa with the help of a single sacred thread!” Murthi Nayanar sighed sorrowfully.

“We are all Dravidians; he is an Aryan. Even in going to the heavenly realm, the Aryan–Dravidian difference has taken root, my friend!” Appar said, shedding two tears.

I rose, declaring loudly that it is to change this state that “The Clarion Call of the Race” is sounding.

“What is it, son—were you dreaming?” my mother asked, waking me from my sleep.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 24 / printed 23 -->

My sleep was broken. But Kamarajar—Gramaaniyar—must wake from their sleep too, mustn't they?
