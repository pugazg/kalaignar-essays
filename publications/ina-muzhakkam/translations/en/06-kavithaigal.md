# Poems

<!-- Tamil source: scan 41 / printed 40 -->

## Scale of Justice!

You will dress a wound with medicine  
and seek a way to heal it!  
Like the pupil within the eye,  
the heart rolls—a widow  
has one too;  
you will realize it!

“My wife has died,”  
you will say you want another marriage;  
then, if you reach the edge of death,  
must your wife make a sacrifice  
of her love?

## Will They Accept!

Dharman and Bhiman and the other three were not enough—  
Panchali loved Karunan too!  
Ah! Lakshmi and Parvathi,  
and Kalaimagal, treasure of music,  
did not hate the sea  
for having lost chastity!  
Not sleight of hand! There is a story!  
Ahalyai, a woman of a rishi clan,  
lay with Indran;  
Tharai, the ascetic woman,  
desired Chandran;  
all these Puranas

<!-- Tamil source: scan 42 / printed 41 -->

were fashioned and given in Muthamizh  
by the sothas of this land,  
because their backbones were bent!

“Even if a mountain comes against us, our chastity  
will not falter,” you women  
who speak such words of valour!  
If all the women named above  
are gods,  
will those with a mind accept your words?

## Saivites!

Eripaththar slew his enemies with an axe!  
Mother, father, wife and children were cut down  
by Kotpuli!  
You call Saivism the firm ground  
of love!  
In the killing field, through Kulachirai,  
Jains died!  
O true devotees, babbling  
with swollen heads!  
For trapping in a net,  
the Saivism you nurtured—  
we shall strike it with death and swiftly  
bring it down!  
We are not the breeze  
that enters a flower garden!  
Know us as the storm!

## Come!

Our poets lap up and drink  
the nectar of art;  
O sotha who swoons and sings praise,  
turn it over and see!  
If obscenity is poured onto a beautiful plate  
and called payasam,  
will scholars drink it?  
Kambar and Kizhar too,  
in our noble Dravidian land,  
shaped fevered art!  
Is art for thought?  
Is art for control?  
O time that rejects and casts it aside,  
come swiftly, you!

## Common Ownership!

‘Of three hundred, four hundred velis,  
O capitalist!  
Has even one poor person  
risen because of you?”—  
O comrade who asks at every moment,  
from whatever land!  
I welcome the question from your sturdy heart,  
honour it and rejoice;  
yet become a tree the moment my question rises!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 44 / printed 43 -->

## Think It Over!

A stone has a thousand velis  
in the temple, friend!  
Not even grass benefits;  
the poor people lament.  
Say one word through clenched teeth,  
O Bharata hero!

## Student Uprising.

Where is the glorious place where the Cheras lived?  
Why did the brave Pandiyan's rule fall?  
In the tireless land where the Cholas roamed,  
how did cowardly policy pile up?  
Questions burst  
in anguished minds.  
We shall drink life!  
Give us the country!  
The great sermon  
that says separation is not wanted—  
the howl of foxes! O bulls  
mad for the country!  
Tear away and fling aside  
those panic-stricken men!  
At the summit of the tiger's life,  
we shall engrave the new.

## Here Is the Sword!

It is only a hut! On one side  
sharp spears and swords  
stand in rows—to seize the world  
and end burning enmity,  
they gleam like weapons forged and kept;  
there is no beauty in the tiger's cave—  
nor is that strange;  
fear, shivering flesh,  
or a nature that stoops ever show their heads:  
the dwelling place of honour—  
the warrior's palace.

At the doorway of the house,  
with rice in a bowl,  
mixing in a little jaggery  
to send it down into her belly,  
opening her toothless mouth,  
she took a handful of rice and put it in—  
an old woman with great grey hair.

A warrior came running.  
“A piece of news, grandmother!” he said.  
Like a little girl who came dancing,  
you are panting heavily.  
Are you a man, younger brother!  
No hurry!  
Let there be a pause between breaths;  
then,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 46 / printed 45 -->

begin your speech, she said—that  
old Tamil woman famed  
for her teasing!

This is no time for fun, grandmother—leave  
your customary teasing.  
‘Your son died in the field,’  
he said—her heart  
broke; the old mother drew herself upright!

“When playing dice, pieces  
are cut down—the battlefield is the same.  
Is the wound on his chest? On his back?  
Tell me,” she said—“On the back,” he said.

The old woman trembled; her heart  
burst; she took the sword;  
toward the direction where the drum sounded,  
she quickened her pace!

“I gave milk to a coward?  
This fellow lying face-down—  
he is called  
a war-hero? Once,  
to answer the spear that came hurtling,  
my deathless beloved showed his chest  
and fell.  
Was this one born to him?  
Ah! Where is honour—lower  
than even a little wall he fell  
and lay. Shame! Shame!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 47 / printed 46 -->

At the Himalayan boundary valour laughs—here  
music throbs in the strings of the veena;  
even that resounds only ‘honour, honour’;  
drinking wine and liquor, dying—  
are you of the shameless Aryan lineage, you wretch?  
You have cast a stain on the warrior-Tamil clan.  
I gave you my breast and raised you  
as a son—you ate and grew fat;  
where are those shoulders surging with arrogance?  
Did they not even itch, alas!  
O coward unable to show  
the spear its way—tell me what became  
of my warrior's milk!”

Thus wailed the poor old woman  
nearing eighty.

Reaching the street corner, she turned over  
the scattered  
pure-Tamil bulls  
and looked—there,  
to protect our fair Tamil land,  
a flood of blood ran!  
Through the heap of corpses, panting,  
she walked!  
Even in the wedding pavilion she had not  
known such joy—when her son

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 47 -->

was born, even that joy  
had a limit—he  
lay dead, showing his chest to the spear!  
She saw it—her heart cooled!

“Whatever I see, I have no more worry;  
my son died a warrior,” she said.  
I was ready to cut away  
the breast he drank from—ah!  
Where is the scoundrel who lied without knowing the matter?  
Here is the sword! Where is his tongue?

## When Will There Be Defeat?

At word that a mouse has come,  
a fierce cat hides  
beside the stove!  
As a snake darts dancing by,  
the mongoose that sees it  
stands sweating in panic!  
If these great spectacles  
unseen in the world  
come to rich Tamil land,  
having lived crowned with victory,  
never shrinking from battle,  
all the lion-like Tamil bulls  
who hid in fear of a crab  
like a fox—to Aryan  
okra-warriors  
would give their victories!

## Still This Clamour?

On the banks of the Kaveri,  
in the rush of the Pennai,  
there is no history! No history!  
Alas, Tamil Nadu!  
It all begins at the Ganges, they say.  
Who saw the Pandyas rule?  
Who carved the Cheras' prosperity?  
Was the Cholas' abundance ever told?  
The tale of the slipper that ruled—  
that is the beginning of history!  
All who deny it have sold their honour.  
Students have come to remake it!  
The warriors of the camp will leap like a flash!  
O sneering mouths! Still this clamour?

## Varna or Death?

In verdant Tamil land that lived with grace,  
singing many paranis,  
a few self-serving sothas  
without even a trace of shame gather;  
though they devise every device  
to establish Varna,  
at the summit of death, to guard honour,  
O warrior Tamilian, fight.
