# He Goes; He Goes..!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 16 / printed numeral not visible -->

Though sharp thorns scratch the hands, the maiden's hands leap to pluck the flower.

Though there are terrifying waves that can stop one's breath, the human heart longs to gather pearls.

Though it knows rocks may collapse and crush the body, humankind digs a mine to take gold.

The flower—the pearl—the gold become ideals. The thorn—the sea—`paaraiyu` become obstacles. The one whose nature does not fear obstacles keeps walking toward the path of the ideal.

That warrior of ideals is a bull shaken by nothing.

“Laugh when adversity comes,”—this Kural is his breath.

‘The ideal is my life’—this is his speech.

Even when cruelties perform a ghastly dance, he carries on his journey in delight.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 17 / printed 16 -->

He calls the people who stand before him as obstacles mountains of flesh. He mocks them as a crushed lead vessel!

“It is not conceit surging from my heart that makes me speak like this! That horror dancing in your eyes...! That indifference dripping from the crease of your lips! That broth of poison boiling in your heart! These are what make me lose my calm,” he will say, explaining his anger.

He did not leap into public-welfare work out of a craving for fame. He knows that work is like climbing a slippery pole! He knows that public service is the act of swimming against a raging flood! Expecting the arrows launched by the opposing movement and the spears thrust by his own movement, that idealist roams the field.

He cannot bear to see people living as moss-covered pools of blood—as rotted cages of bone—as cages of nerves swarmed by `sel`.

He stands face to face and attacks every force that is hostile to life. Religion praised by Mahatmas? The god worshipped from the day bark-cloth was donned? Leaders of religion? Representatives of god? In the forge of his thought, all of them are dust! Dust!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 18 / printed 17 -->

Traitors to society, selfish bandicoots, those who stamp themselves with the seal of idealism and engage in base work, mockers, those who speak from behind, growlers, those who say “I'll kick you”; this list burns and chars before his resolve.

“Look! Look!...pallid rascal! I will turn your influence into worthless coin,” roars an embodiment of jealousy.

“I am not, like you, a worm writhing in fame. The pit you dig for my fame will swallow and digest you and flesh-mounds like you, and will also lay the foundation for my undying fame.” That day he will roar a warrior's cry.

Look here, man......a storeyed mansion. A heap of emeralds, a pleasure-city to your heart's desire; come here and you can lie on jasmine, walk on velvet carpet—such a net of desire will be cast. The idealist speaks like an ascetic and stands far away.

“This body is false, lad—a bag filled with air, lad”—a ‘water-bubble bhajan’ will be put on display. “Show your tricks to those with their eyes shut, man,” he mocks.

Warning. Danger notice—immediate action—syrupy speech—sham work: in all these forms destruction draws near him. But that warrior of ideals goes on along his own path—goes—goes—keeps going.
