# The Story of How the Revolution Grew

<!-- Tamil source: scan 10 / printed numeral not visible -->

May Day! A celebration of those who tightened their grip around the neck of the capitalist class and killed it—the class that tormented workers' lives, chewed their bones and polished its own body. A festival of jubilation for those who crushed the throats of the lords who, when the proletarian people of Petersburg cried out in hunger, made them wade in blood and laughed a monstrous laugh.

Religion, more vicious than a mountain python, vanished. The ‘pomp’ of the rich demon-horde that drank the people's lives and belched diminished.... The lineage of the godly horde that stood like stone, without so much as turning an eye toward those who suffered—who shed tears—who stood distraught—who cried that there was no way out—and that took reason as its prey, was uprooted. With the joy of all this coursing through their nerves, the proletarian people of Russia—why, the proletarian people of the world—remember the heroic Lenin, seated as a corpse in Moscow's Red Square, and perform a victorious warrior-dance—a victory festival!

May Day became a ladder for the rise of humankind—a guide to life.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 11 / printed 10 -->

On March 8, 1917, revolution took shape in Russia! Women textile workers in Petrograd marched in a revolutionary procession. On November 7, 1917, the Soviet government was established. Lenin was the head of the council of ministers. The seed of all these revolutions was the smile of a martyr. In 1905, a young man who could no longer bear repression sprang up and, in broad daylight, killed Prince Sergius, a close relative of King Nicholas and a high-ranking military officer. The killer did not lose his nerve and flee. He believed that he had done something society needed.

The police officers turned themselves upside down trying to get some statement out of that killer. They could not get even a single letter written down. The resolve born of knowing that he was going to die anyway made him mute; he remained silent. He was placed on the gallows. “Murderer! I am going to drink your life,” the gallows exulted. Only then did his lips move—not to speak, but for a faint smile! Do you know how vast and terrible a spark of revolution flew from that smile?

He beat Prince Sergius to death. He smiled at his own death. The satisfaction that society would not forget him.... The confidence that Russian history would not cast him aside

<!-- Tamil source: scan 12 / printed 11 -->

—these were the smile at the edge of that young man's lips. Death's smile!

Before that smile had faded, strikes arose across the country. The Tsar's government could not shoot the strike down. The government floundered. For eight months the strike lived victoriously. It was the time when Russia, having fought Japan, surrendered. The people spat at Tsarist rule. The revolution grew. In 1906 the ‘Duma’ met. That ‘Duma’ wanted to carry out a few reforms. The creator of the Duma was the Tsar!... The creator himself destroyed the Duma. The reason: it had thought of doing good. Duma means a people's representative assembly. Had the Tsar liked such a thing, Lenin need not have been born. In 1907 an election was held for the Duma. Even though those who held the Tsar's tail won... good words slipped out of their mouths by mistake. The second Duma too died. No... it was beaten to death—by the Tsar!

The Tsar did not like every adult having the right to vote. He gave the vote only to those of higher status and held an election for the third Duma. It remained alive for five years. In 1912 came the election for the fourth Duma. From then on, the very pedestal of Tsarist rule began to shake.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 13 / printed 12 -->

The king—one who asked the price of intelligence. One who inquired at which shop manliness was sold. Yet he was a wholesale dealer in injustice. His wife's tender hands were his sceptre!.... Her inner chambers were his assembly hall. Her playful amusements were his law book. Like a scorpion stinging a drunken monkey, the religionist Rasputin drew near him. The Tsar never crossed the line drawn by Rasputin and the Tsar's wife. They became Russia's Mangaiyarkkarasi—and Kulachchirai!.... They themselves became the earthquake that brought the Tsar down. Cruelty danced its tandava. It became a lamp about to go out!.... Members of the Bolshevik Party hung from the gallows. The revolution began to smoke. That foolish king did not realise it was a volcano. At that very moment came 1914. The World War bared its savage teeth. Sharp claws reached out. Russia entered the war and in 1915 suffered a crushing defeat at Tannenberg at the hands of the Germans. The dispirited king thought of forming a new council of ministers. Before that thought could grow, the king's wife and the arch-sinner Rasputin burned it away!

In 1916......another murder in the royal family. That lion cub who smiled on the gallows in 1905... the echo of his smile......in 1916, Rasputin was

<!-- Tamil source: scan 14 / printed 13 -->

shot dead. Repression roared. The revolution too seethed.

The Tsar at the war front! Here, the women flew the flag of revolution—March 8, 1917!

The Russian capital trembled ‘kidukidu’. From the battlefield itself the Tsar gave the order: ‘Shoot them down!’

Who was to shoot whom? When volcanoes surge and overflow, are oil wells to stand against them? The Tsar himself set out in person! There was no track for the train that brought him. The Tsar's wife lamented: is this the fate of the emperor of emperors who ruled the land...? The people's hearts roared that he was a rhinoceros ruling the jungle. An emergency meeting of the people's representative assembly was convened. They knew only one way to suppress the revolution! It was the “Tsar-king's abdication ceremony.”

That very day was an abdication festival for the Romanal royal dynasty itself. A graveyard was built anew in Russia that day for the capitalist–religionist class! The capitalist and religionist hordes that had possessed the boon of immortality lost their title of Chiranjeevi and were placed upon the pyre. God-ism, the life-companion of that tyrannical horde, sang, “Pyre! Pyre!”

The revolution subsided. The rain stopped; the drizzle did not.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 15 / printed 14 -->

For about eight months there were petty disturbances in Russia. Lenin drew up a plan to gather the whole strength of the workers to the side of the Bolshevik Party. On November 7, 1917, the All-Russia Soviet Congress was held in Petrograd.

While the meeting was still under way, the Bolshevik forces seized the government. Kerensky, prime minister of the Provisional Government, escaped disguised as a woman. The Soviet government was established. No... a foundation ceremony was held for a new life. That victorious laugh is what echoes on today's May Day. Will that echo raise in Dravidam the voice of the race—the voice of Self-Respect?
