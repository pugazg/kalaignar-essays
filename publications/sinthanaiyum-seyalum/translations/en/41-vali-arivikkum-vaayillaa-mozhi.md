# The Mute Language That Tells of Pain!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 178 / unit marker only -->

Why are you looking at me like that? Why are you bewildered?
Why are you covering your ears?

You look and enjoy; why, you rejoice at what is done as well; you are astonished—you tremble, you panic—you shake with fear, and many times you are transported with wonder.

You cannot see me with your eyes—but know that everything your eyes can see is the work of my hand.

Do you know my name? I have no name at all; then how can you name me?

Human beings like you, existing in many different groups across the world, give me a name of their choosing and call me by it.

You may say, “Oh! I have found it—your name is ‘God.’” But since even that divides people into those who “accept” and those who “reject,” let the name that has come to be mine as a common name, a name no one can deny, remain **Nature**.

Let us keep it that way for identification!

“Wherever you look—there is Power!
The seven seas are her colour!”

They say Paavendhar Bharathidasan sang these lines while seated near Bharathiar;

<!-- Tamil source: scan 179 / printed 178 -->

see Bharathidasan himself tell us what that Power is!

“O vast sky, O open space—source
of all that has arisen everywhere,
you who gave the wandering air and water,
earth and the red fire,
the known sun and moon—seed
of the world filled with many forms,
all the deeds you perform are
new! new! new!”

Was he not singing of me—of **Nature**—and of my power?

When I breathe, you call it the breeze; when I heave a great sigh, you call it a storm, a cyclone!

I shed tears of joy; you call it a waterfall! I wail and lament, beating my belly and my mouth; you call it an earthquake! In the new language you call the sea cataclysm a “tsunami”!

At least now, do you understand my power—Nature’s power?

Your human race is carrying on a continuous struggle against me. And because that struggle is moving towards a state in which neither side can reach a final conclusion,

“Those who strive without faltering,
never lowering their effort, will see even fate turn its back,”

and

“What is mightier than fate?
Whatever else one plans, fate arrives first,”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 180 / printed 179 -->

says Ayya Valluvar himself. When we compare his call to make tireless effort to defeat Nature with his words of great wonder praising Nature as mighty, with nothing more primary than it, can we not understand that the struggle between the people of the world and me remains in an unresolved state?

Because I am compared to “the earth that bears those who dig into it” when speaking of one who tolerates those who insult him, how much can I myself be expected to endure? When you try to test me beyond measure, what am I to do?

If the oldest remembered count of India’s population was thirty crores, and it is now crossing one hundred crores—and if the populations of all the countries of the world are added together—do you and I not know that the human race is sitting in council, planning and acting to meet the corresponding need for facilities and means of life by begging them from me, by looting them from me, or by waging war and seizing them from me!

What am I to do? I am a compassionate being. As long as I have something, I give it. As far as possible, I do not stop you from taking it away. Yet it is not merely like the saying that even a stone wears away when ants crawl over it; if processions of elephants pass again and again, the forest too will wear away—the country too will wear away—one day even the turning of the world will cease!

What suffering you put me, Nature, through! If it were hunger for food, flesh-sucking infants would drink milk from the breast and rejoice. But yours is hunger for knowledge, is it not? To satisfy the needs and desires of the people,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 181 / printed 180 -->

you search for the very source from which the milk swelling in my breast is produced. In the distress caused by that, I cry—I writhe—I surge in waves—I rise in fury and explode. For me, Nature, these actions are the mute language that tells you of my pain!

You who act through artificial means—show a little restraint, keep me too within bounds, and you may drink in my beauty and benefit from it!

If, instead, you think you have found an enslaved woman whom you can keep subdued and continue striking blow after blow, then the proverb **“when the meek one is enraged, even the forest cannot contain it”** will keep frightening the human race!

10 - 10 - 2005
