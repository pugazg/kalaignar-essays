# Protect Before It Comes? Or After It Comes?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 182 / unit marker only -->

A.D. 2004—last year, in the assault of the sea cataclysm called the tsunami, beginning with Sumatra and extending through the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, the people of coastal cities, small villages and towns of Tamil Nadu lost lives in the lakhs and property in the crores; and—

A.D. 2005—this year too, Nature’s fury has risen as earthquake, rupture of the earth and tremors. Parts of the northern States of India, Jammu and Kashmir, and the neighbouring country Pakistan have suffered a vast disaster, with estimates that the loss of human life alone would cross one lakh and with horrifying reports that many towns and villages were buried into the earth itself. Dr. Naganathan and I were reflecting on all this as we took our usual walking exercise. In our conversation we began searching for the causes of these natural upheavals that produce such vast destruction, and we had an opportunity to discuss the views that scientific thinkers had arrived at and published through their research.

At that point Dr. Naganathan began with Amartya Sen—the world-renowned economist, Nobel Prize winner and scientific thinker proud to be an Indian citizen—and went on to speak of scientists Albert Einstein, White Bord, Vijayoshi Matsumo and Mahmoop Ul Haq, and the causes they had assigned to the natural upheavals behind these great disasters. He explained that my recent article on Nature’s fury, “The Mute Language That Tells of Pain!”, had been written on the basis of the research views they held.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 183 / printed 182 -->

Those explanations seemed to me like masses of dark clouds spread across the sky. I said it would be good if all those clouds joined together and poured down as an article-rain useful to everyone. And so he produced an excellent, knowledge-based article under the title `Worship of Nuclear Weapons—and the Threatening Catastrophe`. It appeared in `Murasoli` too. Letters of praise! Telephone calls!

In that article he points out that the American scientist White Bord, comparing the periods before and after nuclear-weapons tests, had stated that before 1950 there were on average 68 earth tremors a year, while after 1950 the average was 127 a year, and that the intensity, which had been 5.8 on the Richter scale, was continuing to rise.

Did I not write as though Nature itself were lamenting and crying—

“What suffering you put me, Nature, through!
If it were hunger for food, infants would suck the breast and drink milk with delight—but yours is hunger for knowledge, is it not? To fulfil the needs and desires of the people, you search for the very source from which the milk swelling in my breast is produced! In the distress caused by that I cry—I writhe—I surge in waves—I rise in fury and explode. For me, Nature, these actions are the mute language that tells of my pain!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 184 / printed 183 -->

Only after Nature, angered and furious, has subjected lives and property to vast destruction do governments and charitable service organisations rise eagerly to perform relief work—as protectors **after** it has come. We ourselves must make arrangements to protect **before** it comes. Whatever the government, it must come forward without hesitation to take the measures needed for that.

When an earthquake or a great wave strikes, if ten thousand people in one region are said to have died, it is not the case that all ten thousand were directly struck by the earthquake or great wave and buried in the earth or drowned. Of those ten thousand, perhaps only a few hundred are buried because the ground beneath their planted feet shakes or splits. More than nine thousand lose their breath trapped and struggling in the debris when the houses in which they live collapse under the tremor, rupture or great-wave assault.

If you wish, look at television: when these tremors and shakings begin, tall houses and multi-storeyed buildings are shaken and brought down in a single second, turning the people living there into heaps of corpses. You can see that this is what happens.

That is why, while undertaking relief work to protect people after a disaster is one thing, governments that administer countries—and we ourselves—must learn to live as those who protect beforehand, locking the door against allowing such destruction.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 185 / printed 184 -->

When a multi-storeyed house or palace displaying its luxury is struck and shattered by Nature’s fury, if iron beams, stone pillars and granite walls fall upon us, can we escape being crushed?

Therefore, we cannot prevent Nature’s fury; but we can control, at least to some extent, the great loss caused by that fury.

Countries such as Japan, through the experience of the past, first arrange the homes in which they live accordingly, so that volcanoes, earthquakes and sea upheavals do not cause immediate destruction—or similarly produce catastrophe.

Rather than looking at those who have lost everything and stand without even a hut to crouch in and promising, “We shall build you permanent houses,” if temporary houses themselves are built from the beginning as protective houses, a condition need not arise in which they collapse and fall and people die because of them. Every country in the world must understand and consider this plan of protection-beforehand and come forward to put it into practice at the proper time with the urgency that **a delay will no longer help**.

If walls are raised with bamboo frames instead of stone, and light boards are used for roofs, then even if such a house collapses, experience shows that the damage will be to the house in which one lives; it will not read out a death warrant to the human being who still has life to live!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 186 / printed 185 -->

Let us for a while postpone the attempt to expand still further the already developed grandeur of scientific genius, to leap at the sky and conquer the earth, pouring out crores upon crores of money for it.

Instead, we must sharpen that scientific vision and make our immediate work the learning of a `science-art` that gives us the ability to know beforehand the form, the name and the place from which Nature’s fury will come.

“Scientists of England, Germany and Japan have continuously warned that conducting atomic-bomb tests weakens portions of the earth’s crust (`Crust`). Because of this, earth tremors are being triggered. They have calculated that, after 1950, there are 30,000 nuclear weapons of increased power throughout the world. Many lakhs of crores of rupees are spent on them.

“Five thousand nuclear weapons are kept ready to be launched rapidly within fifteen minutes. As an example, if a nuclear weapon aboard a Russian nuclear submarine were unexpectedly launched by accident, a firestorm would burn seventy lakh people in eight American cities. Thereafter the effects of radiation would send nearly one crore people to death. That is why Amartya Sen has described the great-power countries that conduct nuclear-weapons tests as `Merchants of Death`.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 187 / printed 186 -->

Thus has younger brother Naganathan pointed out in his article. Therefore the question before scientists is this: **“Sirs! Do you want your discoveries to be banana peels laid on the path along which human beings walk? Or do you want them to be handrails that keep them from falling?”**
