# Iraiyanar's Kural and Iniyavai Narpathu!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 195 / unit marker only -->

A life of income and expenditure that does not know the measure of what is in hand and live accordingly will seem to exist, then diminish and disappear. Though Ayya Valluvar, when he said that “if a person does not know the measure of the strength and resources he possesses and live accordingly, they will diminish and disappear,” was not speaking of life itself, after strength and wealth have vanished, does not life too become useless?

In another Kural in the chapter called `Valiyuruthal`, he does not fail to explain that even if income is small, if one ensures that expenditure does not cross its limit, the evil called poverty can be prevented before it comes.

The great poet Puthanchenthanar points to further strengthening counsels for making a person’s life pleasant in the work `Iniyavai Narpathu`. This too is one of the `Pathinenkeezhkanakku` works.

“It is sweet to give according to known income;
it is sweet to keep one’s resolve from taking a side in a dispute;
even if the gain be very great, it is sweet not to rush into desired acts;
it is sweet to live without deviation.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 196 / printed 195 -->

This poem says: “It is pleasant to give according to one’s income; when there is a dispute between two people, to stand on neither side but act impartially according to conscience; even when a great benefit may come to oneself, not to desire the act and plunge into it hastily without examining it; and to live without departing from one’s nature.”

A person starts, on a very small scale, a bicycle shop. He buys and sells bicycles, receives a margin from those sales, increases that sum, then begins renting out bicycles himself and earns profit through that. In time, through his business skill, the bicycle-shop owner acquires prosperity little by little and finally rises to become the owner of hundreds of buses. If so, are not prudence, unceasing effort and business ability the first and fullest causes of that rise!

When he rises, the world easily calls it “luck”; when he falls, it calls it “fate.”

One must not wish, at the very outset, to reach the summit of the mountain.

Can one reach the mountain summit without first going to its foot?

Standing at the foot, one must look up at the summit.

One must make use of the small rocks that lie towards the summit—to place a foot upon, or to grip with a hand while climbing. After travelling a certain distance towards the summit, when one bends and looks below, the head trembles.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 197 / printed 196 -->

One must shake off that trembling and continue climbing upward and upward with confidence. Only at the end of that effort can one touch the crown of the mountain.

How did the man who kept a bicycle shop become the owner of several hundred buses and a provider of employment to thousands of young men?

I am not speaking now to say that you should become a bus owner and capitalist, nor to argue the other position that private domination must not be allowed to raise its head in the bus industry and that the buses must therefore be nationalised. The government once conducted under my leadership itself stands as evidence that, between those two positions, I recommend the condition in which all members of the public—including people in the smallest villages—benefit. Leaving that issue aside now, I speak only to insist that for any trade or commerce to grow and flourish, whether individually or through an institution, uninterrupted labour, unfaltering effort and self-confidence are fully necessary.

In my youth, a friend of mine said, “Watch me—I am going to build small and live large,” invested five hundred rupees, and opened a small provisions shop beside his house. At first, business was excellent. The monthly profit was enough to make him happy.

But as the days went by, every item needed for my friend’s household—salt, tamarind, chillies and the rest—was taken as a matter of right from that shop, without paying a paisa!

And what was the result? It came to the point where a closing ceremony had to be held for my friend’s shop!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 198 / printed 197 -->

Therefore, without placing responsibility on something or someone else by saying “luck,” or “can we change what was written for him that day?”, any person who undertakes a task with the firmness of mind that **we ourselves are responsible for success and failure** will not be approached by weariness or discouragement. As long as that person engraves Ayya’s Kural deep in the mind and follows the counsels of elders such as Puthanchenthanar, there will be no backward step in that life!

Meaning:

`Pettavai` = things desired.
