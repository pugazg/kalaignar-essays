# The Guru Peedam and the Kural Peedam!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 192 / unit marker only -->

Though truth and falsehood stand opposite one another, Ayya Valluvar points out that there is a way for falsehood to come and sit in the place of truth. What way is that?

First let us understand what truth means. Ayya Valluvar says that truth is speaking a word that causes not even a sesame-seed’s tip worth of harm to another.

It is also important that the word, though spoken for someone’s benefit, must not cause harm to anyone else. Hidden within this is another condition: those others too must be people who intend harm to no one.

“What is truth? It is the speaking
of words that cause no harm at all.”

In Vyasa’s `Mahabharata`, the character Parashurama, an incarnation of the Lord, can be understood from reading the Bharata as one who wandered in a frenzy for `varnashrama dharma`. Drona too was one distinguished in such arts. Kannan first went to Drona to learn archery.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 193 / printed 192 -->

When Drona refused, Kannan went and appealed to Parashurama. Since Parashurama would not teach the art to a Kshatriya, Kannan falsely said that he was a Brahmin, became his student, and received training. One day Parashurama fell asleep with his head resting on Kannan’s thigh. At that time a beetle bored into Kannan’s thigh. Thinking that if he moved because he could not bear the pain his guru’s sleep would be disturbed, Kannan remained seated without moving. Blood poured from the thigh pierced by the beetle and flowed everywhere. Parashurama woke with a start. Seeing Kannan’s firmness and fearlessness, he said: “You cannot be a Brahmin—you are a Kshatriya. You lied and deceived me. Therefore, may the skill you learned from me fail to serve you at the moment you need it!” He cursed him and drove him away.

More important than saying that this punishment came to Kannan because he told a lie is the fact that the principal reason for the curse was that he was not a Brahmin.

And what, after all, was Kannan’s offence? He lied and became Parashurama’s student. With the good intention that his guru’s sleep should not be disturbed, he even endured the pain of a beetle boring into him.

Parashurama punishes him—curses him—because he told a lie.

What harm came from that lie? According to the practice of that time, was learning archery an evil act? No! Then why the punishment?

If Ayya Valluvar had considered the falsehood Kannan spoke, how would he have looked at it? From what angle?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 194 / printed 193 -->

“Even falsehood may take the place of truth
if it produces faultless good.”

If it brings about good without harm, even a false word may acquire the place of truth.

The guru peedam of the Bharata story is intent upon exacting vengeance and stands as a descendant of `varnashrama`.

But the Kural peedam of ancient Tamil literature welcomes even falsehood into the seat of truth, if there is a righteous path by which it yields good without harm!
