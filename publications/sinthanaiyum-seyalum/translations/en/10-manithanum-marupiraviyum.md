# Man and Rebirth

<!-- Tamil source: scan 52 / unit marker only -->

“Thanthai Periyar’s thought—‘Man was not born by himself; therefore he must not live for himself’—is one that can send a thrill through thought. Kalaignar, who stands among us, can be seen as a form given to this thought of Thanthai Periyar.”

So wrote my beloved younger brother Veeramani in this year’s `Murasoli` special issue for my birthday. Today’s written portrait is drawn from that thought.

There are those who say, according to the burden pressing on their minds or the tastes they enjoy in life, what they wish to be born as in the next birth—and what they must not be born as. Such things can be found in tales of imagination and in books crowded with hard puranic notions!

“To pour gruel into the parched stomach,  
O sugar-candy! O honey syrup! O Tirukkural!  
be born in Kanchi!”

In accordance with Mother Tamil’s plea,

“Born as our Anna;  
as a king of intellect,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 53 / printed 52 -->

as the breeze from Pothigai hill,  
becoming a bud and blossoming,  
as the fresh fragrance of Tamil feeling,  
as the meaning of a hymn,  
as the puram-song of the ancient Tamils,  
Anna arose—like a ray  
rising in the lower sky—”

I sang this in my elegiac poetic tribute. Even the idea that, accepting Mother Tamil’s plea, Valluvar was born in Kanchi as Anna is only a poetic, ornamental imagination used to strengthen the thought. If we dig deep and think, we can understand that it is not a truth that scientific rationalism can willingly accept.

Many creators who do not openly admit, as I do, that they wrote for poetic beauty and to make an idea remain firmly in memory have, without realising it, stamped their approval upon the superstition called rebirth.

* “In your next birth you will be born as a fox,” says the curse.

* “By whom, and at what time, will that curse be lifted?” asks the prophecy.

* “O Goddess Parvati! In your next birth, be born as a fisherwoman and marry Lord Shiva, who will be born as a fisherman!”—and then,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 54 / printed 53 -->

Parvati nods her head in consent—

and the story proceeds exactly so—

these are our old Puranas!

* “Every year, in the name of `tithi` and `divasam`, we send rice, vegetables, cash and `dakshina` through priests to those who have died. But the dead would have taken another birth, would they not? If so, where do the rice, lentils, vegetables and money sent to them according to the priestly mantras go? Who receives them?”

It was Periyar who made these questions arise within us and compelled us to ask them. On the subject of man’s birth and his life, he carved in our hearts a sculpture of thought in the compact form of a Kural:

“Man was not born by himself. Therefore  
he must not live for himself.”

What height—height—towering height—what a lofty philosophy—what an incomparable philosophy!

“I shall arise in such-and-such a person’s womb  
and be born in this manner”—

all that is a fabricated tale acceptable only to puranic thinking—an outpouring of imagination—a coating of gold laid upon poetry.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 55 / printed 54 -->

If man could plan his own birth, he could decide: shall I be born a cow? a monkey? a peacock? a cuckoo? or once again a human being?—and then be born accordingly.

When Periyar strikes the point home in one blow that man was not born by himself, teaching us that birth is the continuing result of nature, what does it mean? Man has learnt to nurture, destroy and transform the embryo formed for the production of life. But just as no tree appears without a seed, no human being, without conception, manifests by himself in the open emptiness of mid-air as an avatar. Yes—not even one human being was born by himself.

No human being who has been born knew beforehand, “I am going to be born like this.” Therefore, one who was not born by himself—one who did not come into birth knowing how he would be born—must not, after birth, develop the selfishness of wanting to live for himself. That, one may say, is Thanthai Periyar’s admonition.

Your birth itself was not your act; it was something that occurred in the turning of nature’s wheel. When that is so, is it not absurd for you to claim ownership over the acts of others as well and seek selfish gain? The meaning is that selfish attachment must not accompany the natural result called birth!

6-6-2005
