# Women's Reservation!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 112 / unit marker only -->

Among those in political movements, there may be differences of opinion—clashes of opinion—between people belonging to one movement and those belonging to another. Yet on social equality, social progress and the principle of egalitarianism, a common ground of agreement must be created; these questions must be approached and resolved beyond the party politics followed by each side. Open-minded people who leave no room for likes and dislikes will accept this.

Our movement, a political movement that acts by placing social questions in the forefront, passed a resolution as far back as seventy-five years ago, at the 1929 Chengalpattu Self-Respect Conference in the presence of Thanthai Periyar, calling for a law guaranteeing women equal rights in property. Sixty years after that resolution, in 1989, when I was Chief Minister, we passed it as law in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and brought it into effect.

In a democratic system, all people under a government must possess all rights; those lying in a pit because of backwardness created over many years must be given a hand, provided the necessary and appropriate concessions, and helped to reach the shore; and, on the basic understanding that the word “people” refers to both men and women, rights possessed by men must not be denied to women. Those with social concern will show interest in these principles and act—and they have acted and continue to act.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 113 / printed 112 -->

Let rules and laws concerning women’s rights at the level of the Union government come when they come; within the limits of authority available at the State level, we declared that women would be given reservation in local bodies and government employment, and it was when Tamil Nadu was under our administration that we implemented it. That is a truth no one can deny.

One among the continuing achievements honouring women’s rights and welfare was the “Women’s Self-Help Group” inaugurated by me in Dharmapuri district in 1989 under our government.

Today, from every corner of the Indian nation, the policy drum that resounds carries one demand: women must be given 33 per cent reservation in Parliament and the Legislative Assemblies.

Why must a separate law for women’s reservation be enacted? Can women not now contest for those responsibilities? Does any law prevent them?

The law does not prevent them! Just as a man may now contest an election as a candidate for the Legislative Assembly or Parliament, a woman may contest too; the Constitution does not prevent it. But patriarchy does not allow them to contest. How long can we go on fighting this Sanatana tradition of patriarchy—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 114 / printed 113 -->

that is why, if a certain number of places are reserved for women and the law provides that only women may contest in those places, the sacred work of laying the foundation stone for the rights due to them will begin to be accomplished.

The reserved-constituency system already established constitutionally at every level to give a hand to the great oppressed community called Dalits so that they may obtain these rights should be taken as a “model”; without causing any damage to that existing arrangement, movements with social consciousness, intent on establishing the justice of granting rights, must come forward to accept the women’s reservation now being demanded and make a beginning.

It is the act of responsible people to wait patiently for public opinion to form; but “waiting” merely in order to postpone the demand becomes something that cannot be accepted.

Even if the votes required to pass the law are not obtained, can we not at least identify before the community of women those who refused to support this Bill?

Do you know what Rousseau—the great French thinker who lived in the eighteenth century, one of those whose writings laid the path of awakening for the French Revolution, and a contemporary of Voltaire—said about public opinion?

“When an agreement is reached to determine the public opinion required for undertaking something new, everyone’s view must be of one kind. But after that, at every stage, that public opinion cannot be shaped according to everyone’s wish. When differences of opinion arise in that way, the view held by the majority must be taken as public opinion.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 115 / printed 114 -->

Those eager to enact a law for women’s reservation must consider this view of Rousseau as well and come forward to think and act.

If, among the more than 500 seats in Parliament, certain constituencies are now to be made double-member constituencies, increasing the number of members and providing a place for both a man and a woman, does that not mean that men are unwilling to share the seats they now hold with women and lose 33 per cent of them? Therefore, does not the proposal “increase the number of seats further in order to give women separate places” become, indirectly and without those making it realising it, a stumbling block placed before the Bill?

If so, should there not be reservation for the Backward Classes and the Most Backward Classes?

There is only one answer to this: did we not argue and struggle—and win—first for Indian independence, and next for linguistic States?

At present, among constituencies for Parliamentary and Legislative Assembly elections (apart from those that are reserved constituencies), there is no constituency marked for men alone; nor are constituencies among men classified as Backward Classes and Most Backward Classes. In this situation—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 116 / printed 115 -->

the reservation being sought for women is only 33 per cent of the seats that already exist.

That is why we wholeheartedly welcome first making certain the 33 per cent for women, and then, step by step, making “internal reservation” for the Backward Classes and the Most Backward Classes!

First, let us accept the invitation to the feast and enter the house.

Then, among the dishes served at the feast, let us say which agrees with our body and which does not, and have them served accordingly.
