# Where Is Heaven? Where Is Heaven?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 188 / unit marker only -->

This birth—the next birth; this incarnation—another incarnation; in good Tamil, this world—the other world. Of these, this world alone is real! The other world is mere imagination. Though I say that the idea that man will be born again is imagination, I do indeed believe—without calling it imagination—that after he dies he reaches heaven or reaches hell.

What is this—some new confusion? A man who does not believe even in rebirth believes in heaven and hell? Is that not strange? One is bound to ask!

The moment a human being is born, he becomes a resident of this world—and while he is still living, he makes an advance reservation for a permit to enter either the world of heaven or the world of hell. Only very rarely does he exchange the permit for one of those two worlds and take back the other. What he takes back may be the permit for heaven—or, very rarely, the permit for hell. What is heaven? What would it be like? What would hell be like?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 189 / printed 188 -->

I am not going to compare those two worlds by describing their city layouts, roads, or the facilities available for the lives of those who dwell there. More or less, all these arrangements may be found without much difference from one another—hell appearing like heaven, and heaven like hell.

Then on what basis do we identify this as heaven and that as hell? The answer lies in the character of those who have already settled in those places and those who continue to settle there. Only by that can we determine what is heaven and what is hell.

There is an old story. Kalaivanar N. S. Krishnan often recalled and told it at various public events.

I shall now add a little seasoning and serve that story.

An elderly man lies ill in the hall of a house. At that moment the sound of a conch is heard in the street, announcing that a funeral procession is passing. The old man calls his grandson Murali and says, “Murali! Go out to the street and see. Follow the procession going to the cremation ground, and find out whether the person who has died is going to heaven or to hell!” So he sends the grandson away.

The grandson goes into the street, follows the funeral procession, and returns saying, “Grandfather! He is definitely being admitted to heaven!”

“How did you find that out?” asks the grandfather.

The grandson replies.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 190 / printed 189 -->

“I listened to what the people following the procession were saying. I also saw women standing at the entrances of their houses shedding tears. They said he was one who did good for everyone without expecting anything; one who gave and gave without turning his face away; one who embraced everyone with a blossoming heart; one who utterly disliked flattery.”

“Surely a man praised like this will receive heaven and go there, Grandfather!” said the grandson.

“Hey, Murali! He cannot go to heaven. Because there is no such separate thing as heaven. Do you know where heaven is?”

“What is this, Grandfather? You are contradicting what you said before. I do not understand at all!”

“Understand it now! Heaven is right here. The people who walked in the funeral procession and those who stood in the street watching—did they not speak of him? Did their eyes not fill with tears? **That** is the heaven he has reached.

“If, on the contrary, the people of the town feel relieved and say, ‘The affliction is gone,’ then that itself is hell for that person.”

The grandson said with a smile:

“Grandfather! Now I understand clearly. This world exists; there is no other world. At birth itself, a human being comes with his permit to be a resident of this world—and separately with two permits, one for settling in heaven and one in hell. Which of the two he uses is decided by the way he lives while he is a resident of this world—whether he decides it or not, his life itself decides it. Am I thinking correctly, Grandfather?”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 191 / printed 190 -->

“One hundred per cent correct. The other world is illusion—foolishness! Heaven and hell are not separate worlds. The praise that lives on after us is heaven; the contempt that lives on is hell. To understand that clearly is wisdom!”

Between the words of the two,
a song is heard.
Both listen
closely—

“Where is heaven?
Where is heaven? It
is here;
not there.”

It is a song by
Udumalai Narayana Kavirayar!
