# Conduct

<!-- Tamil source: scan 75 / unit marker only -->

Thinking, “How much the people of the town would look down on us if they came to know! Those who have turned hostile would get a chance to revile us! Even the way they look at us would be different!” and therefore making sure that such a situation does not come near oneself—a man or a woman being well-behaved for that reason cannot simply be called “chastity”.

Avoiding an act because murder or robbery will bring punishment, and not deviating from proper conduct because others may condemn us—if each is weighed in one’s own conscience, there is no value in it at all. Just as the breeze naturally has coolness and the summer sun naturally has heat, the quality called proper conduct is worthy of praise only when it naturally exists in men and women alike.

Otherwise, making oneself a person of proper conduct would be no different from installing devices to keep the surroundings warm in winter and pleasantly cool in summer.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 76 / printed 75 -->

Think of theft: “Good heavens! That is an evil deed! If I am caught, prison!” Rather than restraining oneself out of that fear and, because of it, abandoning the thought itself, is it not the greater “dignity”, worthy of praise and respect, for that thought never to arise in the mind at all?

Not theft alone: with any evil act, with cruel deeds such as murder and rape, “integrity” means not merely avoiding them after thinking about the consequences they may bring, but having the evil thought fail to arise naturally in the first place! Those people of integrity are the ones who will be placed in the ranks of the humane and honoured.

If, instead of conduct arising naturally—and not merely out of fear of consequences—acts opposed to proper conduct and hostile to humaneness nevertheless take place, either because one failed to think about their consequences or even after thinking about them, methods have been devised through the ages to safeguard social conduct by bringing those responsible before the law. Those methods differ from one another in many parts of the world.

However many forms they may take, their fundamental purpose is to prevent those crimes from occurring again by imposing punishment for crimes.

But rather than punishments, fear that society will condemn us, or the warning that the law is watching being used to prevent crimes—or merely serving to preserve an individual’s conduct—it is being naturally upright that can make men and women alike worthy of being praised as chaste, humane, dignified and people of integrity.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 77 / printed 76 -->

A disposition that honours culture belongs not only to the society of old; it belongs also to a progressive, modern society travelling on the path of rationalism. If these ideas take the form of action on the basis of that general thought—understanding Socrates’ teaching, “Know thyself”, realising oneself, and holding the faith that “if one conquers oneself, one can conquer the world”—they can show society a new path, a revolutionary path.
