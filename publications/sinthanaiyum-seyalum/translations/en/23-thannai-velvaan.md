# He Who Conquers Himself

<!-- Tamil source: scan 101 / unit marker only -->

An argument is put forward: “Astrology is not false; it is astrologers who end up speaking falsely.” Many examples are also offered to strengthen that argument.

“Astrology and omens are nothing but calculations. They are calculated from the positions of the planets. If even the tiniest fraction goes wrong in that calculation, what is foretold will fail to come true. Look at the Mittadar from the neighbouring village: several hundred acres of land—plenty of money in the banks—property handed down generation after generation from the days of his grandfather and great-grandfather. He moved to the town; somehow all the property vanished; now he supports his family by working as a clerk in a private company. There are people who tell us, convincingly enough that even we may believe them, that it had already been calculated and written in his horoscope that this would happen—that he would lose everything by playing cards and gambling on horse races—and that the horoscope itself was shown to them, and that they read it and were amazed. Such events may even have been indicated beforehand in coded words under a small heading such as ‘future results’ in that Mittadar’s life-record.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 102 / printed 101 -->

I do not close my eyes and deny all this merely because I must deny it at any cost. The reasonable argument I put forward is simply this: what benefit is there in learning our horoscope’s prediction beforehand? One may feel like asking, “Could not that Mittadar have remained alert and avoided gambling?” But how could that be possible? If his horoscope says he will develop a passion for gambling, and if the zodiacal results and planetary positions at the time of his birth have drawn in firm lines that he will therefore suffer a great loss of wealth, will his horoscope allow him to cross that line and act differently?

If that horoscope had foretold that at a certain age he would develop a passion for gambling and lose wealth because of it, should it not also have said that, according to that very horoscope, he would receive such a warning, take the good path, and escape?

If the horoscope says that he will escape in that way, then is that not something that must happen by itself? There is no necessity for a fortune-teller to announce it in advance, is there? If so, should the horoscope not also say that the fortune-teller will announce it beforehand and that, on that basis, the Mittadar will protect himself? Yet that question is left without an explanation!

If the Mittadar keeps himself alert and avoids going down the wrong path, that too must have been recorded in the horoscope exactly so. If it is not recorded, does that not leave the horoscope without any basis for credibility?

What happens; letting what is to happen simply happen—preventing what is to happen—and, when prevented, its not happening—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 103 / printed 102 -->

if all of these too are only according to the horoscope, would not vows of atonement and rituals for removing `dosham` be wasted like water poured on barren grass? There would be no need for anyone to worry about them at all!

Whether we will worry or not—the horoscope itself should say that too. If events take place contrary to what it says, then why believe in the horoscope at all?

Suppose the horoscope says that the same Mittadar has only one more year to live and that one day within that year he will suddenly die of a heart attack. Would he not spend every day, every minute, thinking of that alone and worrying? Even if, as I mentioned at the beginning, we were for a moment to accept that a horoscope is trustworthy, is it necessary to hear such a thing and know it beforehand—and then, because of that very worry, actually suffer a decline in health? Does that question now assume a gigantic form or not?

Therefore I say: instead of confusing the brain with stars, Rahu Kalam, Kuligai Kalam, Amrita-yogam and Marana-yogam; with which house Guru occupies, which house the Moon occupies, which house the Sun and Venus occupy; instead of wasting a portion of one’s lifetime in the middle of life itself; instead of sinking into beliefs in astrology and horoscopes and allowing needless waves of emotion to disturb blood pressure and become a problem—let us understand that the brain exists to create new things in the world, and that bodily health and favourable surroundings must be shaped accordingly. Those who live with the full confidence that knowledge and strength enough to conquer the earth will gather in one who conquers himself will not cling to omens and astrology and weep over them!

11 - 8 - 2005
