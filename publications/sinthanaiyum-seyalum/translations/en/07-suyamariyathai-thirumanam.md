# Self-Respect Marriage

<!-- Tamil source: scan 42 / unit marker only -->

“You wrote to the Prime Minister asking that Self-Respect marriages be given legal validity throughout India, and you sent copies of the law passed in Tamil Nadu in 1967 under Anna’s government to the Prime Minister and the Speaker of Parliament. `Dinamalar` has published an article criticising this. What do you say about it?”

“Very happy!

At least under the guise of refuting and condemning it, they have brought this important news to light, have they not?

It has been about eighty years since Thanthai Periyar introduced the Self-Respect marriage system. Thousands upon thousands have conducted their marriages in this manner. In the sixty years of my public life, the number of marriages I myself have conducted in this manner would not be fewer than a thousand. If we count the marriages conducted by Periyar, Anna and the leaders of our movement, the number would run into tens of thousands.

Yet until 1967 these marriages had no legal recognition. In Tamil Nadu itself, nearly forty years passed before that recognition was obtained.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 43 / printed 42 -->

Only after Anna’s government came to office in 1967, and after consultation with Thanthai Periyar, did that long-standing demand become law.

I have recorded this in the first volume of `Nenjukku Neethi`:

“Thanthai Periyar was the mother who first set Self-Respect marriage crawling in Tamil Nadu.

Rejecting the old marriage ritual performed in a language the couple could not understand, with irrational scriptural rites and ceremonies, he fashioned a reform marriage: in one’s understood mother tongue, the bride and groom make a pledge of a life contract and exchange garlands. That is enough.

In the beginning there were walls of obstruction—verbal blows—stone blows. Yet, overcoming all of them, tens of thousands came forward to conduct such marriages.

But those marriages had no legal protection, especially in matters concerning property and related rights. In some cases, courts too had ruled that such marriages were not valid.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 44 / printed 43 -->

When the DMK came to power, one of its important programmes was to give Self-Respect marriages legal validity. In July 1967, Law Minister and friend Madhavan introduced the bill. It was sent to a committee headed by him and was later passed into law.”

Leaders and scholars from other states too have praised Self-Respect marriages.

Giving Self-Respect marriage legal recognition does not mean that everyone is compelled to marry only in that manner. Those who wish may marry by the reform, Self-Respect method; those who wish may use the priestly method. It depends on one’s choice and one’s interest in rationalism. There is no compulsion.

`Dinamalar` says that Self-Respect marriage has not attained prominence throughout India.

Buddha’s doctrine, subjected to a blackout in the land where it was born, later flourished greatly in other lands. The same kind of blackout has been imposed in Tamil Nadu upon Periyar’s doctrine.

But Periyar’s movement for dignity has not remained confined to Tamil Nadu. Its principle has grown and gained strength to the point where even the Government of India must accept a secular position.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 45 / printed 44 -->

In the same way, if the marriage with which life begins is founded on Self-Respect, there will slowly—but surely—come a time when it gains acceptance throughout India. Even in Tamil Nadu it took nearly forty years to receive legal recognition.

So I welcome even the indirect support offered by the `Dinamalar` writer. Though he has not come forward to support it constructively, instead of blacking the matter out completely, he has at least published a rebuttal and thereby brought it before the public.

Swimming against the current—always—
is the training Periyar gave us.
Even now, we shall win through it.”

13-5-2005
