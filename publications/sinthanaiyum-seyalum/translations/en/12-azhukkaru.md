# Azhukkaaru

<!-- Tamil source: scan 59 / unit marker only -->

“For those possessed by envy, that alone suffices;
even if enemies fail, it brings ruin.”

No other enemy is needed to bring down those who possess the trait of jealousy; that trait itself will bring them down!

Wise people will not allow an `azhukkaaru`—a foul stream—to run through the middle of a town, or to stagnate and swell in some part of the town.

Drinking the water of that foul stream—
bathing in that water—
the stench that rises from the foul stream,
mixing with the air and entering the town—
because these cause grave harm
to the welfare of the people,

we set it apart as harmful to body and life. We move away from it.

The trait of jealousy too is called `azhukkaaru`. Ayya Valluvar says that if a person is blessed with the quality of being free of that trait, there can be no greater good fortune.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 60 / printed 59 -->

The word `aaru` can mean a way; it can also mean a river. `Aatrupaduthal` can denote guiding something along a way and sending it onward.

None of these should be joined with dirt. Only when dirt joins them and it becomes `azhukkaaru`—a dirty way, a dirty river—does it become clear that this is an “evil path” into which we should not even attempt to descend and look.

Just as an `azhukkaaru` within a town is a great danger to that town’s health, the jealous trait called `azhukkaaru` can pollute the human heart itself.

When does jealousy arise?

It arises when someone else gets something that we did not get! When someone else gets something even a little higher than what we have received, jealousy twists us up and torments us! Even if that other person is a friend, jealousy grants no exemption; intent and vigilant, it carries out its duty.

This disease of jealousy has a nature that lets it take hold easily among people in the same field—music, art, writing, public speaking, government offices, intellectual research, administration, the judiciary, politics, economics, society and fields of that kind. It is among people within the same field that we can see this disease present without let-up. Why stop there? Among those who proclaim one policy and one goal and march beneath the shade of the same flag, a split that even the opposing camp cannot create can be produced by the sinner called `azhukkaaru`, entering without permission, says the Kural!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 61 / printed 60 -->

There have even been incidents in husband-and-wife relationships in which this has produced `kalaam`.

We praise the American scientist who goes to the sphere of the moon, leaves his mark there and returns. But if a man named Chandran, living in the next street of our own town, achieves something like it, jealousy gets there before our praise does. Leave aside honouring and praising him—can we at least refrain from being jealous of him?

What does this show? Even if someone near us, someone familiar to us, is a scholar of many arts, the very fact that he is nearby becomes his weakness.

The interest we show in watching a colourful butterfly flutter in the sky does not turn toward the rooster with colourful wings walking about in our own backyard, does it?

When our hearts throb at seeing the esteem due to the talent and distinction of one of our own reduced in this way, giving room to the sinner called `azhukkaaru`, who enters without permission, would be just like setting out a feast for a “plague”.

The preventive measure that will keep that plague from even peeping toward our border is to rejoice by treating all the good, prosperity, fame and praise that come to others as though they had come to us.
