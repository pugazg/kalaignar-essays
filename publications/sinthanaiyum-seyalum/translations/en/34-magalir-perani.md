# Women's Rally!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 148 / unit marker only -->

In an editorial Periyar wrote in the `Kudiyarasu` newspaper in 1928, he said that men raising their voices for women's rights and women's liberation was mere pretence; that no man truly laboured for it with genuine feeling. He warned women not to be deceived by the false voice of male domination. In the same editorial, he also pointed out that women raising the cry for women's rights on behalf of women could likewise end up fruitless.

We must not rush to the conclusion that Ayya Periyar therefore held such a sweeping and categorical general opinion about men and women alike.

What stood firmly rooted in Thanthai Periyar's deepest mind was that no one should put on a costume and pretend to labour for women's rights.

Just as he identified those who called themselves devotees of God while committing wicked acts in that very God's name, Periyar stripped away the disguise of those who pretended to speak for women's rights while being dyed-in-the-wool patriarchs. Because women too, through age after age, had become steeped in a servile mentality that accommodated that patriarchy—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 149 / printed 148 -->

we must understand Periyar as saying, in his own manner of speaking the truth in a loud voice, that it would not be so easy for them either to free themselves from that prison.

The use of the words “male domination” and “women's rights,” and vows that “we shall remove male domination and establish women's rights,” must not be understood to mean that male domination disappears or women's rights flourish only in a way of life that completely destroys the system in which men and women live together family by family, sends men wandering one way and women another as they please, and brings the two sexes together and separates them only as needs arise—whether the need is for food, for sexual relations, or for maintaining physical health. Such an understanding would carry us back to the old, old, very old age of savagery.

Therefore, after moving from a condition in which man or woman wandered and lived as each pleased to a condition of living together, when one person's conduct wounds the other's mind, both man and woman having the legal right to obtain consent for separation—without any threat that separating from each other is a sin or will bring a curse—is itself a benefit suited to both sexes.

In a social order where men and women have equal opportunity to obtain legal sanction to shape a path for their lives—and, if they realise that they have gone astray on it, to alter and reshape that path—the great deficiency is that they do not have equal rights to engage in public life and perform public service!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 150 / printed 149 -->

Who says there is such a barrier? One may feel like saying: if even public-welfare service for the people requires entering governing bodies, can any man or woman not contest as a candidate, win, and go there? Though that is the factual position, it is more accurate to call it a rusted factual position that has become useless.

The example is before us: even though men and women possess an equal right to contest any number of general constituencies, men continue in practice to occupy far more places, because of women's disposition to yield.

In Tamil Nadu, from 1996 under the DMK government, women were given 33 per cent reservation by law in local bodies, including municipal corporations, and that issue was resolved smoothly. While the system of reserved constituencies for Dalit people continues, reservation for women in local bodies too remains in practice.

Today's demand from women is that women be given opportunities in general constituencies and that, so that a specified number of them—at least to the extent of 33 per cent—may sit in Parliament and Legislative Assemblies, women's reservation must be provided by law.

For this demand to be fulfilled, experience itself shows that it is not practically possible merely for political parties to allot seats to women without enacting a law.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 151 / printed 150 -->

If a law is enacted and election rules framed so that, among those who contest a hundred constituencies, win and sit in Parliament or a Legislative Assembly, thirty-three are women—what danger will come of it!

They say the powerful deities Mari and Kali are female deities.

For wealth, they say Lakshmi; for education, Saraswati; for strength, Parvati.

Only Bharathi sang “Fatherland”—

all the rest of us call it “Motherland.”

In the motherland, is it right? Is it proper? to stand as a stumbling block before the just rights of the community of mothers?

They praise to the skies the Ardhanarishvara philosophy of half man and half woman; can they not come forward to support at least 33 per cent for women, if not 50 per cent?

There is no need even to praise and applaud the great rally, the first in all-India political history, in which three lakh women alone participated. At least understand the feeling in that voice of rights, and realise that it is the great duty of those born from a woman's womb to struggle so that the women's-rights battle begun by Periyar may, in Anna's path, achieve a great victory! Realise it—and act!

22 - 9 - 2005
