# Hitler

<!-- Tamil source: scan 104 / unit marker only -->

Generally, when autocrats and dictators are described, it is customary for only cruel portraits of them to be drawn. It is therefore natural to imagine that the pages of the life of Hitler—born in 1889, one who joined the ‘Nazi’ party in 1920, became a dictator in 1935, and was crowned with the title of a war-mad man who made the whole world tremble until 1945—could only be pages that make one shudder. Yet, while reading the book `Hitler - First World War`, one comes to know another side of the life of Hitler, whom this whole world denounces as a cruel man.

“Where is Germany? It is following behind Hitler. Where is Hitler? He is striding proudly ahead of Germany.”

Adolf Hitler once stood as the proud possessor of such lines of praise. I want everyone to know what I learned about him from that book, and so I give it here in the very form of the words of the book’s author, Mr. V. Saminatha Sarma.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 105 / printed 104 -->

“Adolf Hitler would speak with great affection to children, whoever their parents were; he would joke with them and run about playing with them. Seeing the extraordinary affection he had for children, the people of the country, during the celebration of his 47th birthday (20-4-1936), bought about a thousand cradles. He ordered that all of them be given for the use of poor children.

Hitler was outstanding in personal discipline. That is why he was full of self-confidence and strictness. He did not use cigarettes and such things; he did not drink alcohol; he would not even touch meat with his hand. The food he ate was like that of an ordinary peasant. There were not many varieties. He was satisfied with bread, fruit, milk and one kind of pulse. Even this simple food he would not sit and eat alone. He would eat only with one or two friends as guests.

He behaved towards women with great affection and respect. The fact that he gave motherhood a lawful place of honour itself shows the reverence he displayed towards the world of women.

He would surrender his mind entirely to high arts such as music and painting. He had a great desire to watch plays. He had much love for the music of Wagner, Beethoven and others—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 106 / printed 105 -->

whoever performed it, he would listen in self-forgetfulness. At such a time he would not even think of the dignity of his office. When the song ended, he would embrace the performer.

Hitler would do nothing for another person’s favour or indulgence. Especially in handling public money, he was extremely frugal and strict. Even Vankaar, who differed with him during the November revolution of 1923, says: ‘I do not agree with Hitler in every matter. Yet I cannot deny that he is a very honest man.’

Whether someone came to speak with Hitler in an official capacity or privately, he would rise and stand to receive them. When they took leave, he would accompany them for some distance, see them off, bow his head and offer his respects. Looking at his dress and manner, one could see no difference at all between him and an ordinary low-ranking employee. As in everything else, he was without ostentation in dress too.

With the aim of seeing him, hundreds of men, women, children and elderly people would gather from distant regions. They would wait for hours wondering when Hitler would come out and show himself. They would not mind snow or sun. Hitler would often come out and appear before them; he would mingle and speak with some; he would honour some by shaking their hands.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 107 / printed 106 -->

Hitler would observe everything keenly; he would study deeply; and from what he read he would note down important matters from time to time. It cannot be said that he was a great scholar like Mussolini. But he possessed abundant practical knowledge. He was a man of the people. It is said that no one else had the power he possessed to captivate ordinary people.

On the day the Saar region was handed over to Germany (1-3-1935), did he not suddenly come to Saarbrücken? There was heavy rain that day. Yet, out of eagerness to see Hitler, lakhs of people stood in the streets getting drenched. Hitler stood on a motor vehicle and showed himself to the people. Troops marched in formation before him. At that time Hitler was wearing a ‘raincoat’. Saying, ‘When all these people are getting drenched in the rain, why should I alone have this protection?’ he removed the raincoat and threw it away. He too stood in the rain. For more than about an hour, standing in the same place, he returned the respects of the people and the troops. As a result he caught a cold. But how greatly did this captivate the people?

Like all historical figures, Hitler wished to remain an ordinary peasant. He was satisfied with living a simple life. For that very reason he was especially loyal to poor people and farmers.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 108 / printed 107 -->

He was also eager to win their affection.

However much affection he had for children, he loved young people even more. For were they not Germany’s ‘military strength of the future’? When travelling, he would meet young people and ask what they were studying. He would pat them on the back and encourage them.”

*

When so many good qualities are said to have dwelt even in one portrayed as a cruel man, we must not simply calculate how many good qualities might therefore be piled up in those praised by the world as noble people. Could not some who were praised in that way have been—or even now be—impostors, people merely acting the part of the good?

These details about “Hitler’s other side” lay the basis for that doubt!
