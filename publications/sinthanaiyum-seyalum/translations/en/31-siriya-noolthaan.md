# Only a Small Book

<!-- Tamil source: scan 134 / unit marker only -->

For a long time, a library containing many thousands of books and furnished with the facilities needed to study them properly has been functioning at Anna Arivalayam in Chennai. Known as the “Perasiriyar Research Library,” it gives us great joy to see, day by day, the growing manner in which those thirsting for knowledge and those with an interest in literature make use of it.

Many rare books concerning every field have a place in that research library. Along with books bought and kept there, many generous people continue to donate large numbers of books as gifts. Mr. Sundararajan, who works there as librarian, has classified the thousands of books subject-wise; and there is no one who does not appreciate the way he himself comes forward, searches for books according to the needs of those who come for research, and helps them.

Those who come to see me, and those who have the habit of presenting books, give me piles of books. I keep them at home for a few days and then send them on to that research library.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 135 / printed 134 -->

Seeing that one such pile consisted of books concerning medicine, I have also been using some of them for writing under the title “Thought and Action.”

While taking up and stacking some books with the thought of sending them to the “Perasiriyar Research Library,” I found a book with the English words `Heart Attack` written in Tamil letters and with a dirty, torn cover. The book bore the number `4906` in my home library. Seeing its pitiable condition, I turned its pages with sympathy and caution so that it would not tear further.

`Heart Attack (Maaradaippu)
Practical Questions — Explanations`

The author of that book is the multi-field enthusiast Dr. A. Syed Ibrahim, known as “Himana Syed.” Dated 1-7-1990 and signed by him, the book had been presented to me fifteen years earlier. As I read it, and as I saw on its cover the address marked “Place where the book is available”—`Mallari Clinic, Sitharkottai, Panaikulam, Alagankulam`—it was easy to understand that the author belonged to Ramanathapuram district.

When I was in government, at a special function of the Ooty flower show, I once said in a speech: “I see the Nilgiris, enchanting us with their groves; and I think with distress of regions such as Mugavai district, which present the appearance of a desert.” Whenever anyone says “Ramanathapuram,” that speech immediately comes to my mind. Work to turn the desert into a green grove also continued under that government!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 136 / printed 135 -->

When I connect the title of the book in my hand—`Heart Attack`—with Ramanathapuram, I remember that I was among those who saw, heard and shed tears over the cruelty of a single family in which the father and three sons—all four of them—did not live beyond forty-five, but died of heart attacks before that age!

In politics they laboured night and day as Anna's younger brothers—as my beloved `Udanpirappugal`. Yes: Sathiyendran, Mahendran and Rajendran. These three brothers, one after another, were struck by heart disease and died before reaching forty-five. They had served as Members of Parliament and the Legislative Assembly and as municipal chairmen. Sudden heart attacks ended their lives!

“This may be called a hereditary disease. That is, if it has occurred to someone else on either the father's or mother's side of a family, this disease may occur.”

In keeping with what the author himself has written, the father of these three brothers, Kuppusamy Servai, too died at a young age from the same cause—a heart attack.

Because my friend Thennarasu and younger brother Thangavelan had told me the continuing tragic history of that family, I warned those brothers many times and advised them to undergo surgery beforehand. They hesitated to follow that advice, out of fear of surgery!

Thennarasu delayed, and because he delayed, time conquered him too. Beginning with Cumbum Rajangam, M.P., then Cumbum Natarasan, M.P., and recently—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 137 / printed 136 -->

K. So. Ganesan—how many losses have we had to face because there was no caution, appropriate treatment was not obtained, and doctors' advice was not taken to heart. This small book, `Heart Attack`, priced at eight rupees, which I have found and which has come into my hands, can be of great use in preventing, to a considerable extent, young people in every movement from meeting such an end.

I am not writing this with the intention of advertising the sale of that book. Books about heart disease—and books about every disease and about treatment—have appeared in large numbers. Merely finishing reading them is a great task! But this book is small, can be read quickly, and briefly explains many matters such as how to recover if heart disease strikes and how to prevent it from coming in the first place. I write about it only with the thought that everyone can easily read it and benefit from it.

In this book I mention,
there are many important details
about heart attacks; yet the advice
given on how those who have suffered
one heart attack must remain cautious
can also be useful in preventing
a heart attack from occurring
even the first time.
It is not enough that those who read
this book merely understand that—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 138 / printed 137 -->

they must act in accordance with what they understand.

Doctors' treatment—their advice—and, standing beside them with the feeling of a friend, this small book called `Heart Attack`: because it can serve as such a companion, it may be brought out in many more editions and spread widely. This is not writing produced merely because something had to be written. My writing, at any time,

—is not something that belongs
on such a list;

it is written so that
everyone may live!

10 - 9 - 2005
