# The Power of the Ballot

<!-- Tamil source: scan 39 / unit marker only -->

The people who bear the responsibility of establishing people’s rule—government formed by the people for those very people—hear someone say from public platforms that the power to create that government lies in their own hands. They read the same thing in newspapers and books. Yet when they hear it or read it, they do not seem to feel any pride in it.

An even greater pain is this: they do not, of their own accord, come forward even to express or use that magnificent power they possess. I think of this with deep shame, as one tiny drop in that very multitude of people.

Why has the dignity of people’s power faded in this way? One major reason is that some of the pillars the people chose and planted to build the hall called people’s rule begin to sway; some totter without a firm hold on the ground; and some pillars strike hard upon the very heads of those who chose and installed them.

Monarchy,

autocratic rule,

rule by a council of lords,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 40 / printed 39 -->

people’s rule—

though many forms of government are named, Aristotle accepts the conclusion that the democratic system of people’s rule is the best: it is the least flawed, and because all people take part in government, there is a way to punish or remove officials appointed under that government when they commit wrongs.

Thus democracy, considered the best among existing systems of government and followed as such, is the system put into operation in our sacred land of India. If the abuses that have sprouted like weeds even within this system are to be removed, the fundamental force required is again the strength or power of the people. For only they can completely remove the evils that arise in a government established by them.

The numerical strength of the people called the majority is used to support some view or to choose some person; but the people themselves are the ones who make up that majority, are they not?

It is precisely to demonstrate their numbers that the electoral system requires votes to be recorded at polling booths and then counted.

These days, even to get voters to enter those polling booths, welcoming arches seem to have to be erected for them.

The cruelty in this is that some people regard the performance of this democratic duty as a one-day wage job available to voters—and some voters agree, accept that wage and then carry out the task!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 41 / printed 40 -->

Is not the philosophy of people’s rule the very thing that gets forgotten—made to be forgotten—in all this?

“You belong to this country. In this country, whether at the Centre, in the States, in metropolitan cities and towns, in large villages or small villages, the governing bodies that come into being are formed by you. Whether the country grows or declines depends upon the vote you cast—”

When the very people through whom an election is conducted shrug off the responsibility of determining victory and defeat in that election, does democracy not become a farce?

If we ourselves do not understand the power and dignity of the ballot paper placed in our hands to create an organization and to choose a leader or a member for it, can anyone say that this mass of people, which includes us too, deserves even a sesame-seed’s tip worth of respect?

Therefore people must not think of themselves merely as voters. They are the sculptors of this country, the guardians of society, the machines that determine the future. In short, as Bharathi put it, they must establish the truth that “Everyone is a king of this land”—they must establish it, and establish it without fail.

Only then will democracy, now gathering dust,

become a deeply ploughed field where ripened grain sways!

9-5-2005
