# A Deep Ocean of Spirituality

<!-- Tamil source: scan 85 / unit marker only -->

He was known as Kundrakudi Adigalar among Tamils spread across the world. In Sivaganga district he was the head of the Kundrakudi Adheenam, bearing the titular names Arutperunthiru Adigalar Deiva Sigamani Arunachala Desikar. He was a deep ocean in the spiritual field. Into that sea he launched the boat of knowledge, gathered progressive pearls, and happily set them in place so that humaneness might flourish.

Whenever Thanthai Periyar addressed him on public platforms, he would call him only, “Kundrakudi Maha Sannidhanam!” When some ardent rationalists questioned Periyar about this, do you know what answer Periyar gave?

“If Adigalar had merely remained immersed in the spiritual field, without concern or labour for the growth of knowledge or the advancement of Tamils—if he had been a man without social consciousness—many among the people would have celebrated him merely as a Maha Sannidhanam. But Adigalar did not shine merely as a spiritual sage; he shone as a rationalist thinker, a worker for social reform, a man of Tamil feeling. Only if we too accept and address him as ‘Maha Sannidhanam’ will it be an honour to us—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 86 / printed 85 -->

an honour to him—and a guide to others that this is how he should be addressed.”

That was the detailed answer Thanthai Periyar gave.

When I was in charge of the government, I had the privilege of nominating him as a member of the Tamil Nadu Legislative Council. At first he hesitated to accept that responsibility. I sent the then Law Minister, Mr. Madhavan, to him; after that he gave his consent, and the appointment took place in August 1969.

In connection with it, Kundrakudi Adigalar wrote and sent me a poem expressing his thanks. It appeared in many journals at the time. It also found a place in `Nenjukku Neethi`. The poem is as follows:-

“Steeped in Tamil and graced with compassion,
sweet Kalaignar! In the distinction
bestowed by your triumph, these have blossomed!
Valluvam, laying down the way to live,
raised its voice for labour to uplift the community
in this splendid world, never saying
that any season is a season for idleness!
We saw the great scholar Anna,
who lived as rare literature unto that voice,
without deviation, thinking of this good,
and pouring out love without shore or limit.
Valluvar said that living itself is an art;
Valluvar’s path and Anna’s path—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 87 / printed 86 -->

taking these as the two legs by which life proceeds,
raincloud Kalaignar of flourishing Tamil,
patron who creates a new `Purananuru`!
Patron! Patron—not merely a patron,
but a mighty poet who conquers even peerless cadence,
an upright orator whose speech flows in ordered sweetness,
a writer of soaring stature,
a minister who day after day seeks and does good,
ploughing red words in the heart until they ripen,
sowing the seed called love and reaping its yield—
dear Kalaignar, what did you have in mind
in causing us to remain in this world?
Even after thinking for a long time,
it is not clear; it is not clear!
Was it love kindled by that foremost word, ‘Anna’,
in the names of our Adheenam
and the Annamalai Adheenam?
Was it because, by the language we speak
and by the path we cherish,
you thought us bound to the Tamil people?
Was it to stand and keep Kundrakudi from declining?
Did you think, ‘Let us raise this man and see’?
Was it to take one who sat withdrawn in a monastery—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 88 / printed 87 -->

place him in the Assembly and watch the spectacle?
From the day we learned the language into which we were born,
the savour of the path of service has been our destiny;
was it to lead us too along that path of service
and spur us onward? Was it the affection of saying
that in Tanjai soil, famed across the earth
for its ancient distinction, we were born together?
Was it your thought to see and remove
the continuing pain that came from taking up
certain fields and rendering service in days gone by?
Though we possess no fitting great scholarship
in the field of the Tamil language,
was it the broad thought that we are rich
in love for beautiful Tamil?
Did you remember the affection Periyar bore toward us,
he who worked to raise a people
that had lain branded as a low caste?
We know not what it was! Nor can we understand
what we are to do! One thing alone we ask:
may your friendship remain as it is today!
For giving us the opportunity to stand beside
the work you do for this fame-bearing earth,
our heartfelt thanks! We have spoken with joy.”

Thus turning question marks into exclamation marks;

<!-- Tamil source: scan 89 / printed 88 -->

“With all seven births too, we shall hold it in our hearts;
we shall hail the power of enduring love.”

With these words Adigalar brings that poem to a close, overflowing with gratitude!

We who belong to the rationalist movement are not wholesale opponents of spiritualists. And spiritualists too must become workers for a social order that is spotless and without high and low—the very work of the rationalist movement. For Periyar, Anna, and we who labour in their path to stand as examples of this, this one account concerning Kundrakudi Adigalar is itself fitting evidence.
