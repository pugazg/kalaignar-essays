# Astrological Sorrow!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 82 / unit marker only -->

The person who examined and calculated the good day and auspicious time for Rama’s coronation was no ordinary man. He was Vasishtar, a master of every art who had absorbed the scriptures as though he had dissolved and drunk them. Yet on the very day he fixed, Rama—who was to take up the sceptre and rule the land—had instead to wear bark garments and enter the forest with his wife Sita and his younger brother Lakshmana. Even after reading this in the Ramayana epic, human beings still wander about surrendering their reason to almanac-beliefs—omens, astrology, day and star, Siddha-yogam, Amrita-yogam, Marana-yogam.

There are still people living who regard this condition as a defeat suffered by the rationalist movement—and as a victory gained by believers in superstition.

What cruelties have shattered many families because of such belief! How many lives have been ruined! How much deception have people been subjected to! How many swindlers have been caught in the grip of the law! Even after hearing all this as news, does not the number of people who still make room for those beliefs and ruin themselves continue to grow?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 83 / printed 82 -->

Here is one report! In Udayampalayam, Coimbatore, there is a street called N.G.R. Street! Yes; that street’s name stirs an old memory in me!

N.G. Ramasamy was a trade-union leader—a communist rationalist—who was killed by enemies because of political hostility. In 1946–47, at Singanallur, I lived in a house belonging to his brother-in-law (Annasamy’s house).

On the street bearing that N.G.R. name, a man called Raghupathi runs a hotel. His wife is Chandrakala. They had married only two years earlier; a child was born to them and they named him ‘Vigneshwaran’. Their life had been going well when an astrologer intruded into it.

Raghupathi gave him the child’s horoscope and asked him to examine it.

“The child Vigneshwaran is a danger to the father’s life; therefore the child must be separated from the father—this is what the horoscope says,”

said the astrologer.

What happened after that? Here is what a newspaper published on 21 July 2005 says:-

“Distressed by what the astrologer had said, Raghupathi from that day began to look with hatred upon the infant Vigneshwaran, who was only nine months old. Raghupathi, who often came home after drinking, would abuse the child and quarrel with his wife. In this situation, last Sunday he came home intending to kill the child.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 84 / printed 83 -->

Raghupathi asked his wife to let him take the child out on his motorcycle. But his wife Chandrakala refused. He then snatched the child from his wife and, without even considering that it was his own child, struck him again and again on the hands, legs and waist. He then appears to have thrown the child onto the cot and assaulted him there as well. As a result, the tender infant’s head drooped and he lost consciousness. The child was first admitted to a private hospital in Peelamedu. Later he was admitted to the Coimbatore Government Hospital, where he died pitiably. In connection with this, the Peelamedu police inspector registered a case, arrested the father Raghupathi and sent him to prison. Chandrakala, the mother who had lost her child, wept and lamented.”

Is this just one such case? Two? There have been so many cruel incidents! People lose their reason—and calamities arise from it!

If horoscopes and astrology were true, should not that astrologer have said, “According to the horoscope under which the child was born, the child will live for only nine months; within those nine months the child’s own father will kill him”? Therefore, let us think how much faster and how much more widely rationalist propaganda must spread in order to prevent the disasters caused by such fraudulent astrologers—and let us act accordingly!
