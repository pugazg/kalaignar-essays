# Gurukulam!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 33 / unit marker only -->

There are those who mock gurukulam training as something belonging to olden times. It once existed on the scale of “one guru—one student.” Today, however much teaching may take place in classrooms, students still go in search of gurus who, under the English name “tuition,” train no fewer than ten or fifteen disciples at a time. The name may be different if you wish; gurukulam training is still the foundation beneath it. A guru does not seat the disciple before him every morning, evening and even at midnight and personally teach him akara practice day after day.

Just as a guide does not travel the entire journey along with the traveller, a guru may spend a day or two showing the disciple how to practise akara so that the voice gains richness and the throat comes under control. Having shown the path to be travelled, the guru, satisfied that the way is clear, thinks, “Now it is for the disciple to sing,” and turns to his own work.

Massaging the guru’s body, washing his clothes, running at the call of the guru’s wife and happily completing the tasks she gives—such customs have now largely been set aside. Yet here and there we can still see the practice of staying closely with the guru and receiving training as part of his household.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 34 / printed 33 -->

Nor can I reject that practice outright. In my youth, the meetings at which Erode Periyar, Kanchi Anna and Pattukkottai Azhagiri spoke were my classes in rationalism. I had already learnt something of the lesson of Self-Respect, but there can be no two opinions about how greatly my gurukulam stay at Periyar’s `Kudiyarasu` office in Erode helped me gain greater clarity in that training. Before me, that gurukulam stay had helped people such as Anna enormously. My friends Thavamani Rasan, Karunanandam and Sanarthanam were gurukulam residents in Erode along with me. Beloved younger brother Veeramani too received gurukulam training and gained both the qualification and the opportunity appropriate to the guru’s own seat.

Whether in science, in the realm of music, or in such varied fields as painting, epic literature and sculpture, whoever has won fame—even if they now carry the full weight of old age—will, when interviewed, never fail to speak of the distinctive experiences of the gurukulam in which they received individual training.

In musical training—whether vocal music, string instruments or skin-percussion instruments—to reach mastery one remains close to the guru’s seat and learns day after day. In the same way, with no particular hour fixed as “teaching time,” I learnt at the Erode gurukulam whenever opportunity and time allowed: after the evening meal in the moonlit courtyard of the upper terrace; during the day in the office room; even on the house verandah in the predawn before morning had budded. How much pride—how much distinction—how much firmness the benefit of that learning still gives me today!

Even after one has learnt well and gained mastery—even after receiving countless good certificates—when one sometimes treats even a friend as a guru and clears away doubts,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 35 / printed 34 -->

there too we can see the experience of the gurukulam at work.

I was able to make a transition between gurus who taught principles: Periyar and Anna. Both taught the same lessons of rationalism and of dignity for our people. But when the opportunity came for me, in turn, to become a teacher, they taught me two different ways of teaching those same lessons. There was one medicine for curing the disease: Anna taught me the method of mixing it with honey and giving it; Periyar said, “Mix it with nothing—swallow the powder as it is.”

Both methods were learnt by staying close within the gurukulam. The method of making the medicine is the same! Only the ways of taking it are two! So, even if “gurukulam” appears to be an old style, it can give us the special training that makes us wielders of the weapon of knowledge. This is a truth I have learnt from my own experience!

3-5-2005
