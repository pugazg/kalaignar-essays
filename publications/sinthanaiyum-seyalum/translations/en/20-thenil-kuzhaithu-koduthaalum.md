# Even If Mixed with Honey...!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 90 / unit marker only -->

Among the nations of the world, a country’s antiquity and culture may proclaim its greatness. Painters, sculptors and poets may be the reason for a country’s fame. There are countries renowned for their literature. Histories continue to herald a country’s distinction.

There are great countries that have gained many countries as friendly nations.

There are powerful countries that have brought many nations to their side through military strength—

crocodile-like countries that, through commerce, have bent many countries around themselves and stand among the first-rank nations—

countries that have gained strength in the economic field—

countries that have achieved success in scientific advancement and space research—

and countries speeding toward that success—

we can take down the books stacked in libraries and read about any country that wields influence in these many different ways. In today’s computer age, we can learn about them easily through the “Internet” as well.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 91 / printed 90 -->

The world watches with deep concern as the number of countries being affected in these ways—by good things or by things that are not good, rightly or wrongly—continues to increase in the present day.

When I speak of a country or of the world, no one should take me to mean merely a geographical expanse of land. It should be understood as regions in which people live—or, to put it still more clearly, as referring to masses of people.

The deeds of Hitler, the antics of Mussolini, the outrages of the Tsar, Rasputin and others, and the cruelties carried out by the Orabi kings still remain as scars from festering wounds inflicted upon those countries. It is around those scars that histories have taken shape, so as to teach wisdom to future generations.

Without gathering armies like the tyrants I have mentioned, without parading forces in the sky and on the earth, a savage cruelty has begun to set foot—suddenly, suddenly, to a startling degree—first in one or two countries, and now rapidly in very many countries.

Its name is “terrorism”; there are also those who more delicately call it “extremism”. Both produce the same result.

Suppose a snake is hiding in one of four or five anthills in a flower garden, but one does not know exactly which. To catch the snake, destroying every anthill by laying waste to the entire garden—that is the evil sport enacted by an extremist attack. Perhaps, many years from now, whether it is called extremism or terrorism, it may be eradicated completely.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 92 / printed 91 -->

Whether the writer of this and those who today stand stunned on hearing news from every direction of extremist acts will live to see those peaceful, good days or not, my great hope is that these stones too may prove useful in the work of laying the foundation for days when well-being and peace prevail and people can breathe a great sigh of relief!

What is the root cause of extremism?

Chiefly, religious conflicts!

Border disputes!

That conflict burns a train; why, it delights in burning an entire town! Lofty towers curl into ash in the torches of extremists; they are shattered into fragments!

Entire families are going to the pit of death under extremist attacks! What have the children done? Did a child in a Muslim home insult a Hindu? Did a child in a Hindu home demean the little one in a Muslim home? Then why? In attacks carried out by extremists, those poor children are struck down as though the necks of green parrots were being snapped!

Are not the bombings carried out by these terrorists prime examples of the saying that the sin lies with one and the blame falls on another?

What of the old people who lie groaning, their pulse weakened, their nerves worn, their flesh and skin dried and shrivelled—did they come carrying brutal weapons, for terrorists to tremble in fear?

Then why, in the name of extremism, should everyone indiscriminately become a victim of the terrorists’ dreadful conspiratorial acts?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 93 / printed 92 -->

Can religious conflict be resolved by this?

Instead of bombs, if both sides exchange bouquets, argue truthfully, and bring about a condition in which—what Hindu, what Muslim, what Christian, what Buddhist, what Jain?—every religion is acceptable; if border disputes too find agreement in the contest of claims called “your country, my country”—until such a rare condition is created, believing that the preparations for it must be made through extremism, and arranging practice accordingly, can only amount to sitting on the tip of a branch and cutting the branch beneath it.

To attain any goal—whether it is accumulated material wealth, land, power or secularism—abandoning the unwanted thought that each of these, or all of them, can be won only through violent extremist acts is the good path of peaceful means that, even if somewhat delayed, can bring benefit to human society.

“No, no! To drive away the bedbugs, I will burn down the house!”—that is perverse argument. Acting on it is not merely a disastrous act; it is a painful act carried out by those who do not understand what they are doing! Therefore, let us throw down the torch of extremism; let us apply the proper medicine to drive away the bedbugs and save the house; let us unfailingly practise inner purity and outward purity! Even if mixed with honey and given to us, ‘extremism’ does not become medicine. It remains an incurable poison.

5 - 8 - 2005
