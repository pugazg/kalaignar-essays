# The Enchantment Drug

<!-- Tamil source: scan 78 / unit marker only -->

Some people falsely claim that there are certain drugs with which a woman can enchant a man, draw him to her side and keep him there. Some women believe this, buy those drugs and give them to men, and the men may become ill because of them. Saying that such a drug has been placed in a man, that this is why he has fallen ill, that they will remove the drug from his stomach and that he will recover once it is removed, some holy men or bogus physicians take a great deal of money and begin such treatment.

Mothers-in-law and sisters-in-law may even curse a daughter-in-law, saying, “She has drugged and bewitched him; he is lying there under her spell.”

Dr. Thirumalai Natarasan, R.G.M.P. (Siddha), who has written and published many valuable books on Tamil medicine, explains what a fraud this business of drugging someone into enchantment is. In an article titled “Drug-Placing Enchantment Disease” in his book `Siddha Maruthuva Kalanjiyam`, he records an experience of his own. It is as follows:-

“Whether a person can be drawn to one’s side by means of a drug is the important question.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 79 / printed 78 -->

Even today there are people who come to me believing that they suffer from this disease.

Whatever one tells them, they keep repeating what they themselves have said.

Some medical books say that there are ten kinds of drugs capable of bringing a person under one’s control, and they also give methods for preparing them. It can be stated with certainty that today no one can properly prepare even one of the drugs mentioned there. Even identifying and understanding some of the ingredients specified for these drugs is quite impossible.

This is a cruel conspiracy practised as a profession by a few people.

For the most part, what we hear is that women have drugged men and bewitched them. We can see such patients too. It is rare to hear that a man has drugged and bewitched a woman. Do these enchantment drugs work only on men? Would they have no effect if given to a woman? We must think. A drug is common to men and women alike.

Those afflicted by this supposed drug-placing disease describe the symptoms as stomach pain, faintness, lack of desire for food, no pain when they go to the house of the person said to have placed the drug, the return of hunger there, bodily weakness, being unable to leave that house, and having no thought at all of their own home.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 80 / printed 79 -->

There is another amusing claim as well: the drug supposedly remains in the stomach as the very lump that was placed there, and when it is removed it supposedly comes out as a lump.

I once went to watch the removal of a drug from someone I knew. The “great physician” who removed such drugs gave him a powerful purgative. The man who supposedly had the drug suffered stomach pain and writhed. Diarrhoea followed. He passed stool in the open ground. After every bout of diarrhoea, the great physician poked through the faeces with a stick and examined it. I was there too. There were eight bouts. At the end the great physician again stirred the faeces with the stick. I saw him put a little ball in with the stick and then take it out with his own hand, but I watched as though I had seen nothing. He washed the ball and showed it first to me. I took it in my hand, looked at it, and within a second knew exactly what that ball was.

A few days later the same person again had stomach pain. They went to the same great physician. Saying that a little more of the drug was still inside, he removed the drug again. I went too. The great physician again took out a piece of “drug”, this time much smaller than the one he had removed before. I returned without saying anything on this occasion either.

One day I went to see that great physician. I found him. I told him, “The way you remove drugs is magnificent.” “Very happy,” he said, and then, “This is very difficult, sir.” I could not remain silent. “What is difficult about rolling wax in charcoal powder, putting it in the faeces and then taking it out?” I asked. He was stunned. “It is for my livelihood, sir,” he said pitifully.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 81 / printed 80 -->

“Sir, do you know one more thing? Some people really do get cured, sir.” “Yes,” I said. I asked, “If he gets stomach pain again after this, what will you do?” “I will have to say, ‘The woman who gave him the drug has done sorcery,’ and perform a mantra,” he said. “And if the illness does not go away even after the mantra?” “Then I will have to pass him on to a bigger sorcerer,” he said. What can one do? I returned thinking that society, without understanding people like this, continues to nurture them.

Why have I had to write all this? They believe in ghosts without understanding illness; when will these people reform?”

People have been pushed into the darkness of ignorance even in the name of medicines. Why, even now, people in rural areas continue to live with such beliefs. Educated young people must come forward to reform all of them and enable them to live with understanding. They must make the spreading of rationalism their task.

18 - 7 - 2005
