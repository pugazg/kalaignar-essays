# My Name Is Revolution!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 28 / unit marker only -->

“Revolution”—the upper lip and lower lip press together; the sound breaks past them and bursts out; the tongue strikes the row of teeth as though beating a drum. There is a surge even in the very utterance of my name!

The moment I come out dressed in red, torn clothes and tatters gather around me, raising their hands like daggers. Will you throw them all into prison? Will you impose life imprisonment as a severe punishment? Or, because I would gladly welcome a death sentence that ends in an instant and thereby find a place in history, will you give me that punishment? Whatever it is, give me a punishment fitting one whose name is Revolution!

When I rose in fury against King Louis in France, the first thing I did was bury mercy in a grave; only then did I bring the ruling class of that day before the guillotine.

Otherwise, before seizing those power-mad men and pushing them into the pit of death, I might have begun melting like this: “Alas, poor man! He was one who bathed in rosewater; today he must bathe in red water! He was one who turned maidens into dolls and played with them in the bedroom; today, when death chews him up, poor man, will he not wilt under memories of the past?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 29 / printed 28 -->

He was one who lay touching and clinging to women’s laps, shoulders and thighs until one wondered whether he drank the wine or the wine drank him; will he not tremble at the very thought of the guillotine chopping him apart on the block?” If I were to melt away like that, the name Revolution would not suit me!

When it is said that the Russian Revolution began from the French Revolution—and when the revolutions they carried out show that the Russian people were capable of sacrifice even at the very moment victory seemed within reach—I cannot accept being shoved and buried everywhere I do not belong in the present age.

So I have come forward to change my own nature. As the first step, I have changed the place where I stand.

Where people were shot dead,

where they were cut down,

where, even if he knelt, if he was a king,

he would be punished,

I once roared amid the smell of blood. Now, after changing my place, I have decided to move away from the old condition of facing repression with weapons and to use another kind of weapon. Its name is the weapon of knowledge! The weapon of love! The weapon of peace!

The work proper to my old name, Revolution, was to drink down lives, stack bodies like empty bottles and plant a victory flag upon them—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 30 / printed 29 -->

now I have become

an intellectual revolution nurtured by learned greats—

a political revolution that removes domination—

a scientific revolution nurtured by discerning minds—

an agricultural revolution of farmers—

a social revolution that brings down divisions of caste and religion—

a revolution of the dignity of our people, driving away the spider-insects that today seek to ruin the people who planted their flag and ruled through the ages—

a revolution of knowledge that suppresses arrogance, the clamour of injustice and rowdyism even in the assembly hall—

a reform revolution in the spirit of Siddhar Sivavakkiyar, who asked:

“What is this mantra you mutter,  
calling a planted stone a god,  
placing four flowers upon it  
and circling around it?”—

a spiritual revolution in the spirit of Ramalingar, who sang that blind custom must be buried under the earth forever, and that we must not fall into the heap of scriptures—

a rationalist revolution in the spirit of Bharathi, who sang:

“Ignorant fools  
who wander seeking,  
saying there are  
a thousand gods!”—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 31 / printed 30 -->

a Self-Respect revolution in the spirit of Paavendhar, who wrote:

“Let us make way for love,  
and find a door  
that shuts the path to conception”—

a revolution of sacrifice that, in the righteous struggle through which a new society is to bloom and flourish by means of all these revolutions, dedicates itself and stands ready even for the sacrifice of giving its precious life—

I have taken shape as so many revolutions. Do not imagine that merely joining me to a name will make my character lend that name fragrance! Even a corpse, after a few days, gives off a kind of smell—if you take that smell to be the nature of Revolution, they will call you deranged!

Revolution is not killing and heaping up those who hold opposing views—those who enter the field so that their view may prevail—and then wearing the wreath of victory.

There was a time when that was what they called revolution!

Today, changing even those who hold a different view and turning them into allies of our own ranks—that is the great victorious revolution!

Otherwise only stain will come upon us—

what are you staring at;

<!-- Tamil source: scan 32 / printed 31 -->

wondering whether Revolution has suddenly become a “vegetarian eater” like this?

Enough! I was a “meat-eater”; I put Mahatma Gandhi himself into my mouth and swallowed him, and sang “Vande Mataram!” Enough of that!

The country needs an industrial revolution!

Industry needs a knowledge revolution!

Knowledge needs a rights revolution!

Rights need

resolve! resolve! a revolution of resolve!

I shall stand only on the side of those with resolve!

I shall live only in the hearts of those who guard the ideal to the very end!

If they are not like that, I shall climb down from their lap saying, “Hey, you are a bad fellow!”—

I, called “Revolution,” shall remain immortal—

as fragrance in a flower....

as patience even in a storm,

I shall cherish and nurture you!

Greetings. I am going.

Do you ask where I am going?

Nowhere in particular; wherever there is a dwelling place worthy of me,

that is where I shall go.

There I shall remain.

1-5-2005
