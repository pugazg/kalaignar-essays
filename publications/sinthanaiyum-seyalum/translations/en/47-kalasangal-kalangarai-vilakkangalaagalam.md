# Finials Can Become Lighthouses!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 204 / unit marker only -->

The poems I have written from my youth onward have been printed as a large book of 1,107 pages and published by Seethai Pathippagam, Chennai, under the title `Kavithai Mazhai`.

My dear friend Chinnakuthusi, who has written the introductory appreciation for that book, begins it by writing:

“‘One who can grow towards perfection
without having to be patted and encouraged is a first-rate poet,’
wrote Pramil, also known as Arup Dharumo Sivarāmu,
in the preface to a collection of poems!”

After reading that, I say a few words for today’s “Thought and Action” as one who has made this calculation about himself: **one who can grow towards perfection—not one who has already grown into perfection.**

In the many hundreds of letters I continuously write each day to `Udanpirappugal`, I habitually conclude simply with “M.K.”; in all the Tamil letters I write to others, I sign and send them as “M. Karunanidhi.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 205 / printed 204 -->

In the preface I wrote for the novel `Selva Chandra`, which I wrote in 1937–38 at the age of thirteen or fourteen and which has been preserved until today without being printed and published, I signed myself “T. M. Karunanidhi.” Even if, when I read that novel now, I myself feel embarrassed, a feeling of anguish still rises when I see with what throbbing of the heart, even at that tender age, I wrote in my own hand in that preface:

“Present-day Dravida Nadu is caught in the net called caste,
and, through certain superstitions,
is floundering after losing the art and civilisation
of the Dravidian people.”

If my Tamil writing skill and form of those days were like the lines left by crabs running across the damp sand of the seashore, and today they appear to have acquired at least some quality, does that not show that the effort and labour of these intervening eighty years have not gone to waste!

No one should smile inwardly thinking this is self-glorification. Even if they do not attain perfection in full, the young people and students who are the stars of future hope must understand their own strength, increase it, grow and win laurels, so that they too may travel at least some distance towards perfection, as I have. I possess, like a mountain rock, the firm conviction that today’s “Thought and Action” can be especially useful for that. As an example, I place before you one of my life-dear friends!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 206 / printed 205 -->

“Kannadasa! O beloved one
who sweetens every thought of mine!”

I began,

“You who gave the heart delight
with poems of young summer!
Why did you stop
the beating of your heart?”

I cried and wept,

“You are a child who stretches out his hand
and leaps towards everyone who reaches out to fondle you!
Did you leap in just that way
into the lap of the woman called the grave,
to drink the milk of peace and fall asleep?”

I sang in grief, yearning and suffering.

That life-dear friend Kannadasan planted his flag upon the summit of the mountain of poetic fame.

As evidence of his own strength, his own effort, and the victory he achieved by using both, here is an authenticated piece of information.

This information appeared on pages 20 and 74 of the weekly magazine `Kungumam` dated 16-10-2005. It is a letter of Kannadasan received from Mrs. Jayanthi Kannappan, wife of film producer A.L.S. Kannappan, who was the son of the poet’s elder brother A.L. Srinivasan.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 207 / printed 206 -->

It was a letter he wrote to his elder brother. While staying in a hostel and studying in the sixth standard under his birth name Muthiah, he wrote to his brother listing the things he needed for his own use in the hostel. A copy of that letter bearing his handwriting has been reproduced with the item.

(Copy of the letter written by Kannadasan)

What deserves our attention is that, when my friend was studying in the sixth standard, he wrote `thalagaani urai` instead of `thalaiyanai urai`—“pillow cover.”

When Kannadasan, still Muthiah in the sixth standard, mistakenly wrote `thalagaani urai` for `thalaiyanai urai`, did Mother Tamil curse him and cast him aside? No! Not at all!

Mother Tamil sprang forward, embraced him, blessed him and rejoiced! She raised him up, telling him to climb the steps of effort!

Accordingly Kannadasan used his own strength—he laboured without interruption to increase that strength—he made the multilingual scholar Appaduraiyar his Tamil teacher—he advanced through effort—he learned Tamil properly. When Kavi Ka. Mu. Sherif and poet Maruthakasi introduced him to me in the rehearsal hall of Modern Theatres at Salem, the stage manager who was with him, the unforgettable friend Sulaiman, pointed to Kannadasan and told me:

<!-- Tamil source: scan 208 / printed 207 -->

“Sir! He is the editor of the newspaper `Chanda Marutham` here. Just as they say, ‘If he rises, he is Kalamegam itself!’, if this man rises, he is a storm-wind itself!” Even today, remembering that praise makes my hair stand on end.

Later, at a public meeting in Pollachi, I crowned him with the title `Poet`. From then on, wherever Tamils lived across the earth, he began to reign as `Kaviyarasu`, King of Poets.

When the pattern of time changed and he stood in another camp and scolded me to his heart’s content, I still said, “For your sweet Tamil, I shall bear it, ayya!”

Film songs? Words themselves formed into supporting ranks and bowed in the direction where he stood!

Words falling like pieces of sugar candy folded their hands in worship before him and, at his command, took form as poems!

If we understand that the man who once wrote `thalagaani urai` instead of `thalaiyanai urai` came to shine at the very summit of the Tamil world chiefly because of his own strength and his own effort,

then, if today’s young people imprint that cause in their minds and labour, can they not become the finials atop towers? And if firmness of principle joins them as well, is it not certain that those finials can become lighthouses?

Therefore my appeal is this:

Let young people and students
shine like rising suns—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 209 / printed 208 -->

read history—

honour the heroes of history—

learn about warriors—

understand the wise—

know the scientists—

learn the innovations of science—

praise those who sacrificed—protect
our sacred land!

Undertake the journey of life
along the path of rationalism!

Read as books the lives of those
who rose through labour!

Accept as guides those
who advanced through effort.

27 - 10 - 2005
