# One Calculation for a Boat; Another for a Country?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 199 / unit marker only -->

The monthly magazine `Pothu Arivu Ulagam`, edited by Dr. Akilan Ramanathan, reached me with three or four months’ issues together, and I had the opportunity to read every page of them.

There are somewhat too many spelling errors here and there. If there is an error in letters, it is easy for us to put the words or sentences together and say, “Oh! This is how it must be.” But if, instead of an error in letters, an error occurs somewhere in the numbers, will it not create confusion about time and history itself?

If this shortcoming is removed in future issues, there is not even an atom’s room for doubt that the magazine `Pothu Arivu Ulagam` will become a treasure bearing new information—a treasury of ideas—a chest of history—a storehouse of facts—a mine of knowledge.

Now let us enter the information it contains. Under the small heading `An Overview of India’s Human Development`, there appears the following boxed information:

(When you hear “box,” do not turn around before and behind you with eager astonishment. “Boxed information”—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 200 / printed 199 -->

means information given inside lines drawn on all four sides. And when I say `porul`, please understand it here as `meaning`!)

Here, through these statistics, we can understand how far India’s human development gives us cause for alarm.

India’s population has crossed 104 crores and is **going on**. Notice especially that I say it is “going on,” not “standing still.”

Bharathiar’s time was not some remote antiquity; he lived among us during the freedom struggle that has now passed into history. In his poems the population of India appears as:

“A union of thirty crore people,
common ownership for the whole;
an unequalled society, a wonder to the world—
long live,
long live Bharatha society!”

*

“She has thirty crore faces, yet life
surges in her as one.”

If we go by these songs, a population of thirty crores was only about one-third of today’s population of one hundred crores. And that was before Pakistan was separated! When Burma too was joined with us! If even those are taken into account, India’s population must have been below thirty crores. We must think about how many times that number has multiplied and how it is now crossing one hundred crores.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 201 / printed 200 -->

Of these one hundred crores, the boxed information says:

those up to 14 years of age — 32.2 per cent;

those from 15 to 64 years — 63 per cent;

those above 65 years — 4.8 per cent.

Birth rate — 23.28 per 1,000

Death rate — 8.49 per 1,000

Average life expectancy — 63.7 years

Total literacy — 59.5 per cent

Engaged in agriculture — 60 per cent

Engaged in industry — 17 per cent

Engaged in services — 23 per cent

Unemployed — 8.8 per cent

The number unable even to earn enough for food has risen to 4 crores.

The total number of families across India is nineteen crores and twenty lakhs.

India spends 80 dollars on the health of each individual—but China spends 224 dollars!

The United Nations development institution that publishes an annual `Human Development Report` pointed out in its report for 2004 that India stood at 127th place in the calculation of the development index. According to that report, Norway stood first, Sweden second, the United States eighth and England twelfth.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 202 / printed 201 -->

Each country is compared and assessed through indicators showing, in particular, where it stands in human longevity, education and standard of living.

Nor can we forget or deny that population growth is one important reason India has moved from exporting food products to importing them.

Natural resources such as water, land and minerals already existed within a fixed boundary. When they cannot grow in competition with population growth, and the number of people alone increases three or four times within a certain span of years, future generations must be prepared to live lamenting that the country’s economic progress has become paralysed—that there is no growth, only decline.

Food cooked for three people can be stretched and served to five if two guests arrive. But if the number becomes ten, must not some go hungry if the others are to be served? Despite explanations like these having once been given in detail, it is a great deficiency that the family-planning campaign conducted by the government itself is no longer running at full speed.

“Let us make way for love,
and find a door to close the path to conception;
what wrong is there in this?”

When Paavendhar Bharathidasan, poet of the Self-Respect movement, asked this, it was not merely to say that there should be birth control within the home. It was also to insist that, if population control is to be achieved in the country, couples in every household must show concern for it.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 203 / printed 202 -->

Even in those days, Self-Respect movement conferences passed resolutions supporting contraception and insisting on family planning.

Even today, with social awareness and beyond party divisions, everyone concerned about the country’s growth must come forward to implement the family-welfare programme.

If a boat carries more people than the number it is calculated to carry, what happens to the boat? What happens to the passengers?

We must think and act on a rationalist basis: **the calculation that applies to the boat applies to the country as well.**

26 - 10 - 2005
