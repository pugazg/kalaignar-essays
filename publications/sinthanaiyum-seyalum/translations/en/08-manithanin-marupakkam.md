# The Other Side of Man

<!-- Tamil source: scan 46 / unit marker only -->

For every side there is another side. Every object and every living thing has another side. The earth has another side; the sky has another side; the planets have another side. Seas, rivers and canals all have another side. Even air, when measured by volume and dimension, has its other side.

And man? Does man too have another side?

I am not speaking merely of the other side of the body that geometry might describe. Nor am I speaking of the various “sides” of a human being that doctors examine in the course of medical investigation.

I am speaking of the other side of man.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 47 / printed 46 -->

Let us come straight to the point.

A man may be praised as an intellectual—as a learned person—as a peerless sculptor—as an eminent painter—as an unmatched writer—as an actor—as a musical genius—as a summit among poets—as the greatest among artists—as a spiritual sage—as an ascetic overflowing with love, free of desire and attachment—as a fearless revolutionary—as a philanthropist—as the very embodiment of truth—as a discerning wise man—as an adept—as a scientific genius—as one who walks the divine path—as a compassionate friend of the poor—as a social worker—as a leader—as a selfless worker—as a holy person—as a Buddha incarnate—as a jewel among devotees—as a flame of rationalism!

Whether man or woman, opponents or others may fail to discover that person’s “other side.” But can anyone hide it behind a screen from his or her own conscience? At times that very conscience tears the screen away, removes it altogether, and lets the other side become known in all eight directions.

Feelings arise from knowledge and character. Knowledge may be sound understanding—or petty understanding. Character may be good—or bad.

When sound understanding joins good character, there is very little “stain” on the other side. Even if there is some, it can be removed.

There can be bad character even where there is sound understanding. Worse still—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 47 -->

when petty understanding joins bad character, cruel people are born.

Thus the “other side” of man is shaped by the combinations of sound understanding, good character, petty understanding and bad character. We can judge it from a person’s actions—but only to the extent that those actions come into the open.

I know my own faults and weaknesses. Others may infer some of them from my actions. Yet there may also be things that remain hidden, deceiving those around me.

If sound understanding means full knowledge, and ability joins hands with bad character, the harm it can cause will be enormous.

Whether poison is mixed into milk or milk is poured into poison, the milk is spoiled. In the same way, when sound understanding is mixed with bad character—or bad character with sound understanding—the understanding itself is corrupted.

Without a mirror, however much we twist and turn, we cannot see the whole of our own other side.

Only if we hold before ourselves the mirror that Socrates called “Know yourself”—a mirror coated with the reflective substance called the “weapon of knowledge”—can we examine that other side and correct it.
