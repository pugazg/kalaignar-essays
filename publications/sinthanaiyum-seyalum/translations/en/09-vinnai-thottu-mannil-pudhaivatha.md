# Touching the Sky, Buried in the Earth?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 49 / unit marker only -->

The world has reformed; the country is moving toward civilisation. We desire still more progress.

We do not know whether the “future” allotted to us will end quickly or slowly. We shall not live long enough to see every stage of the progress that is yet to come. Even so, we long for human knowledge to climb to its summit, and we wish to see and enjoy the world shaped by that growth.

Scientific discoveries and inventions bring development and happiness. Natural disasters that lie beyond the reach of science bring decline and sorrow. To prevent them, protect against them and find remedies, the world of science labours without rest in its laboratories.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 50 / printed 49 -->

On one side, human society blooms like a garden of lofty thought. On another, there are pits and stagnant sewage pools splashed by streams of blood.

Pain of this kind exists throughout the world. And shameful events of this sort appear in the news of India too.

At first sight you may wonder what connection there is between the preface I have just given and the incident I am about to recount.

But when man struggles against nature and advances through science, culture and social reform, an event that occurred in Punjab—the land of Bhagat Singh—seems to bury culture itself and cast away the human heart.

A news report says:

“In a village in Ferozepur in Punjab, a young woman was preparing for marriage. Her mother had agreed to give her in marriage to a young man named Bali. After that, the mother fell ill and died.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 51 / printed 50 -->

After the mother’s death, the girl’s father, Chinna Singh, tempted by a large sum of money, arranged to marry her to another bridegroom.

But the young woman firmly declared that, in accordance with her mother’s wish, she would marry only Bali, the bridegroom her mother had chosen.

Enraged, members of her family not only shaved her head and tortured her; they stripped off her clothes, made her naked and paraded her through the streets of the village.”

A case was filed, arrests were made and the cruelty was condemned. Even so, what happened did happen!

However far we may have travelled and advanced from the age of barbarism, if such cruelty—especially cruelty against women—is still inflicted in a way that proves that we too are people living in that barbaric age, does not the preface placed at the beginning of this article fit after all? On one side is the growth of knowledge straining to touch the sky; on the other is the collapse of humaneness, buried in the earth.

16-5-2005
