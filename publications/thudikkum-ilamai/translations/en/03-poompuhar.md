# Poompuhar

<!-- Tamil source: scan 20 -->

The old situation in which people said that the Kamba Ramayanam alone was the river of poetic honey, and Kamban alone the emperor of poets, has now begun to melt away. Those who rejoiced in garlanding Kamban alone are coming forward to honour Ilango Adigal too. Those who had thought that only a Kamban conference could be a conference of poetry have broadened their outlook enough to hold a Silappathikaram conference.

No one can deny that we of the rationalist movement brought revolution in Dravida not only in politics, not only in society, but in the literary field as well. Our criticism of the Kamba Ramayanam broke the superstition that had ripened to the point of building a temple for Kamban and worshipping him as a god. When we criticised Kamban, the arrows hurled at us were not few; we were showered with harsh words. We were called people without artistic sensibility. We were struck with the burning insult, “Creatures without the taste of the ear, knowing only the taste of the mouth.” Yet victory came to us. Our aim was not to make the Kamba Ramayanam itself unavailable in the country. Our aim was that everyone in the country should know the wrong Kamban had done. The Kamba Ramayanam is a spider's web that portrays the Dravidians as demons and monkeys. That is why we said it must be rejected. That is the fundamental reason for our rejection. Kamban made the Aryan Rama into a god.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 21 -->

We condemned that villainy. We explained that for such a book to be an object of worship for the Dravidians was an act without self-respect. The devotees of Kamban said that, after all, one must allow for the ideas in the Kamba Ramayanam according to its age! We did not object to that. Our chief accusation is that Kamban betrayed the Dravidians by depicting an entire people in a degrading manner; that is what many scholars fought against. Even then we said: we are not enemies of literature. Though works such as the Silappathikaram contain many ideas of their time, they do not speak of the Dravidians with contempt; rather, they show the Dravidians in an elevated light. We explained that we do not reject them.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 22 -->

When Tamil treasures such as the Silappathikaram lie buried, we have asked: why do these fools hold festivals for the Kamba Ramayanam, which calls the Tamil a demon? We asked not because we had acquired devotion toward the Silappathikaram. Not because of any feeling of divinity. Nor because of the extravagant marvels of imagination found in it. We support the Silappathikaram because it is a work that shows how Tamils lived in the age of the Silappathikaram; because it reveals the greatness of Dravidian warriors who broke the crowns of northerners; because it speaks of the glory of Dravidian kings who lived for justice; because, even if we do not believe that the character Kannagi performed deeds beyond imagination, it gives us an opportunity to understand how the courage of women of that age was portrayed; and because it is a rare work through which we can learn details of the rule of the three—Chera, Chola and Pandya. There is no other reason.

Why do we praise the Tirukkural? Because Tiruvalluvar is called the “divine poet”? No; because the Kural is a fine picture of the Tamil land of that age and because it records Dravidian principles of justice, we celebrate the Kural. The Kural is not without faults. Those faults are outweighed by one fact alone: it was a book written in that age. In the times to come, even the revolutionary ideas written today may be called old.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 23 -->

In general, in works such as the Kural and the Silappathikaram, the greatness of the Dravidians shines; therefore we praise them. Kamban made the Dravidians into a degraded people; therefore we opposed him.

The fact that even those who had clasped Kamban to their chests have now come forward to hold a conference for the Silappathikaram shows that our words have power.

But one thing: do not make Ilango Adigal into another Kamban! At the conference gathered in Kaveripoompattinam, R. K. Shanmuganar has advised people to celebrate the Indra festival! A Silappathikaram conference must not exist to nurture such superstitions.

“Konkanars, Kalingars, the fierce Karunadars,
Bangars, Gangars, and warriors of many formations,
with northern Aryans, in the clash with flourishing Tamil—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 24 -->

at Kadamalai, in the hunt before my eyes;
at the fierce flood of the great river Ganga,
on the day our king bathed his daughter there,
before five hundred Aryan kings,
you alone stood in battle, your fierce eyes blazing—
Death itself beheld it with a terrible eye.”

Passages such as these should be explained and used to remind us of the ancient valour—and justice—of the Dravidians. Those who gathered at Poompuhar and held a conference are not the only people entitled to the Silappathikaram. It was we who made them think about holding a Silappathikaram conference. Though the Poompuhar conference gives us satisfaction, we fear that people like Shanmugam may nurture new superstitions such as the “Indra festival.”
