# Lamp of Victory!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 25 -->

They have held the cup of poison in their hands—held courage in their hearts—held principle on their tongues—and given up their lives. Even when they were thrown into fire, the body could be reduced to ash, but their lofty ideal could not be burnt away. Bullets have left guns and pierced their chests. Even then, no hole was made in their principles. They wandered. They were driven away with no one to support them. They endured showers of abuse. Before the people they were treated as madmen. They were treated as people unfit to be seen—as murderers—as villains. Taunts on one side—ridicule on the other—arrows of opposition were fired at them in turn. They faced all of it; they did not collapse.

Self-interest never circled their lives. Personal matters never smashed their principles to pieces. Family never became an obstruction in their—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 26 -->

path. They never desired pomp. They were beaten to death. But they did not die. Instead they gained a new body. They gained the body of fame. Fire leapt upon their bodies and charred the flesh. It melted the bones. But their principles became gold refined in the furnace.

Why did they suffer so much.... Why did they deliberately embrace hardship.... Was it to become kings? Or heads of monasteries? To sit smiling on jewelled swings, emerald couches and peacock thrones, and dance in pleasure? Had they thought so, would not a cross—or sacred ash—have been visible in their hands? What they held in their hands were books of knowledge! Had they desired status, would they not have conducted discourses on God—God's story—God's miracles? What they desired was this: to reduce religion to dust, destroy folly, and make human society into a garden of renewal. They knew that the revolutionary path on which they were setting foot was filled with danger. Knowing it, they set out courageously. Even if they themselves were destroyed—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 27 -->

they reached a state in which their principles could not be destroyed.

Not only in our country—throughout the world, thousands of years ago, there were conditions in which civilisation could not even sprout. The religious beliefs of Western lands were more degraded than ours. The gods there were more numerous than ours. The priests there were crueller than the priests who make us dance to their tune. Where are they all now? Reform sprouted. Superstitions that had lain grinning went into a corner and sat there veiled. Reformers died. Reform did not decay. It took up the sceptre. In Western countries, statues of gods have gone to museums. In our country, the Ganapati on the riverbank remains exactly where he was. Ignorance is watered and cultivated. The government itself takes the lead in doing it. Our government now lifts up the god of Somnath, smashed to fragments, installs him on a pedestal and rejoices. Is that all? A holy man wrapped in neem leaves arrives. A naked holy man wanders in Tillai. At the Ayyappan temple, the Lord has come in the incarnation of a snake—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 28 -->

Ramana has died, a comet has appeared, they lifted Panduranga and he said he would not come, the All-India wonder-astrologer has declared that there will be no election this year. Such news keeps coming. Almost all the newspapers have become almanacs. Here, an effort is underway to dig the grave of an already fallen society still deeper.

It is to break that effort, uproot aged superstitious doctrines, and find a new path that the rationalist movement is at work.

One part of that work is the Social Reform Conference to be held at Tindivanam on May 20. For us, who for many years have endured hardship and carried on the work of rationalism, the Tindivanam conference is a lamp of victory. It is a sign-lamp telling us that we are advancing along the path of our ideal and have reached a particular point. We are walking along a road filled with darkness. The lamp lit at Tindivanam shows us how far we have walked—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 29 -->

Already there is a great distance still to walk. But there is no darkness on that road now. Lamps have been lit in a row. It is that news of victory that Tindivanam is going to proclaim in sweet, delectable Tamil.
