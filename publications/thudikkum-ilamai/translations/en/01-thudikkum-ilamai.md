# Throbbing Youth

<!-- Tamil source: scan 5 -->

Chairman! Mothers! Gems among Dravidian students! Greetings. What I must speak about at this annual celebration is throbbing youth! Sometimes the throb crosses the boundary line. That is not my fault.

Throbbing youth! Yes, youth throbs. On a night when the lovely moon comes to caress, the lover throbs. The beloved has not come. Such are the restrictions at her home! Youth, which has come to resemble the moon-struck lover when it looks at the condition of other countries, throbs with the longing to live as a citizen of the Dravidian land. It is not longing alone that throbs! The youthful heart is a camp that, even while it feels anger at Aryan rule and compassion at the condition of the Dravidians, raises the warning: “Base fellow who brings disgrace upon your race! Die before the spear reaches you!” The icons of ahimsa may ask whether there is no strength to act peacefully.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 6 -->

They say Ashoka found peace only after conquering Kalinga. The Tsar and Rasputin, who had enthroned religious folly and lain embracing capitalist cruelty, did not fall because of a mild-mannered Lenin. It ended because of a revolutionary Lenin driven by fervour. Who can deny that the end of the workers' suffering in Russia was the result of such throbbing? Even the gods do not appear to have behaved with ahimsa. Let the lovers of ahimsa at least look at the pictures in which Siva holds a trident, Vishnu a discus, Murugan a spear and Rama a bow; and let us move on, insisting that a throb is indeed needed to regain lost rights.

What causes this throb to arise in Dravida? Politics that lived embracing injustice caused revolution in Russia. The Pope's false disguises drove Martin Luther into revolt. Cruelty, the crooked conduct of priests, and the rule of Louis XVI produced the bloody revolution in France. In today's Dravida, there is not one cause for student hearts to boil! Not two!! There are a thousand!!!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 7 -->

A blind man ought at least to have ears that hear. If even that is to be dismissed, he ought at least to possess the natural ability to speak. If not that either, at least his arms and legs should be sound. Even that is absent. The same condition has come to prevail in Dravidian life. Is there equality in life? When Valluvar, Vallalar, Buddha and the Siddhars have spread equality, and when equality itself appears to us necessary, essential and urgent, can we refrain from raging at those who ring the death-bell for the feeling of equality? In the alphabet primer, the Aryan is written of as “Aiyar,” while the Dravidian who toils day and night is written of as farmer, goldsmith, carpenter—the man with self-respect leaps up at such audacity! Can that be stopped? How many books have been made sacred scriptures for the Dravidians with the aim of spreading equality? The Manu code, which instructs that if a Sudra sits with a Brahmin, the Sudra's backside is to be cut away! The Ramayana—the compassionate history of Rama who, enraged that a Sudra named Shambuka had performed penance, cut off his head! The Nandanar story of the Periya Puranam, which establishes that a Paraiyan may enter the temple only after plunging into fire and acquiring a Brahmin form!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 8 -->

The Vedas—Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharvana and the like—to lay the foundations of Aryan doctrines! Are these the Dravidians' sacred texts? Is this their path? Dravidian, who once lived by “one clan, one god”! It is when I wonder whether you have been wiped out that the heart throbs. Is the sewer called Hinduism to lay down the law for you? Has any country ever seen a religion like this? Gods—thirty-three crores! Sages—forty-eight thousand! Mahatmas who preach that this world is illusion while wallowing in unending pleasure! With so many of them, we cannot see a single field in which this country is made to prosper.

Why are there twenty thousand divisions within Hinduism? If one man writes of Vishnu's divine play, another writes Siva's divine sport in competition. How fierce is the Saiva–Vaishnava rivalry? A Saiva tale says that when the poet Idaikkadan was exiled in the Pandya realm, Madurai Sokkanathar followed him! Would an agitated Vaishnavism merely stand and watch? A purana showed that when Tirumazhisai Alvar was expelled from the country under Pallava rule, Kanchi Varadaraja Perumal followed him! Is it a crime to expose the way ideas travel through such jungle scholarship? Or is that youth exceeding its bounds? Give me ten thousand rupees and ten months, and I can show you a donkey turned into a god.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 9 -->

The worshippers of the cremation-ground dweller may immediately come protesting: “Will you make a donkey into a god?” Roaring, “Can a donkey become a god?” the lords of the boar incarnation may run up with their dhotis hitched high. I will tell them one thing. The spectacle of an ordinary Sai Baba dancing in dollars! The marvel of a black stone quarried from rock appearing as Parandhaman! The wonder of the Hanuman monkey becoming an Alvar! The barbarity of a mere bird becoming Garudazhvar! The purana that depicts a dung-eating pig as Mahavishnu! Devotees who have seen and heard all this have no reason to be astonished at a donkey becoming a god. Has any god spread through this country without money, publicity power, open-mouthed dupes and deception? Let someone say, and we shall see, that this country has not sunk to the level where a donkey can become a god.

Is there education in the country? Or are there instruments for enlarging knowledge? Is there a path for research? Has any patriot reflected on the condition of a country in which pictures of a man and a woman must be put up even in latrines to tell people which is which?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 10 -->

Is what is taught to the people in the name of education nothing but the Kamba Ramayanam and the Kanda Puranam? Seeing all this, will the youth of this country not throb? When one sees U. V. Swaminatha Aiyar write, “When speaking and when writing, the Tamil language alone must be used,” does the heart not truly become molten fire? When a Raghava Iyengar can fashion Purananuru pieces such as *Boothapandiyan Deviyar* and *Veerathaymar*, while a Tamilian, Sethupillai, goes on writing *The Fall of Lanka* and *The Maiden of Lanka*, what student's mind would not become a volcano? What have the bands of poets who cry, “Do not crush the Kamba Ramayanam! Do not burn the Periya Puranam!” done until now for Tamil, Tamil art and the Tamil land? Is it enough to speak in flames that Cheran Senguttuvan placed a stone on the heads of Kanakavijayar? Do the poets not see the Tamilian of his lineage placing gifts upon the heads of Brahmins? When opponents remind us that the same Senguttuvan who defeated the Aryans in battle knelt when an Aryan named Matalavaraiyan performed a great sacrifice, does not our honour suffer? What use is it to be an enemy of the Aryan in politics,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 11 -->

but treating them as maternal uncles in religion? That is the question throbbing youth puts! Is there an answer, Dravidian who once ruled the world? Dravidian who carved two standards on the Himalaya, who thrust your armies into Burma and lived in victory overflowing with valour—why did you fall? Why does your hand that once bore the sword now bear the feet of deceivers? You who enriched life and wore the garland of victory—why were you captured by the words “Do not trust life”? Will tender young hearts not rage the moment the answer comes: the injustice done by Aryans and their servants? Youth throbs on seeing the homeland of the words “falsehood, a bag filled with air, a water-bubble, useful to none” rise in self-defence. Youth throbs on seeing North Indian commerce grow strong in Dravida, where once there was independent rule. Youth throbs on seeing patrons who cannot bring themselves to say “Here, eat a little” while people cry “Hunger! Hunger!” conduct a kumbabhishekam for a Kokila darshan. Youth throbs on seeing the Aryan–English embrace sever the original Tamils from political bonds. Youth throbs on hearing sophists place obstacles before sweet Dravida—an expanse equal to two Englands and twenty-seven Ulsters—living separately.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 12 -->

Youth throbs on seeing that Indian history has not drawn the history of the Three Crowned Kings who lived without equal.

Youth throbs on seeing the vile acts and racial attachment that oppose reform of Hinduism. This throbbing will not stop with annual celebrations like these. The echo of these annual celebrations will end only in celebrating the festival of a redeemed Dravida—the “May” Day on which folly has died away. You performers of poisonous doctrines! The time has come for throbbing youth to seize the sword. Fix in your hearts the warning that the pounding heart is an erupting volcano! With that warning, I call upon the people of this country to channel the throb of youth!

Come, Dravida! Greetings!!
