# Iyer Announces!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 12 -->

There stands that Iyer on Chennai's seashore. All who come and go look at him as they pass. He does wear the sacred thread. The thread is only hidden beneath his shirt—that is all. To those coming toward the beach, he says, "Countrymen; countrymen, a message." And he doesn't stop there. "There is a word called Dravidam; it is sweet-tasting honey, language sugar-candy," he sings. You will be astonished.

An Ayyar accepting Dravidam? You will be startled. True, friends, true! One day, my friends and I met that Ayyar on the beach. He sang the song above. He cried, 'Dravidam, Dravidam'. Even one member of the "Enothano" party—one who, at the very word Dravidam, leaps as though he has stepped on fire—heard that Ayyar's cry and went away, head bowed.

Go at once to Chennai's seashore. At the gate of Presidency College stands

<!-- Tamil source: scan 3 -->

that ayam. You will know whether what I said is true or not. Once you know—

then tell those Enothanos—those things that lay somewhere—those place-seekers—to go and see. And along with that, read carefully what follows below.

The words written beneath the statue of Dr. U. Ve. Saminathayyar at the gate of Presidency kalluri on Chennai's seashore:

"Maha Mahopadhyaya Dravida Vidya Bhushana Dr. Saminatha Ayyar"—those are the words!
