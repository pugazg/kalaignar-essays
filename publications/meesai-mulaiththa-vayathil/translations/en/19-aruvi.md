# Waterfall

<!-- Tamil source: scan 85 / printed 84 -->

An artist who, with his brush, creates the mass of blue cloud—the form of the splendid peacock—the beauty bestowed by the grove—the sandy spread of the desert!

A horse for an ornate chariot—a gem-crowned king—a dancing beauty—a sulking maiden—pictures displaying the colour of his hand!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 86 / printed 85 -->

The weaver of cloth—the maker of pots—the plaiter of baskets—the man with a groaning heart—the poor human being—all these are paintings that echo his thought!

Among rulers, assemblies of scholars and groups of artists, he had distinction everywhere. Delighting in the thought that no one could set another painter beside him, they would say: “There is only one sun; there is only one such painter.” Each year the king would honour him with gifts, framing them in gold and inscribing congratulations in diamonds; and he would divide the diamonds and gold among the poor who came in procession, as though asking whether the king wished to see again the gift he had given!

Had it been the rule of Muthamizhars, the grievances—the wants—of the poor would not have existed. But this was the time of some great Pallava king, one famed as a master of art!

The Tamil who piled up flawless paintings had one want!

When painting a cloud swooning as it embraced a mountain, he would imagine: if only a woman embraced his shoulder from behind and tasted him—if only that pleasure were his—and, whirled by longing, he painted a thousand forms of men yearning so!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 87 / printed 86 -->

When his stylus moved as though two doves drew close to each other on a tree branch, he lamented that there was no pair of lips drawing close to his own!

He had painted in colour the speed of a forest torrent! But if one were to paint the river of love whose speed shakes the mind—is there a poet for that?

One day a woman came to buy a work of art by putting a price on it!

He rejoiced as though it were a festival day! Amid their talk he cast a net of love! Once a leaf has been spread and food served, will a hungry man say no? They resolved to become companions. “I gave my mind to the painting,” said the beautiful woman. “This day I have received delight,” said he as well!

“I came to buy a painting; I have received a living painting... I shall return after nine days. Once I have obtained my parents' consent, there will be no barrier to lying embraced upon your shoulder,” she said. “Come, let us rejoice beside the waterfall,” she said. He agreed.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 88 / printed 87 -->

They sat savouring the great beauty of the waterfall falling like a white cloud-mass collapsing, while it offered a simile for hair the colour of a heron.

“Aththaan,” she said. “Why?” he asked. “Will you paint a picture of this servant-girl seated after bathing in the waterfall, all her clothes soaked?” she asked. Opening lips he had kissed, he said that picture itself would be the wedding gift! She left, after giving him delight through all that time...

He took up his brush to paint Inbavalli seated after bathing in the waterfall. Rejoicing that he had found in woman's form a beauty unseen even by Valluvar, who divided the sections and shaped the Kural, he began to paint!

He portrayed the beauty of her eyes so that they seemed to tell story after story! While painting the radiance of her lips he kissed the brush! Was it not a picture of her seated after bathing? There was only an upper cloth; so, after beautifully drawing the bodily fullness that revealed her youth, he swooned! As though it had bowed its head after losing a contest, he placed a golden pot in her hand! A perfume-box in the right hand!... When the work was done he stood worshipfully, looking in the direction from which his beloved would come!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 89 / printed 88 -->

Nine days having passed, she came. He ran and embraced her. “The picture of you seated after bathing is ready,” he said. She said her parents had agreed. “Is this not supreme bliss?” they said, swelling with joy. Taking leisure to see the painting, they entered the art hall. She marvelled at the picture: her golden body drawn without even an atom's difference from herself, with the golden pot set up in rivalry against it! She rejoiced! There itself the two became one in an embrace.

Suddenly she scattered a mischievous laugh. “You have lost, Aththaan—to this peahen!” she said. He was bewildered!

“For a woman who has risen from bathing, her hair wet, and is sitting on a rock—why is there a kumkum mark on her forehead?” she asked and laughed!

The painter slipped out of the embrace in which they stood... She followed... He was nowhere to be seen... She searched a long way... Then she returned to the art hall... She saw that the place where the art hall had stood was a forest of fire!... Unable to find the painter, she cried aloud; she ran to the waterfall and looked...

<!-- Tamil source: scan 90 / printed 89 -->

Seeing what looked like a human form floating in the waterfall, she jumped in... It was he—turned into a corpse! The good woman who had come to fix the wedding day joined him! While bathing, she had then asked the waterfall-water not to erase the maiden's kumkum. Had she known that the waterfall-water would erase two lives, she would not have asked!
