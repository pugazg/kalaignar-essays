# Annual Festival

<!-- Tamil source: scan 129 / printed 128 -->

**She:** “Aththaan, leaving this parrot all alone, you are going to hunt tigers! Is there not even a drop of mercy in your heart?”

**He:** “Living statue untouched by chisel! Lamp of light! I shall go to the forest and give work to all the arrows lying idle. Give me leave—I shall return quickly.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 130 / printed 129 -->

**She:** “The other day you called these eyes of mine arrows. Is it fitting, Aththaan, to put this arrow to sleep and travel to the forest to give work to those arrows?”

**He:** “Quarrelsome woman! Here, a kiss this very instant. Count it as my reward for this speech!”

**She:** “Do not go after making my whole body boil with that kiss and deafening both my ears with the loud, yearning beat of my heart! Do not touch me and cool me, only to leave without staying as a blanket for the body that will then shiver. No; no!”

**He:** “O moon! You say touching brings coolness; before this you said your body would boil like fire. Which am I to believe?”

**She:** “Even the moon that pours out coolness has heat, they say—and that fits perfectly with the third section proclaimed by the Kural!”

**He:** “When I took your hand, you lay mute for one day. Before a year has ended, you pour out fruit-sweet speech capable of conquering the whole world!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 131 / printed 130 -->

**She:** “There is still one way to make me mute, Aththaan...”

**He:** “What is it, my dear?”

**She:** “There is a world without speech. That world is not made by God; it is a new world made by man and woman together. Shall we go there?”

**He:** “Maiden who shows arrows with your eyes, bends the bow of your brows, shows nectar at the edge of your lips and calls me to walk along the shore of delight—if I kill two tigers and return, the reward the king gives will fill this whole house! Stay alone for one day. I shall return tomorrow!”

**She:** “I will not be alone. The sign of our love, growing from unripe fruit toward ripeness, is playing in my lower belly. I shall ache because I cannot see its beauty—yet with boundless joy I shall find delight even in the trouble the child gives!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 132 / printed 131 -->

“If you were beside me, we could both savour the dance your little one performs inside my belly!”

**He:** “There is no objection to savouring that, my gold! But if I kill two tigers, what great delight for the Chola who rules the earth! And for your kiss that rules me—a golden marriage pendant of tiger claws! The coming ‘new life’ will shine with jewellery made of tiger teeth!”

**She:** “Then go—but must you refuse even to remain with me through the time between nightfall and dawn, and rise with the rooster before you leave?”

**He:** “Why refuse? Is there anyone who would reject a meal mixed and served with lentils, ghee, syrup and honey? But there is one thing—only if I leave by night can I hide in the forest and catch the tiger!”

**She:** “All right, Aththaan, go! You set my heart aflame and now go to catch a ‘rat’—go, go, leave at once!”

**He:** “Beauty with a waist fit to be compared to lightning! I gave you my heart on seeing the way you walked carrying nature's pot upon that waist.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 133 / printed 132 -->

“Your father examined compatibility for our marriage. But did his astrology examine whether there was compatibility for your fruit-cheek to rest upon my mountain-shoulder? No! He said some dosham hung there and that there was no chance at all of ‘child-bearing’. We dragged out and rebuked the omen-reader who said it, and lit the family lamp—this very year, before it ends, we shall cherish and raise the banner of our lineage. In the meantime, give me leave to be apart for one more day!”

**She:** “I am not crying because we will be separated for one day. If you fall into the grip of a cruel tiger, I will stand having lost a precious treasure—I will have surrendered my very life. That is why I beg you: ‘Do not undertake this poison-test!’”

**He:** “It is the king's annual festival. On that day one may go not only to watch the spectacle but to win a reward as well, my love!”

**She:** “The king has an annual festival now—but on this day you are eager to kill a tiger and win a prize. I am going to give you a golden prize; wait four months!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 134 / printed 133 -->

**He:** “A little flower—a colourful peacock—a Tamil-speaking child—a lump of gold—”

**She:** “Holding in my hands red Tamil sweet as sugarcane, I shall stand before you with every thought made sweet and a lamp of laughter lit across my lips. Is the great prize you will receive by killing a tiger finer than that? Tell me!”

**He:** “My precious gold who shows nectar in your cheeks! Our shapely beauty and the budding beauty we have made are delight indeed; but is it not greater delight to hide in the forest and kill the tiger that destroys the beauty of the land?”

**She:** “You praise the row of my teeth as a pearl garland. Has the battle-line now become greater to you than that?”

**He:** “I did not say I do not want the pearl garland; nor am I an ascetic who despises your garland of kisses. If I had not attained you, the birth I took would not be a birth at all. Everything needs its time; one must understand the situation.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 135 / printed 134 -->

“The war-drum is sounding; this is the hour when the formation sets out for the wild forest to destroy the tiger. I shall see the pearl garland, then lend my ear to the drum. I shall complete the good deed that protects the land—and after the deed is done, I shall give you a pearl garland as a feast for thought!”

**She:** “My understanding is clear now, Aththaan. To destroy the wild beast that kills the people of the land—that is the duty of a Tamil son!... There, the drum sounds. Set out quickly!”

**He:** “Say that while showing the pearl garland of your mouth!”

●

*(A smile spreading across her face, she sends him on his way.)*
