# The Ladle Siddhar

<!-- Tamil source: scan 36 / printed 35 -->

“The sorghum gruel that the prison guard scoops up and pours for me, as I stand weary with a plate in my hand, laughs aloud. ‘Brothers who fought to defend shackled Dravidam and now stand here with a plate in hand! Why be dismayed at the sight of me, this gruel?’ it asks from inside the ladle! There is meaning even in the speech of this Ladle Siddhar. There is a new, invigorating idea in it too. How many cool truths this Siddhar's hot tongue pours out!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 37 / printed 36 -->

‘Brother! From the cool shade where you have been, come and experience a little of summer too! Only then can the hardship of the man who channels water into cracked fields, the toil of the cowherd boy who runs about to keep the herd grazing in order, the suffering of the hand-to-mouth traders who wrap gunny-sack cloth around their feet, tie it with banana fibre, and walk through the burning sands of the countryside with vegetable baskets on their heads, and the pain of those weathered human beings—those tender shoots—who roll from village to village begging, plucking a wholly indigenous tambura made by hollowing a bottle gourd and fitting bamboo into it, while singing in a rough voice, “I was born into a lowly life, my lord”—only then can all that touch your heart. Come out of cool shade into summer! Do not go from Courtallam to Kodaikanal! Taste a little of the summer blaze!

Is it enough for you to eat six-taste meals all your life? Is it enough merely to know about yourself? The world surrounding you is vast! A great crowd lives only on gruel; another mass of people withers without even that. When will you think about them?’

<!-- Tamil source: scan 38 / printed 37 -->

What—without listening to what I said, you keep staring at me? Is it because I am golden in colour? No—no—you are looking at the husks mixed in with me. The husks too are of my kind. They are my relatives. And what a fine-looking kind yours is, human boy? Your kind has husks too. They are not called husks. They are called capitalists—they are called lords—they are called avatar-men! They are called political geniuses!

‘Your kind too has husks. If you can remove them—then I too can remove the husks from myself! Try!’

So the Ladle Siddhar went on talking. The line of more than four hundred friends thinned away. At last there was I—with the plate in my hand! The remaining gruel leapt into my plate and asked, “Well, brother! Did you understand what I said?” “There is no salt,” I said. “In the body of your human kind too, some people have none of ‘that’,” he said.
