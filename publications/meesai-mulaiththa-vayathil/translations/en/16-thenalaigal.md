# Honey Waves

<!-- Tamil source: scan 63 / printed 62 -->

There, in a cool flower-grove flourishing and swaying, stood a rain-haired woman—a great beauty! She wore a fine cloth threaded with gold, with eyes that called the lover who would embrace her—and honey brimming in her cheeks! Her name was Killi. Teeth—jasmine! Lips—a bow of sugar!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 64 / printed 63 -->

He was a Tamil son, a young merchant. His eyes gathered clouds enough to make a peacock of youth dance. His shoulders rose like mountains. His name was Valavan!

He came to meet the bow-browed one—the proud treasure of chastity who guarded herself and the one she had taken as her own—with the speed of a hungry man rushing ahead of the feast!

Fruit trees would hang their heads at the sight of the beauty-tree she made standing there! From the rustle of the dry leaves around her, she knew the news: Valavan had come. Before she could say “Aththaan,” he had embraced her from behind! Delight in clusters—sweet words in bunches—two seas roaring with waves of love—a great feast! A long silence!... Hearing the music of dawn birds, they awoke!

“Your blue eyes have reddened into fire, my dear,” he said. “Of course! What life is there in them!” she replied, embracing him again.

“It will be twenty months before we can dance and sing like this again, woman—day after tomorrow I leave for the city of Rome, my dear!”

“O handsome one! Was I born only to suffer like a kattumaram on the sea-waves? No—I have resolved to stop this journey!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 65 / printed 64 -->

“No barrier—and no fire in your eyes! Cross the waters and sell shining pearls, and gold coins will heap up like a flood through an opened sluice. The world will know Tamil wealth. Give me leave, my doll—and with it, give me here that shop of your lips that serves a feast!”

“Do you think that, loading yourself with pearls and racing into the roaring sea, you are leaving a standing lamp here to die out... Aththaan?”

“I will not think so, straight-browed one... only because I would have to forget you before I could think it!”

“We have entered this joyous intimacy—if tomorrow you forget, it will be an earthquake in my life! Understand that!”

“One does not keep conscious thought on breathing in and breathing out. O breath of my life! Do not imagine all sorts of things! Without you, there is no me! Without the moon, no sky!”

“What if the stars invade?”

“I do not eat flesh!”

“Rome is said to be a honey-hive where beauties live!”

“There I shall be a cripple. I will sleep thinking of your guava lips and of the branch-honey that exists in Tamil land. In imagination I shall taste it.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 66 / printed 65 -->

“What if you think, ‘Why not taste some foreign fruit?’...”

“Am I a squirrel? I have no habit of tasting many fruits!”

“What if you decide there is no fault in friendship with courtesans...?”

“Even if that is Tamil custom today, I am an exception! Cast away your useless doubt.”

There was no more talk! Like camels setting out across a desert, each gave the other enough of the taste of pleasure for a twenty-month separation. Nature herself came and stood there, refusing them space to bundle up twenty months of pleasure. Then they parted, for dawn had fully broken!

○

A Tamil ship, carrying on its back the pearls of the southern Pandya sea, sails toward Rome. On its deck stood Valavan; with the fire of love blazing in his heart, he thought of Killi and wept like a little child. “Why this sorrow?” asked a honeyed voice.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 67 / printed 66 -->

He turned. She was lovelier even than Killi. Her cheeks were like the sun in the lower sky. Her dress made the very body stand out! The corner of her eye sent a message bidding love to come! Bow-like brows! Above them, a cluster of dots glittering like stars! To tie and finish that dark mass of hair must have taken two days and more than twenty people!

A beauty-spot on her face was like a bee resting on a lotus—an image of delight!

“Who are you?” he asked. “Roman,” she said. He asked her name. “Lecia! Granddaughter of the great merchant Marcus!” she supplied. He saw in her eyes the message that sang of love. He understood her heart.

“Your Tamil land, which gives peacocks, pepper and pearls, has also given me a handsome man to cure my yearning,” she said.

“Who is he?” he asked.

The bold woman leaned on his shoulder. “You,” she said.

“Sea water is salty, lady!” he mocked, drawing away.

“I do not need it to drink; I need it for the boat of my heart to float,” she replied.

“You are a sail-rigged boat!... Do not go wherever the wind blows... hold the helm of chastity steady,” he said.

“The wind is you... the boat is you... the mast is you... the beautiful turning-post and the helm are you...

<!-- Tamil source: scan 68 / printed 67 -->

and you are the sea on which this boat moves!...”

“The sea is water too!”

“There is that water in my eyes as well!”

“I noticed... Do not make me float in the water of your eyes—release me, little sister!”

“You call me sister...?”

“Why be startled? That is a noble custom of Thiruvidam, lady!”

“Is this the value you give the beauty God has granted?”

“Unlike Romans, Tamils do not become slaves to beauty; we marvel at beauty—we nurture it—we honour it—all without slipping even a little from the grammar of our civilisation!”

“Is my desire only an empty dream?”

“I am sorry. I have already given myself to another, Lecia!”

“Who is that fortunate cuckoo?”

“Her name is Killi!”

“Long may she live—will you place this pearl necklace on her as a token of my affection?”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 69 / printed 68 -->

“So be it!”

“I honour her. She is greatly blessed. Long live that good woman who has gained a handsome man of chastity!... O lord among Valavars!... As a remembrance of your affection, something for me...”

“Here... a gift that reveals the tenderness of the heart!... Wear this pearl ring... an ornament a brother gives a sister...”

○

Twenty months passed! A sight as though the moon itself had entered the grove! Killi stood there, ready to rejoice in the pleasure-field, caught in Aththaan's loving torment! Before receiving a kiss, why does her hand keep touching her cheek? Her gaze leaps toward every direction from which a sound comes! Her heart-veena aches: Where is the lord of the yaazh who comes to rescue me? Longing burns! Before the crop could scorch, like rain—before the unchiselled statue could melt away—the radiant Tamil man arrived. “Aththaan!” she cried. A war of lips began—without drum, without advance notice, without end!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 70 / printed 69 -->

Between them, pearls of words!

“How was the trade?” she asked. “Very prosperous,” he said. “Any lightning-cut beauties...?” she began. “None,” he replied, and took something out. “I brought a necklace; wear it.”

“What is this?”

“Workmanship of Rome... pearls of Tamil land!”

“Who gave it?”

“A woman gave it—for you!”

“She?”

“Like a sister to me!... a Roman woman!”

“Truly?”

“I swear upon you!... O radiant flowering bough, give me your neck here!”

“Aththaan... have you seen this pearl ring?” Killi asked. The bridegroom saw it and was astonished.

“You will ask who gave it: a Tamil—a noble Tamil—gave it!”

“Who was that Tamil?”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 71 / printed 70 -->

“Like a brother to me!”

“Truly?”

“Yes... he was Lecia's brother...”

“Lecia—you yourself!”

“Aththaan... I myself was Lecia!”

“Killi! Have you forgotten the old sea-going rule, ‘not with women’?”

“I have not forgotten... I alone am the exception!”

He laughed... placed the pearl necklace around her neck. She gave him a necklace of smiling pearls. Then all night long, the two of them strung necklaces of pearls!
