# Black Woman

<!-- Tamil source: scan 24 / printed 23 -->

Come, night, come! Come, beautiful black woman! You are beautiful—ravishingly beautiful. I like the black woman. I love her. But I am already married! So what; is it a crime to describe your beauty with an unstained heart? If I call you “Emerald maiden! Pitch-dark beloved!” rebuke me; fair enough! I have decided for myself that there is no fault in calling you friend.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 25 / printed 24 -->

O woman of the night! Come this way! Sit down! Have you sat? Good. I am glad! I like the black woman. Do you know why? Only when a black woman smiles do pearl-white teeth win first place. Is not a woman's beauty in her eyes? White eyes in a black body. Ah, what allure! What allure!

Because you too are black and you smile, the world praises the diamond light of your star-teeth. You smile; your mouth, woman, is like a shell split open with a thousand pearls packed inside! Even the fluttering beat of your heart reaches my ears. So still are you! Though you are an ornament of calm, your heart is not at peace. It is full of unrest. I feel its beating. You need a lover. Thieves love you. You hate them. You suffer. Drunkards wander in search of your lap. You groan. Your heart does not fail to tell me that either. In orchards heavy with fruit, on the banks of the Kaveri, hearing again and again the playful love-talk of lovers locked in embrace, your heart has grown heavy with longing. You smile for the outside world! But I can feel your heart weeping. You yearn, asking whether there is no handsome man fit for your love. I am a married man. I have left my queen of freedom outside and I burn

<!-- Tamil source: scan 26 / printed 25 -->

inside this cruel prison!

Poets, scholars and artists set foot in your swayamvara hall—and just as you come bearing the garland—they see your gentle smile, swoon, babble something that might be useful to the world, and collapse.

I have not failed to see the tears you shed lying pooled as dew-water on garden flowers and woodland blossoms. But what am I to do! You look about for some way to be consoled. You wait to see whether any dear female friends will come... They do come. But all of them are women who earn their living by staging ruinous dramas under cover of your good nature—women worn down by life.

Yes; your brother, the moon, gave you a white cloth, did he not? Who snatched it away? Nature-the-mendicant, of course! Ah!

He is indeed a “Maya Kannan.” He takes pleasure in stealing your sari. You cannot stand that thieving rascal at all.

Then how are you ever to be married? O black woman! O lady of the night! All my worry is about your marriage—is my face turning pale for you?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 27 / printed 26 -->

Is it because it has grown late? Because you cannot bear the sorrow?  
Because the fever of separation has risen? Wait, wait—there,  
my friend has come. A lover suited to you.  
Do you know him? He is that handsome fellow  
called Morning. Let us settle your marriage  
right now. They say the black woman and Morning  
are to be married; the name for it is karukkalaam.

Hey! Where are you running, friend of the night;  
ever-young lady! Wait, wait—is that much shyness  
the moment you see your lover? All right,  
all right, go!
