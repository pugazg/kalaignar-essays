# Hail the Dawn!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 34 / printed 33 -->

O dawn! Come! Take my greetings and go! Ah! The moment I say “greetings,” you come running like this—hey, seeker of praise! First the scolding—only then the blessing! Yes—you have woken the rooster—you have woken the red-rayed sun—and between the two, why, man, are you in such a hurry to knock at the worker's door? All night long he rolled upon the earthen floor in bodily pain; at last, exhausted, he has merely closed his eyes. Do you think he is sleeping!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 35 / printed 34 -->

Is it because contempt for the poor has got into you too that you pound so hard upon the hut-door of those lying there spent and curled up? Do your legs tremble to climb the steps of mansions—or is it your hand that fears to touch their doors?

How imperiously you bark, “Poor man! Take up the plough!” Have you ever, on any day, even through the window of a splendid mansion, proclaimed “It is late” in a voice as gentle as a vibrating veena?

The only day you do not wake the poor man—is the day he is dead! You do not wake a corpse! Nor do you wake the walking corpse in the mansion.

I understand the meaning packed into your way of behaving!

Hail, O dawn!
