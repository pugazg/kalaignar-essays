# Peacock Feather

<!-- Tamil source: scan 136 / printed 135 -->

The Pandya realm's royal hall pronounced its judgment: “The Saivite path has won; the Jains have lost.” Along with it, minister Kulachirai, through the kanpudai-pattu long-necked killing-stake array, had the eight thousand counted among great offenders mounted and done away with, and became a Nayanar. After this cruel act, the Pandya country lay afraid to open its mouth. The Jain ascetics who remained feared even to come outside.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 137 / printed 136 -->

Ordinary people who had embraced Jainism while also embracing household life had no path by which to open their minds. Kulachirai's tyranny placed a great barrier before the voice of rights. The regime issued its command that the subjects too must become whatever path the government took.

While Tamilakam stood in this condition, one day!...

He was a warrior at the face of battle; she was a maiden bearing weapons in her glance and the milk-white moon in her face! Where there is a man and a woman, the door to love cannot remain closed! Nalangilli—that was the name his parents had given the fine Tamil youth! The great beauty bore the name Peelivalai! They met... smiled and thought... and grew into a state where the question was who would be first to give the kiss. “I do not want a purse of gold like the belly of a Brahmin at a feast—I shall give you, without objection, the cheek that shows the beauty of the red evening sky! But Aththaan, though I come from the hereditary line of courtesans, I shall not become a monkey's hand-garland—this is certain,” said the picture-like red-Tamil woman Peelivalai, ending her speech with an “ich” of a kiss!

“O fortune who wets my lips with drops of honey! O moon circling the sky!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 138 / printed 137 -->

“You said ‘courtesan lineage’... you paint upon your cheek with tears... do not be troubled! Tender shoot who gives sweetness to me who lay in solitude! Dew resting on that shoot! Breath of Tamil!

“‘What relation are your mother and mine? What kin are your father and mine? How did you and I ever know one another? Like rainwater falling upon red earth are our loving hearts!’ We are such people. Do not fear! Even death cannot separate us!” So saying, he gave her the nectar of his lips—with a selfish purpose swelling in sacred intoxication!

They crossed the flower-grove, crossed the gang of impalement stakes that had thrown the Jains into the field of death, and came to the streets of the capital where hard-hearted Kulachirai wielded his sceptre—to see the beauty of art and to deepen the pleasure of love!

Waistless women showed a dance in their gait and rushed along the green-spread streets of the Pandya land like floodwater that had broken through every sluice!

A crowd moved in procession, soldiers surrounding them, praising the Pandya beneath the royal parasol!

A chariot bearing an image of Siva-Sakthi moved magnificently along Chithira Street!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 139 / printed 138 -->

She saw every part of the festival. “None of this gives the pleasure I had when you embraced me there,” she said. Delighted, he pursed his lips; then he asked forgiveness: “I forgot—this is a street.” Their intoxication clearing, the two staggered onward in the frenzy of love. Before them they saw an elderly Jain ascetic coming, holding a peacock feather.

Ants may die beneath one's foot; therefore the Jain elder held it to be the path of love to sweep them aside with a peacock feather and only then walk! A warrior who had come out to spread his doctrine with a boldness found nowhere else in the Pandya land! He had no teeth—but there was no weakness in his words. “The trembling seen in his body is not in his heart at all! What force there is in the words ‘Long live Jainism!’ He is an old kinnaara who escaped the eight thousand,” marvelled many who had come from other places to see the festival!

A warrior came growling. “Old odhiya tree, you have no right to walk here!” he said, standing before the elder.

“In Tamilakam, whose heart spreads like a peacock's tail, I have no right to walk? Where am I to go, son?” asked the great elder.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 140 / printed 139 -->

“Go somewhere; what is it to us?” he said. Without wanting an answer, he launched a rapid attack upon the elder. “Long live Jainism!” the old man gained a loud voice to say until death! The royal road became a flood of blood! The peacock feathers scattered... so did the elder's skull!

Nalangilli and Peelivalai came trembling to the old man's side.

With the utterance “Long live Jainism!” fixed upon his lips, the elder gave up his life upon Nalangilli's lap!

“O noble one! Living Tamil! O lamp of light who counted life as nothing!” Nalangilli's tongue moved to praise him; seeing four rough warriors before him, he shut his mouth! He picked up one of the scattered peacock feathers and began to walk; the swan-like maiden followed behind him.

From a distance he heard the great cry, “Down with Jainism!” She heard it too. “Why are you looking strange, Aththaan?” she asked.

“Nothing,” he said and asked leave to go—after the feast she forcibly gave him! “You did not savour and drink the feast,” she complained, showing the cheek that had not been wounded.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 141 / printed 140 -->

“See, the tilak is still undisturbed,” she said. “My mind is not steady. We shall meet tomorrow,” he replied and rose. Murmuring with desire, she clung to him and told him with her eyes to stay.

He stood without drinking the juice of love gathering on her fruit-lips, redder than the fruit of the cactus. “Kanna!” she said. His eyes were not upon her. Taking his hand, she leaned upon his shoulder. “Let it be tomorrow,” he said, placed the peacock feather in her hand, and ran away.

The woman sat motionless, mind intoxicated, gazing upon the peacock feather!

Thousands upon thousands of beautiful strands! Yet the form of that blue eye was beauty itself! More beautiful than the rainbow! More beautiful than a diamond crescent! In that beautiful peacock-eye she saw Nalangilli! She saw her own face there too, resting upon his lips! In imagination she painted and rejoiced in the grammar of two lives and one body upon the forehead of that peacock feather!

He, meanwhile, had been shaken in heart by the event on the royal street. There was no path for rights

<!-- Tamil source: scan 142 / printed 141 -->

in the Pandya land! To paint a picture of the thought spoken by one's heart was a great crime! What cruelty! The hunchback Pandya had gone blind! Kulachirai, however, had gained two more eyes! A rain of fire upon the soil of the Pandya country! Muthamizhvar—Muthamizhars—until the bones of their backs ran, until their brains were shattered—is beating them the justice of this band of devotees who claim to open the sluice for love? Great cruelty! Great cruelty!! If these people grow, the country will never dawn! For youths like me, love is not the first work—the first service is to begin the struggle for rights in the Pandya land! They say Jains may not even walk upon the road! Against that ban the Jain people must gather today! There must be one man to lead them! Who is that courageous man? Who is that lion of leadership?

Nalangilli asked the question looking toward the sky! Looking toward the soil of the Pandya land!!

Peelivalai, meanwhile, thinking that today she could consume a great feast, checked her great beauty against her clothes, crossed Aavani Street, crossed the quarter where priests who speak deceptive blessings lived, and waited by the grove seeking food for her aiyappasi! Evening passed; he did not come. The shapely beauty waited in the hall near Malarppathi

<!-- Tamil source: scan 143 / printed 142 -->

and fell asleep. Morning came. They feasted on love. Only afterward did she ask why he had taken so long!

“Confusion of mind, my dear—let the mind rest! Look: you and I are there in the peacock feather, a golden plate inlaid with blue and set with green!”

“Why, Aththaan? You say women are called vines because they climb upon a supporting branch. What is the explanation for calling women a peacock-tail?”

“Among peacocks, the female has no tail! The tail is the ornament that adds beauty to the male peacock! And because the tail gives distinction not only to the peacock but also to human beings, they called woman a peacock-tail.”

“You are a poet of very great learning!”

“You are poetry, my dear!”

“You are Tamil! You must embrace me!”

In the middle of that conversation she woke! An empty dream. The warrior had never come. Day had dawned! She walked swiftly, crying.

On her way was Chithira Street! There walked a young man—without sculpted finery, without any jewels or ornaments. In his hand alone was a peacock tail!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 144 / printed 143 -->

“Long live Jainism!” was the cry on his tongue! Who was he? A warrior violating the law that Jains must not come outside? Was he going to take the path the old man had taken? Alas! If the palace guards saw him, destruction would be his end! Peelivalai trembled at the sight of him. The ascetic came nearer! Darkness covered her eyes; she spun and fell as though a curved dagger were whirling! To keep carrying the banner of the path held by the toothless Jain, to oppose and destroy all the deeds of the sakkais—the great warrior who had risen was Nalangilli! He passed by the maiden intoxicated by pure love! He had thrown away the brush that paints pictures of pleasure!

She ran and blocked his path. “Do not take my life away!”

“So long as there is a ruling crowd that takes away rights, the struggle will continue! There will be no change in this. If you have the heart, wait; if we have life, we shall become one!”

Nalangilli spoke, then went on proclaiming, “Long live Jainism!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 145 / printed 144 -->

From the peacock tail moving through the crush, one feather fell! The beautiful princess Peelivalai picked it up! Her two shining eyes dimmed into pools, and in the peacock feather she saw her form and his form. She saw them as one! For a moment she even forgot love itself, seeing the courage with which Nalangilli, one man alone, strode out to oppose tyranny!
