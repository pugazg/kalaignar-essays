# The Stage

<!-- Tamil source: scan 55 / printed 54 -->

The warrior who declares,  
“My sword has no work entering its sheath”—  
and in his lap, a beauty-brimming maiden  
who makes one ask, “Is she a flower-laden bough?”  
Honeyed music! Southern mode! A salute to the breeze!

O green creeper dancing in the wind!  
O tender vine dancing in the spring of imagination!  
Unfading bud! New peacock of the flower-filled grove!  
With anklets jingling—slender waist trembling—  
with pure-Tamil melody and enchanting eyes  
embracing, embracing,  
the resolve of those who watch  
slipping, slipping—  
ah! What shall I say of that dance!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 56 / printed 55 -->

Crowned kings—honey-tongued poets—  
queen of dance—singing enchantress—poor man—fool—  
splendid hall—workshop alive with labour—  
those who wander for food—seekers after heaven—  
a hymn of freedom—a song of giving—Pari's  
company—a rich man's garden—many, many things  
have I seen! O stage!

Peerless art that shows all this! O stage!  
He said it well: you and the world are one!  
I agree! Yet—one thing!

You display equality between man and woman through costume and ornament:  
a man as woman!  
a woman as man!  
woman within man!  
man within woman!  
With a mere change of dress you can drive away that distinction!  
But look how the world heaves such a great sigh, unable to drive that distinction away through a change of heart!  
So you and the world are not one.

You can even show the lowly as the exalted!

This world, standing helpless at the crossroads without gaining that power—and you—are they one? Who said so?
