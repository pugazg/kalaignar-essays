# O Sea!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 28 / printed 27 -->

O sea that swallows the sun and spits out the moon! Blue garment that lends beauty to the maiden Earth! You are the gauzy cloth fluttering and rustling over the sandal-hued body of the lady of the world! Milk trickling from the coral lips of a green infant! The foam you spit upon the shore! The two are one! The way you scoop up and scatter lovely conches with your swaying, beautiful wave-hands recalls the delightful mischief children make with their little sugarcane-red hands.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 29 / printed 28 -->

I would come to you for rest. With your thunder of sound you would lull my heart! O sleepless great sea! There has been a slight lapse in our pure meeting. Even so, O sea, write a reply to my letter.

“Worker who labours—the capitalist who rises—the scholar who speaks truth—the poet who gives lofty song—the sculptor—the painter—the thinker—everyone gets rest. Why is there no rest at all for you? Are you telling the lazy man whose body never bends, ‘Look, I work without rest’?” I used to ask you that, did I not? Do you think that is what I am going to ask? No; no.

With rock-candy hands she stirs and serves gruel, then gives a kiss sweeter than that and sends her lover off at dawn upon his kattumaram; when the sun itself has gone and still he has not returned, her eyes weary, her legs weary, she stands at the doorway of the hut, waiting, yearning—the young bird of a family that lives trusting in you. Do you carry her husband back each day upon your blue-jewelled shoulders so that she does not faint away, so that she does not collapse and perish? I have asked you that too—do you think that is what I am going to ask now? No—no.

Trusting that Mother Sea will protect them, the palm-leaf huts that have sprouted along the shore—you lift them up and

<!-- Tamil source: scan 30 / printed 29 -->

fling them away; I have asked, many times, whether even you harbour a treacherous mind when it comes to workers. That is not what I am going to ask now!

O great pearl-pond!

One day in prison, amid the wandering of my thoughts, an outrage of yours raised its head and vanished again: they say you stretched out your wave-arm and destroyed Kaveripoompattinam—the beautiful city, the Tamil people's garden of wealth! I am not asking why you committed that injustice. Nor am I going to file a case over it.

But, O sea! You destroyed the prosperity of our glorious city of wealth! Just so, will your arm not leap at Kallakudi to destroy that wicked symbol of the North?

Write a reply to my letter!  
If not, remain silent!  
Both are things you cannot do!
