# Henna

<!-- Tamil source: scan 78 / printed 77 -->

A young woman at the age when beauty drips without a single jewel planted upon her. A form that bends and sways like a smoke-like creeper. Her waist? A young elephant-calf too slender even for one grasp! On her lips, a full measure of honey!... The famed Mullai, said to possess no lack. When she speaks—a little parrot!... Would the torment of love leave her alone!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 79 / printed 78 -->

There was a man born to feast upon beauty... his name was Maruthan. A youth who came like the graceful moon that makes the water-lily smile. “The memory of your spear-bearing eyes torments me; the gleam of your sword-bearing pearl teeth keeps flashing before me; when the elephant mounts, its forehead itself gives me such trouble!” So he would fondle his Anjukam and make her lose her senses upon the soft bed—that wicked bull! A great warrior of the battlefield! Commander of the army!

One day he left to destroy enemy territory, carrying the king's command—and the command of his queen of love. Ten days passed; he had not returned. As the pallor of longing spread and the maiden wasted away, her circle of friends watched for the day of ripeness and teased her in song. “This is the moment—come and end the assault!” she would run, only to see a shadow, be deceived, and stand yearning.

To steady a heart crushed like sugarcane in the mill, only a palm-leaf letter written by the beloved who had garlanded her reached her hand!... It said:

“O cool shade! O dew-flower! I return in victory. Before I go to see the victorious king, I am coming to pluck the kovvai fruit.”

Every letter she saw was drenched after receiving kisses!... If the palm-leaf pressed against her noble heart had not possessed a mute nature, it could have told a thousand stories and shown a thousand similes!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 80 / printed 79 -->

Her friends came. “Do not sit brooding inside the hut like a hen over eggs; in the morning come to the river and celebrate Aadi Perukku,” they said. “He will come in the morning! Aadi Perukku will swell our love as well,” Mullai said. “You mad girl! A man can be there any time; Aadi Perukku comes only once a year,” they replied, and began pouring out, with melody, a poem praising Kaveri. An old woman named Pavalam drew out her voice and loudly delivered advice that they must go to Aadi Perukku without fail!... Mullai too said she would come.... All of them flew to the next field to pluck henna leaves.

She piled up the tender henna shoots they had plucked, ground them, and with her mother's help applied the paste to her beautiful hands and to the tips of her exquisite ivory feet. Henna in the palm like the rising sun! On fingers that pluck the veena... on nails that leave their mark... henna! Henna upon her feet like Kannagi's anklet! Having put henna everywhere, she lay down with an unnamed blush stored in cheeks that stood red even without henna.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 81 / printed 80 -->

“If the wet henna dries through the night and falls away in the morning...

—we shall welcome with new beauty the lover returning from war...” Thinking so, she rejoiced.

Without sleeping, without turning even to one side, lying still so the henna would not be spoiled, she heard Maruthan's voice calling. She rose; he stood before her. She did not move. “I ran here thinking you would leap upon me and embrace me, seal my mouth with your lips before I could even tell you the news from the battlefront—why do you hesitate, Mullai?” asked the man whose mind spun in the intoxication of love.

“You said you would come in the morning, so I put on henna—the palm-leaf gave no detail... If I run and embrace you, the henna will be spoiled; from tomorrow morning all my friends throughout the village will beat the drum about us. That is why I hesitate. Without embracing, let us purse our lips together right now,” she said.

Would he object? He kissed her and lay down alone in the courtyard. Henna unspoiled—a young heart in turmoil—in that state, without sleeping or rolling over, wondering when the morning star would come, the bright-eyed Mullai lay awake looking at the sky.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 82 / printed 81 -->

“What if one night has gone? There is tomorrow—  
that bull is beside me—henceforth  
every day holds delight from which honey may be scooped”—  
so she spoke a word of comfort to her weeping heart!

Dawn came. The heroic commander took leave of his beloved and set out for the king's capital to see the lord beneath the white-disc umbrella and report the news of victory, promising to return before the silver moon rose!

The henna had dried. She brushed it away and looked again and again at her beautiful hands and feet. Red lines—anklet lines—picture lines—with these, her ten-and-a-half-touch gold body shedding radiance, the good maiden set out toward the river with her friends!... They held the festival—they danced! They sang!... Mullai yearned for the broad daylight to contract into night!

The world darkened—her heart gained light!... “Will you not come, silver moon?” she asked; it came too... “Where is he? When he comes there is no barrier to embracing and rejoicing; there are no henna leaves now, no wetness... only the beauty they gave remains in my hands!... We shall spend the whole night at a feast of delight...”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 83 / printed 82 -->

Mullai sang toward the moon... She saw a horse racing toward the gateway. “It is he!” she cried. In place of the man she longed for, a messenger arrived carrying a palm-leaf from him. It said:

“O Tamil! O cool grove!... An invasion in Kadaram! I cross the sea to go. Do not waste away and grow thin... I shall return in a year!”

She drenched those written words with tears!... She fell face-down upon the bed... “I wasted last night... wanting beauty for fingers and feet, I refused the feast—now for whom am I to display beauty for a whole year? What need have I of this henna?” She crushed and rubbed at her hands and feet!...

If only last night she had spoiled the wet henna like this, it would have been spoiled... love-play would have happened. Would it come off today?... It would not! “One year!” she cried, pouring a rain of tears!... Then she heard a “kalakala” laugh and sprang up with a rush—Maruthan stood before her. Asking why he had made her cry, she embraced him! To the maiden who wailed, “Are you really going to Kadaram?” the warrior-clan man laughed: “Which is greater—Maruthan or maruthani?... I need the answer.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 84 / printed 83 -->

“Forgive me; maruthani is a coloured line that fades—you are the picture for me that never fades!”

So saying, she sobbed—and surrendering his heart to the beauty of that sobbing form, he planted kisses beyond count; the fragrance of Mullai filled the whole hut! Love lived!
