# Winnowing Tray

<!-- Tamil source: scan 91 / printed 90 -->

Vezhan, returning home from the hunt, saw the maiden who showed the whole length of the street fail to run and embrace him. Angered, he went into the kitchen.

“I was breaking tamarind, so I did not embrace you. Do not smoke with anger, Aththaan!” she said, killing him with a smile and giving him a fresh kiss.

The flame went out in that singular sweetness; looking upon the taste of the fruit, Vezhan began to speak.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 92 / printed 91 -->

“What is this breaking of tamarind? Is it the skill of taking a chisel and splitting a mountain? Or the valour of forgetting life amid riddles, in the middle of a cave, and killing a tiger?” he asked. The maiden replied: “Who said killing a tiger is valour, Aththaan? Do you know that a tiger which men like you, gripped by fear, subdue with bows and spears was once driven away by a woman like me—with a winnowing tray?”

“The literatures famed as Puram and Akam belong to the Tamil. It is true that a Tamil woman once drove away a fierce tiger with a winnowing tray as her weapon—but that was then, woman! Today you cannot do it, my dear!”

“My Aththaan who embraces the bull! I shall wipe away the degrading saying that women are only for cooking rice! Without spear, without sharpened sword, I shall drive a tiger away with this old winnowing tray!”

“When I say tiger, I do not mean tamarind fruit, maiden!”

“I know. A life weaker than this fruit!”

“Eyes that spit fire! A leap that shakes the heart!”

“Do not try to frighten me—this maiden, this Tamil woman!”

“We shall see that one day. For now, let us join two lips into one,” he said.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 93 / printed 92 -->

“Is there any objection to eating a lump of jaggery? Once the tamarind curry boiling in the pot is taken down—let it happen!” she said.

“Tamarind curry? Tiger curry?” he asked.

“Does a tiger have curry? Is this the beauty of the tiger-hunt you went on?” the maiden laughed. Vezhan walked outside...

The maiden took down the tamarind curry that had boiled on the stove and was ready to play by planting her lips on Aththaan's cheek when there came to her ear a cry of misery: “Ayyo, tiger!”

The time had come to show skill, to show a warrior's nature—she seized the winnowing tray! Let Tamilakam create a literature for her, she thought; tightening the cloth at her waist and across her body, she ran toward the direction from which the sound had come!

As the maiden strode with a warrior's gait, a tiger lying atop a haystack prepared to leap at her. “Who is here? Where is my Aththaan?” she asked, raising the winnowing tray and standing before the tiger!

The tiger rose to spring upon the maiden, crouched, opened its frightening mouth and growled! From somewhere a voice wailed:

<!-- Tamil source: scan 94 / printed 93 -->

“My precious maiden! The tiger has swallowed me!”

“What are you saying, Aththaan? Swallowed you? Then where are you crying from?” The beautiful woman stood with the winnowing tray raised and shouted with tears in her eyes.

“I am inside the tiger's belly—half alive, a quarter alive, joining destruction. Ayyo! The tiger that ate me cannot even walk now; my tongue no longer has strength even to call you. So I am dying—one last kiss!”

“Ruined tiger! You have killed my loving Aththaan who was like my very life! You lie without moving, like a black cobra after swallowing its prey... I shall avenge him and then go with my husband. I shall set fire to you together with this haystack!” Enraged, she brought a burning torch. She set the haystack ablaze. That very instant her husband Vezhan leapt down from the stack and ran toward her. Seeing the tiger skin in his hand, the maiden lowered her head.

“Did you see the tiger you came to beat with the old winnowing tray?” he asked, spreading out the tiger skin.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 95 / printed 94 -->

“I understood your mockery—that is why I sought the help of fire,” said the maiden.

After both had laughed, they planned to go to the lovely grove and set off, intending to spend the whole night there with every atom of thought thrilled. Just as they started, a cat crossed their path. “The omen is not right,” the maiden said, standing afraid.

“O Tamil woman who took up a winnowing tray to beat a tiger!... Your heart trembles because a cat crossed your path!... O sacred lamp of valour! Do not be troubled; come,” he said.

“Does not scripture defeat valour?” she asked.

“There is something that defeats scripture too; its name is reason,” he said.

“How does one obtain it?” she asked, kissing him.

“You came carrying that winnowing tray to chase a tiger. If, with that same tray, you winnow away the foolish husks, it will become clear that all these scriptures and omens that frighten us are mere husks—mere tiger skins!” he said.
