# Friend

<!-- Tamil source: scan 72 / printed 71 -->

Kayarkanni—Nappinnai! Two peacocks in the grove of Tamilakam where Sangam Tamil resounds! Cuckoos of music! The elder one had a laugh as though pearls had been gathered and strung—a face that displayed the full moon! Seeing those coral lips spilling delight, the town would wonder aloud who in this world would gain the chance to eat and rejoice in all the sweetness they ladled out!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 73 / printed 72 -->

The other was rock candy—the younger friend! A body of gold... and in character, a little crooked!

In the stream—in the stream where the cool breeze floated—in the fragrance of the beautiful grove beside the waterfall—the two would dance and sing, flying like a pair of birds! Those who saw them would forget themselves!

The beauty of Greece and the noblewoman of Yavana lands, seeing these two on the shores of Tamilakam, forgot even to draw another breath—bowed their heads—lost their composure!

When foreign merchant-lords came to buy the sandalwood of the South, to load mountain-snake, peacock and pepper, and to stack elephant ivory in their ships, if they happened to see Kayarkanni and Nappinnai they would ask, “Let us exchange Greece and Yavana-land themselves—give us these little parrots!” So the old women would marvel at the fame of their beauty.

Who were the men, from what towns, bearing what names, whose heart-cages would receive these coral-mouthed red birds studded with diamonds? The villagers would talk, wear question marks upon their faces, and disperse!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 74 / printed 73 -->

“Kayarkanni is Nappinnai's friend, is she not? And Nappinnai is our own dear younger sister! If I try through her, who but I shall possess that sweet red honey?” Thinking so, Manivelan set to work! Elder brother of that ruby sculpture—a great warrior whose very occupation was killing deer, tiger and elephant!

Whenever a good chance arose, Nappinnai had exhausted herself measuring out to Kayarkanni's ears all the beauty, skill and strength of her brother!

One day she told her eager brother: “Kayarkanni is not a plume that wilts at a touch! I would call the work of expecting love from her a rabbit's horn! Still, go kill a tiger and bring back its claw. I shall try to turn her mind by saying, ‘Give yourself to the son of a warrior clan who ties a tiger-claw marriage pendant.’ Even that is no certainty, Anna!” And she sent him into the forest.

One moonlit season, Nappinnai came searching for her friend, whom she had not seen until the silver of the stars appeared. She found Kayarkanni walking alone on the white sand of the shore, her eyes all red and pale. No explanation was needed; Nappinnai understood everything!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 75 / printed 74 -->

“My brother went to bring a tiger claw for you—he still has not returned,” Nappinnai said.

“He need not return; even if he does, he need not give it to me—I have already chosen a companion for life.”

As she spoke, Kayarkanni lifted the pearl necklace dancing at her neck and kissed it.

“So he gave you a pearl and got a kiss in return? Fool, you have been cheated! You have abandoned the custom of choosing in marriage the man who proves Tamil valour and presents a tiger claw as a new gift—you are no Maratti!” she scolded.

Letting a smile play upon her lips, Kayarkanni answered in words that shed gold:

“O friend! Do not rush! Olimuthu is a great warrior—a noble man! He captured my heart. I am a Maratti—and so I asked the great warrior for a tiger claw as my gift! He said he would give me a new kind of gift—he brought up a pearl and gave it to me—and took me too into his embrace!”

“There is a great difference, friend, between Olimuthu buying a pearl for you in a shop and hunting a tiger in the forest!”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 76 / printed 75 -->

“Not in a shop! Before my own eyes he plunged into the middle of the sea... then rose again with pearl-oysters!”

“Look, I too found my life-companion last night!... A tiger of a tiger-hunter... a good man named Nambi gave me a claw as a gift. I became his!”

“Must a maiden surrender her heart merely to a gift, praise, and valour?... Would tradition be stained if the bridegroom came with character—with beauty—with prowess? You say he brought up a pearl from the shop; who saw your husband kill a tiger in the forest?”

“Do not say there is no nambikkai! His name is Nambi!”

“A bee for this flower! Hence all this praise!”

“No need to stretch the words—I bless you—I am leaving—my warrior will be waiting!”

Nappinnai's good face twisted; she went away from there.

“Mad girl! Her brother may kill two tigers at once—so what?... Ask at the courtesan's house and you will learn his character!...” Kayarkanni muttered as she left.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 77 / printed 76 -->

Nappinnai came home angry that Kayarkanni had not become her brother's, and found Manivelan sitting broken-hearted. She stood asking forgiveness.

“What now? Let it go; I have become the parrot that waited for the ilavu fruit,” Manivelan said, rising to leave.

“What, Anna—I have found myself a companion!... Look, here is the tiger claw he killed and brought!” she said eagerly, handing it to him.

“Who is he?” he asked with interest.

“Nambi!” she said.

“Him?... He killed the tiger?... Why, it was the claw of the tiger I killed for Kayarkanni that I gave Nambi—after I heard the news of her heart.”

So saying, Manivelan laughed!

The friend who had thought she had married a warrior fell to the ground unconscious!...

Afterwards she heard the news that this Nambi was an Aryan; she offered her life to God! She became a Maratti!
