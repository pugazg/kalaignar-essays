## Article 1 calibration notes

Article 1 establishes the collection's initial English voice baseline:

- compact literary compounds may remain compact in English when intelligible (`sky-sea`);
- direct apostrophe is retained with `O ...!` where the Tamil vocative drives the rhetoric;
- personification is not converted into explanatory prose;
- repeated `I too am a prisoner! / You too are a prisoner!` is preserved exactly as repeated rhetorical structure;
- sarcasm may be rendered idiomatically when the Tamil function is clear (`Some life you have!`) without adding a new claim;
- freedom/prison imagery remains concrete rather than academicized.

## Lexicon discipline

Record especially:

- recurring metaphors and personifications;
- direct-address forms;
- ideologically, socially or culturally loaded vocabulary;
- difficult compounds;
- epithets and labels whose force could be softened in English;
- source-specific puns, coined labels or unexplained references;
- publication/source labels;
- title renderings when they establish a reusable pattern;
- intentional contextual variants.

For every entry:

1. determine the function in the immediate Tamil sentence;
2. preserve rhetorical force rather than choosing a smoother neutral synonym;
3. distinguish a source-bearing transliteration from an explanatory note;
4. allow contextual variation when the Tamil meaning genuinely changes;
5. do not invent a stronger social, caste, religious, political or historical identification than the source supports.

## Quotations / verse / title policy

- A published or web English translation of quoted material is not imported unless explicitly authorized.
- Verse or verse-like source lineation is translated semantically/rhetorically; rhyme is never invented merely for polish.
- English article titles are established article by article and recorded here when a title choice is final enough to govern later consistency.
- Strange or compressed source imagery is not normalized into explanatory prose solely because literal English sounds unusual.
