# Tender Shoot

<!-- Tamil source: scan 42 / printed 41 -->

When one looks at jasmine wrapped in clusters of buds—ah, what delight! Seedlings in the field! A little fish in a many-coloured pond! A fawn in the forest—a child in the home.

Child! You are a treasure of delight, showing your gums in a young smile! Beauty that reveals the river of life! An unkindled lamp! A third-day crescent! Wealth among wealth! Rich gold! Red honey! Sangam Tamil!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 43 / printed 42 -->

Who but you can win a kiss for the foot that kicked and sugar for the mouth that spat? Smile—the whole world would pay its price! Cry—and to still it, the epics poured out from the tips of poets' styluses—ah! living paintings.

How many women, having come to hate elder brother, younger brother, father, mother and husband and wishing to end their lives, have you rescued from death—looking up from their laps, speaking from their wombs!

Your baby speech outdoes even the Kural that says flute and yaazh are surpassed by you.

I know how poets flounder, wondering whether they see your movement in the breeze—or the breeze in your movement!

I have seen even families ruined by poverty that pours down and authority that strikes them down touch your silk-soft feet and lose themselves in delight.

O tender shoot!
Rise!
Grow!

A good mind is compared to a child's mind. Your mind is lofty. Stainless. It is the radiance of a ruby,

<!-- Tamil source: scan 44 / printed 43 -->

with no distinctions—no divisions—no splits—no bends—no hunger—no fear—and one thing more you lack!

You have no thought of God either. You too are an atheist. A good mind is called a child's mind. Therefore atheists possess good minds.

## Atheists Possess Good Minds

“No, no! A child does not know good from bad—it is an age before understanding clears—a time when it plays with a serpent—and so there is no thought of God. The grown man, who understands good and bad and knows the snake as poison, sees God in his thought,” say the arguers I know, my dear!

Just as you play with the cobra, some grown-ups play with destruction—they worship God, praise Him, sing His glory! Does that mean their understanding is clear? In the habit of playing with the snake, are not you and they the same, child!

Paambu—‘snake’

Paambu—in childhood!

Paapam—for the grown man

<!-- Tamil source: scan 45 / printed 44 -->

How did God come leaping in only for them?

I have found the reason, my precious little pearl!

You—a tender shoot with a good mind; the grown-up, a leaf with a good mind.

You play with paambu! They play with ‘paapam’ deeds!

But—

you harm no one—and that is why you have no need of God either.
