# Hail, Mountain!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 39 / printed 38 -->

O mountain!

I would call you a jewelled crown, worked in gold and green, adorning the head of Mother Earth as she sits enthroned beneath the canopy of the sky where diamond lamps shine! I feel like describing you in a thousand different ways! And in just as many ways you present yourself to the eye.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 40 / printed 39 -->

When you wrap your body in mist, I wonder whether all the white clouds of the heavens have fallen down and grown upward into a height beyond the reach of sight!

O misty mountain! Another fancy rises in my mind: I wonder whether you are a ‘gosha woman,’ covering face and body in white cloth.

O mountain! They say you are a great saint. Some hold up your unmoving firmness as an image for sages whose minds do not waver even on hearing a woman's enchanting speech.

As though I do not know your story! When cloud-ladies come of their own accord and embrace your shoulders, you stretch out your cold hand to cool their desire; touched by that hand, those women swoon, cannot go farther upward, melt into rain, and in their passion wrap themselves around you with river-arms—have I not seen this with my own eyes? Is it not after seeing that that I thunder to the whole world that even sages who have renounced everything cannot renounce ‘that’ attachment!

O mountain! You are an insistent lover. And, along with that, a poet filled with intelligence. A poet gives his rising thought to the people; you too give the rain that has fallen upon you so that the land may prosper.

O king of mountains! If you did not rise—if across the world you did not lift upright your tall shoulders rich with agarwood and teak—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 41 / printed 40 -->

where would prosperity be? Where would life be? Long live your diamond shoulders!

Because you give gold and wealth, you stand as a great benefactor to the earth; because you serve the world, as a worker for the common good—O crown of the earth standing laden with fame! You are an artist too! Without you, where would the sculptures of Egypt be? Where the caves of Ellora? Would Ajanta, which adorns the world, exist? Would we go to see the splendour of Sittannavasal and the glory of Mamallapuram!

O mountain smiling as a sculptured cave! All the arts shining upon your broad forehead are lofty. Sublime. They lend beauty to the world.

But, O mountain, there is one flaw! You became the raw material from which gods piled up by the crore in every corner are made!

Even so, you stand higher than man! Though man has trodden upon your head, you stand higher than man!

Reason:
man's religious hunger—
‘God created the mountain!’

Yet is it not from a fragment of that very mountain that man creates God!
