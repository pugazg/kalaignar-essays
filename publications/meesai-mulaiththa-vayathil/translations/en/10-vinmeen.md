# Star

<!-- Tamil source: scan 46 / printed 45 -->

O star, you are a shard of light from the full moon—as though a block of that light struck a cloud-hill and shattered! Creation of a leaping flash of lightning! Scattered rubies from a fallen jewelled crown!

Nature, the master craftsman, has thatched the whole roof of the sky with pearl-gems of precious stone! You stars are the cluster of flowers that the moon-maiden, waiting for the bridegroom's arrival, has spread across the bed of the sky!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 47 / printed 46 -->

I have seen the sight of a turkey that watched a peacock of the forest dance, imagined itself to be the same, and spread its own ugly wings to dance. Just so—seeing you winking in the sky like diamonds spread upon fresh blue silk in a goldsmith's shop, a firefly gathers a conference upon a tree-branch.

It is not only the insect kind and the bird kind that stage this new competition. Shame! Alas, some among human beings too have become just like that!

“Flower-garland in a monkey's hand”—a meaningful, layered piece of sugar-candy speech! A bouquet given by a scholar! Seeing that, some people say, “Do we not know how to stack Tamil too?” They pile up a thousand words under the letter ‘a’, search until their tongues ache for words under ‘ka’, forget what they themselves were saying, and flutter like fireflies before the stars!

Painting, epic, prose, music, dance—and so many other stars—already exist like you within Tamil; is it only natural that fireflies should fly about upon the earth in competition with them?

The prefix ‘sky’ does not turn a turkey into a many-coloured plumed peacock. Nor does the name ‘firefly’ turn an insect into one of the sky's flowers of light! O yellow pearl of light, proclaim this lesson so that human beings too may learn it! O cluster of ruby flowers!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 47 -->

O flame of the jewelled lamp! At last I will say one thing—we sing your shining fame of light in all eight directions; but suddenly, they say, one day one among you sprouts a whole tail—alas! Then this great world that praised it as a ‘star’ mocks it as a ‘tailed star.’ Among human beings too, some great men whose fame has grown suffer from this wicked disease.

O broad lamp in the mansion of cloud! O magizham flowers scattered beneath the shade cast by the full moon! O rain of golden light poured by the sky! O golden flower! O star!

You are a painting seen by the earth.

An epic that gives birth to new imagination.
