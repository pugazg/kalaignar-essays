# Solitude

<!-- Tamil source: scan 49 / printed 48 -->

Woman: My dear lover!

Man: Who?

Woman: Who? Do you not know me?  
       How long I have been waiting  
       to see your gracious face!

Man: How many times must I tell you  
     not to come?

Woman: Why... do you not like me?

Man: Yes!

<!-- Tamil source: scan 50 / printed 49 -->

Woman: Are you mad! All last night  
       you lay there holding me  
       in a tight embrace...

Man: What of that now—come when  
     I call you. Go!

Woman: Fine indeed. Am I some celestial woman  
       who must come whenever you need me!

Man: Be divine nectar if you like—right now  
     I have no need of you.

Woman: You said the drink from my lips defeated  
       even that nectar! Stroking my cheek,  
       you said imaginings were welling up!  
       Touching my kanthal-flower fingers,  
       you told me a palace of epic poetry  
       was rising! In intoxicated speech you said  
       a rain of poetry was pouring down—  
       “Heap of ideas! Treasure of thoughts!  
       Fruit! Honey! Come, come!” You called me  
       with all your mouth, embraced me, revelled  
       in the commotion! You said we would enjoy  
       unending delight upon the open terrace!  
       You described it as flute-song—or the sound  
       of a veena. Are you, the very man who placed  
       such garlands of praise upon me, driving me  
       away with authority today? Lover, was all...  
       all of that merely bedchamber talk?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 51 / printed 50 -->

Man: Have you come to a bedchamber, you blind-eyed woman!  
     Look properly! Is this a bedchamber?  
     It is the guard-room of a prison!

Woman: Yes—but in this room, apart from me,  
       what delightful thing do you have, my lord!  
       Come, let us coil around each other  
       like angry snakes... Why hesitate?

Man: Do not talk!

Woman: The fruit itself comes—and you refuse  
       to taste it. The flower itself comes—  
       you say you will not smell it. Pleasure itself  
       comes—you say, “Stand back.” You are  
       a strange rogue—a novel bee—a peculiar man.

Man: What exactly are you trying to say now?

Woman: What shall I say that you will listen to  
       with desire?.... Shall I tell you how  
       your treasured children, speaking their  
       Sangam tongue, whimper and cry to your mother,  
       “When will Appa come, Aatha?”

Man: Ayyo—enough, stop!

Woman: Shall I tell you the condition of your wife,  
       bathing her pillow in tears?

<!-- Tamil source: scan 52 / printed 51 -->

       Will you listen to that?

Man: No, woman, no!

Woman: Then what—shall I speak of old memories?  
       Your first wife Padmavathi, on her deathbed—  
       before she could ask, “Give a kiss  
       on these lips of mine, trembling for the last time,”  
       she received it....

       That....

Man: Ayyo... no.... Go away!

Woman: Your family—mother, wife,  
       sisters.... children... friends bound  
       to you in deep affection... the father  
       who made your welfare his own self-interest.....

Man: Listen when I tell you! Enough, stop!

Woman: If you do not like any of that,  
       I will tell you a piece of news  
       very sweet to you; this country here—  
       it is an enslaved land. Do you know  
       how many true workers and honest leaders  
       are needed to free it?

Man: Ayyo—leave me alone.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 53 / printed 52 -->

Woman: I cannot—take these kisses.

Man: Let me go—your hand is a burning brand!  
     Your kiss pricks like a thorn.

Woman: Yesterday?

Man: I have forgotten it.

Woman: Last week?

Man: Forgotten that too...

Woman: Last month.....?

Man: Everything! Please, you too forget  
     and get lost!

Woman: Sir Kovalan! We two may forget.  
       But who will forget the picture growing  
       in my belly! It will surely proclaim  
       our relationship to the outside world!  
       The marvellous sign of the love-song  
       we sang through many nights—many days  
       is growing in this servant-woman's belly,  
       my lord of the eyes! It cannot be forgotten!  
       Nor can you deny it! The world itself  
       is going to speak that truth! Inside this prison,  
       where every comfort is forbidden entry,  
       the fact that one woman alone secretly  
       came and went to you

<!-- Tamil source: scan 54 / printed 53 -->

       will one day come out into the open!—  
       the “child” to be born to us will look at you  
       and surely call, “Appa! Appa!”  
       Then the townspeople will ask who  
       the child's mother is! And you will say:  
       this is the tender creeper borne by  
       the golden vine called Solitude;  
       wealth of thought; treasure-house of imagination!

Man: All right, if I must say it, I will—now go!

Woman: Tell the truth! Do you not like me?

Man: I will tell the truth—  
     I am sated with you—stand a little away!

Woman: Impossible! Absolutely impossible!

Man: I am going.

Woman: Where are you going... the prison door  
       is locked from outside... remember that.

Man: I am going to sleep.

Woman: All right—sleep with your head in my lap.

Man: Ada, ada, ada!......

Woman: Hee, hee, hee..... Victory! Victory!
