# “Ahimsa Vilasam”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 41 / printed 39 -->

The Travancore-Cochin region, which is to form the south-western zone of the Dravidian socialist republic, has skilfully carried out the necessary task of digging the grave of Congress rule. The honey-sweet news that arrives day after day explains this delightful matter. The opening chapter of the sacred task of bringing the mansion called “Ahimsa Vilasam” crashing down to ground level has begun in Kerala.

In a few days, the people of Tamil Nadu will be going to the polling booths. We know very well what verdict the Tamil people are going to deliver upon the authoritarian, unjust and repressive Congress. In our writing and our speeches we have set out not one, but a thousand reasons why the Congress must not ascend the seat of power again.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 42 / printed 40 -->

The Tamil people now bear the responsibility of proving the great truth that, when it comes to abolishing Congress rule, they are no less than their brothers in Kerala.

The man fielded by the Congress for the South Madras constituency to the Delhi Parliament is comrade T. T. Krishnamachari. We believe no one will be surprised that the Congress—the guardian of capitalism, reaction and orthodoxy—has fielded T. T. Krishnamachari as its candidate.

What surprises us is that, in Madras, the capital of Dravida, the Congress still dreams of making such moneybags and such ostentatious men—who, without surrendering their hereditary ways, trample, crush and grind Dravidian society under their feet—into representatives of the people. We wish to stress that the Congress's challenge, its open defiance of the Dravidian voters of South Madras, is precisely its decision to field T. T. Krishnamachari.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 43 / printed 41 -->

The residents of Madras are not such fools that they can easily forget the political life of T. T. Krishnamachari, who occupies a place in the front rank of the great capitalists of Madras.

They have not forgotten that, in 1936, he contested an election on behalf of the Chamber of Commerce against the very Congress for which he now stands; that he was elected to the Constituent Assembly through the back door; that he became a member of the committee for framing the constitutional plan; and that he played a part in the virtuous task of drafting the slave charter that stripped the Dravidian people of their vital rights.

No grateful Dravidian will forget the great benefit that the Communal G.O., the communal representation order, had been rendering to Dravidian students and in government employment. The High Court judgment declaring such a communal order contrary to the Constitution; the storm of opposition that rose in Tamil Nadu against the Constitution under the leadership of the DMK; the battle-ranks of Dravidian students; and, as a result, the amendment of the Constitution—these are events of the recent past.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 44 / printed 42 -->

One of the creators of that harmful Constitution is T. T. Krishnamachari. He is the man who has now come before the people with the “cow box,” asking them to cast their votes.

Because they opposed Hindi, thousands of comrades, mothers and tender children regarded the prison as a flower-garden where the mango-cuckoo sings. Because they opposed Hindi, Dravidian youths shed blood and bore baton blows and the scars of sacrifice. Hindi turned the Dravidian treasures Talamuthu and Natarasan into corpses. Hindi cast Anna and Periyar into Bellary Prison and Madras Prison and ruined them there. For the offence of picketing against Hindi, Dhanabhagyam, a woman in the final stage of pregnancy, was dropped from a police lorry in a trackless forest. And now T. T. K. has gone on a triumphal tour before the people preaching that this very Hindi must become the national language of Tamil Nadu within fifteen years: “Tamils! Study Hindi.”

<!-- Tamil source: scan 45 / printed 43 -->

At a time when Dravidians have not forgotten the greatness of their culture, in these days when thousands upon thousands of sturdy young men stand ready to offer their lives for the Dravidian language, T. T. K. says: Study Hindi. We ask you to think how wrongly he must have judged the Dravidian people to be able to say such a thing.

“The Dravidian became a slave to the *kamandala* and prayer-beads of those who, driving cattle and goats, entered the Gangetic plain two thousand years ago!” The twentieth-century representative of that lineage has now come driving a cow before the suffering Dravidian, who is engaged in the sacred struggle to smash that chain of slavery, and with audacity and arrogance asks: lease your cultivated land to us again for another five years.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 46 / printed 44 -->

We ask you to think how spiritless and shameless he must have imagined the Dravidian people of Madras to be, for him to have acquired such courage. It is a matter of pride that the Dravidian people of Madras have been given the opportunity to teach him “a good lesson.”

The man contesting against him is comrade P. Balasubramaniam, editor of the *Sunday Observer*, supported by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. He is not merely a man who has signed the DMK pledge document; when the question of Dravida Nadu was still an embryo, before it had acquired shape and beauty, he was a commander who protected and nurtured it. At a time when the Dravida Nadu question was still a riddle, his *Sunday Observer*, labouring without weariness, carried the song of our ideal to London, Paris, Washington, Moscow and Peking. The country knows the immeasurable service he rendered in the last anti-Hindi struggle.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 47 / printed 45 -->

In the defamation case brought by Achariyar—described as the modern Chanakya, Kulluka Bhattar and the Indian Machiavelli—he stood alone in court without a lawyer and, by the strength of his own powers of argument, planted the flag of victory. Today that victory stands in the law books not merely as a piece of legal literature, but as a crown upon the fame of Dravidian intellect. The voice of a fighter endowed with such knowledge of English and such power of argument must ring out in the Delhi Parliament. His rough and rugged, yet ringing voice must make the ears of Delhi's domination-mad men tremble. Delhi must cry out on hearing us say, “Behold the worth of Dravida! Behold the ethnic awakening of the Dravidians!” The Dravidian voters of South Madras have received that fine opportunity, that moment.

O Dravidian voters of South Madras! Every vote you cast for comrade P. Balasubramaniam—

<!-- Tamil source: scan 48 / printed 46 -->

—is a messenger you send to Delhi on behalf of Dravida. Each is a bomb you place so that the Congress mansion may collapse.

Therefore, if your faith in a separate Dravidian state is genuine; if the pledge taken by more than two hundred thousand of you at the State Conference under Anna's leadership—that we shall surely attain Dravida Nadu—is genuine; if your faith in democracy is genuine, then give your votes to comrade P. Balasubramaniam, friend of the poor, companion of the worker, valiant Dravidian, and make him victorious. His victory is your victory—the victory of Dravida.
