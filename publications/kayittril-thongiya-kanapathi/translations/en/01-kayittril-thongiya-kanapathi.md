# Ganapathi Who Hung from the Rope

<!-- Tamil source: scan 6 / printed numeral not visible -->

Imperialism that delighted in swinging Bhagat Singh on the gallows—imperialism that beat Tiruppur Kumaran with batons, made blood run all along the street, licked it up and trumpeted in triumph—here it is today, in the form of the Malayan Government, tasting Ganapathi too with its rough blood-tongues. The gallows keeps feeding on countless human beings and singing in revelry. Countless human forms have been caught and destroyed between its cruel teeth. Not content with throttling the throats of criminals, the gallows also finishes the lives of innocents in half a second and stuffs its belly! Do you know how many noble lives have had their histories written in the blood congealed on the gallows? If each severed sinew there—each bone flung and scattered—were to begin to speak

<!-- Tamil source: scan 7 / printed 6 -->

how many judges' ‘integrity’ would be exposed! An innocent condemned to death in the prisoner's dock—the dry-hearted man who heaps false charges upon him and, delighted at having destroyed him, shakes hands with the lawyer—the man who deserves to hang lies in the lap of a peacock-like beauty!... The poor man who knows not how to resist, in the embrace of the noose!...... That is how those innocent corpses would begin to speak.

He is a rich man.

The man next door is rich too.

One is a lakhpati.

The other a crorepati.

The crorepati thinks, ‘What if the lakhpati grows into a crorepati? Won't my prestige disappear?’ Or the lakhpati envies the crorepati. The thought grows. It ends in murder. One of the two walks to the gallows. When news of the murder appears in the newspaper, those who curse, “Murderous sinner!” do feel a little sorrow when they hear that the murderer has been hanged. Why? Did not those who cried, “Won't Mussolini perish? The wicked man!” and showered him with ‘curses’ raise a sad cry of ‘Oh, what a pity!’ when he was killed? If this is people's state of mind even when punishments befitting criminals are carried out, then when a man without guilt—one who never even thought of cruelty

<!-- Tamil source: scan 8 / printed 7 -->

—a man of gentle speech and a good heart—is to die unjustly, would not the news fall upon the ear like the thunder of a thousand clouds gathered together?

The news ‘Ganapathi will be hanged’ reached Dravidam—why, the whole world—and Dravidam sagged and fell. At the same time, consolation raised its head a little: ‘In a free land—as free warriors—with leaders of freedom—why should we fear? Why this vain anxiety? Our government will not let our Ganapathi die an orphan in Malaya,’ we steadied our minds. That was not all: trusting that our relationship with the Commonwealth would save Ganapathi, we wiped our tears. Before we could even lower the hand that had wiped them, we heard the gallows belch after devouring Ganapathi.

“Alas, Ganapathi! Beloved treasure of precious Tamil land! How did your life leave you, my son! The womb that bore you burns!... Even when danger came near you, I believed our government would save you!... Little did I know, my son, that Jawahar, Patel and those who rule Tamil land would regard you as an ant to be crushed underfoot!...”

—Mother Tamil wails here!... The gallows looks at her and laughs.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 9 / printed 8 -->

“Did you see, Mother Tamil!... You said that once the sons of Bharat came to rule, no one could touch us...... You said good times would dawn when the white man left... Look now!... I tear the flesh of your son and eat it. This is the food the white man gave me! Very sweet! At this very moment Nehru, enemy of the white man—is dining in the white man's country... I too am dining here”—the gallows laughs. It claps its hands and laughs; it laughs aloud. Beside it, the corpse laughs.

How many people die every day in the world. Do we grieve for every one of them? Likewise, how many are hanged.... But Ganapathi's punishment has a special importance for Tamil Nadu. Ganapathi himself becomes a lesson for Tamil Nadu! His life is a political class for Tamils. His last letter is a war address to the Tamil battalion.

“A flower blooms......spreads fragrance......withers and dies.”

A flower blooming and withering away are examples used by those who declaim, “This body is false, my boy.” But the flower also gives fragrance—that is what the rationalist movement wishes to point out. “Man is born, he dies. Going back by the road he came is the mark of man,” say those who are disgusted with life. They see only the flower blooming and withering away; the pleasant fragrance the flower gives, they do not even begin to feel

<!-- Tamil source: scan 10 / printed 9 -->

it. Even if they do feel it, they refuse to speak of it. Does a flower refuse to give fragrance because it is going to die?...... Would people prefer such a flower? Who welcomes a long-lived jasmine that never wilts? Because we are going to die, should we fail to do the work society requires of us? Should the flower refuse to give fragrance?...... Ganapathi was precisely a flower that gave fragrance. He did not sing, ‘All crowned kings burned and became a handful of ash,’ and build himself a sage's ashram...... Nor did that hero sing this Mukari lament on the gallows! “I am a flower that gives fragrance... I am being crushed and thrown away”—he turned that voice of his heart into a letter. His letter is a whip for those who desire ‘that world’!...

“Many have looked at me and said that I keep working without rebuilding a family that has fallen from a very high position. I have not worried about that.”

He mentions this too in his last letter to Comrade Sargunam, that noble man who laboured for the town.

How many come declaring, “I am the leader of the country,” because of money-power......because of influence within the family. They bundle up the country and carry it home. Elder brother, younger brother, uncle, brother-in-law, house, property—these alone are their dreams. Finding jobs for relatives—kicking away even those who worked for them and made them a leader, if they claim even a small right—this is an inborn trait of some leaders of high

<!-- Tamil source: scan 11 / printed 10 -->

families. But Ganapathi had no time even to look after his fallen family!... Even in the place he went to labour, he gave his life for the people. Even as life left him, he sang with fervour, ‘Long live the world.’

The passage below is the part of the letter that thrusts a spear into all of us. Yes! It is also a part of our propaganda!

“You know how I came here after our family was ruined. I studied English while selling bread and washing mangu vessels in Rani's house.”

In Tanjore district...in the fertile Chola land where golden paddy grows...Ganapathi, born in Thambikkottai, ran to Malaya in his youth for the sake of a span-long stomach. A golden house for Thillai Nataraja...... a diamond cap for Tirupati Venkatesan...... why, even the black-stone Ganapathi on the riverbank gets adhirasam and akkāra vadisal!... But...unbearable hunger for Thambikkottai Ganapathi. A livelihood in Malaya. Tamil land is a granary of grain! A place where gold is dug! It has seas brimming with pearls! Why should Ganapathi run to Malaya? One Ganapathi? How many Ganapathis...... Ah! Fallen Tamil land! You...have sent them to Malaya and Ceylon. Chariot festivals for bronze idols!...... Pomp and splendour for wealthy lords! For Ganapathis to eke out life, ‘Ceylon and Malaya!’

<!-- Tamil source: scan 12 / printed 11 -->

Is this fitting, Tamil land!...... You weep that your beloved son is dead......you rage......you wail and writhe...... Was it not you who drove him away? Was it not you who said there was no food for him!...... Was it not you who said he must not live in a good house!...... Was it not you who forbade him to touch gold and pearls!......

Now cry! Cry well! Cry until your eyelids grow heavy! Cry until blood begins to flow from your eyes!

This is what rings out in Ganapathi's loud voice, with fervour. Can anyone say it does not?

Ganapathi, who went to Malaya under British rule, after the song “Let us dance, let us sing the pallu” had grown old here, was felled as a corpse by that same British government. In the land of his birth, the conch-blast of ‘Goddess Freedom’! That sound was not heard in the land to which Ganapathi went to make a living. It was not blown loudly enough to be heard. Why? Is the conch cracked? Or is there no strength in the voice of the one who blows it?

There is no case that Ganapathi shot and killed any white capitalist. For the sole crime of having a gun in his hand, this terrible death. Dravidam was stunned by the verdict. Those who came to rule had no time to think. Because concern arose over the problem of raising their salary to a thousand! No blame lies with them—the ministers.

<!-- Tamil source: scan 13 / printed 12 -->

The tableau is not yet fully arranged. There a Tamil's heart is being split; here Achariyar opens the cave of the Pathala Lingam. Congress leader Kamaraj has not enough day or hour to finish glaring fire from his eyes and abusing Dravidar Kazhagam. In this crisis(!), that hero forgot Ganapathi altogether. “Have you hanged him! That is a very wrong thing,” he now roars. Kamaraj's heroic roar is like that comic bit where a man, after getting beaten, cries, “Will you hit me? Son of your father! I'm a lion, man!” Before his roar is even over, Veerasenan has been shot down like a worthless animal! Sambasivam has been placed at death's edge. They say that if you blow a conch into a deaf man's ear, he will not hear. If the blowers themselves have no lips, what a painful farce it becomes! Such is the frenzy of the Malayan Government and the Indian Government's chapter of appeals!

At a time when Kamaraj ought to be in Malaya, he is here wiping down the ministerial chair. Is that just, Kamaraj? Are Jawahar and Patel going to condemn Tamil corpses being stacked on the gallows? Even if they condemn it, will there be any heat in it? If they had concern, would the rope that tightened around Ganapathi's neck be hanging before Sambasivam? If fire had flashed in their eyes, would bullets have flown

<!-- Tamil source: scan 14 / printed 13 -->

toward Veerasenan? Having offered two strapping young bulls as a sacrifice to the Malayan Government's repression, then sending statements flying—doesn't that resemble grandly performing the final rites after death for a man ignored while he was alive!

Ganapathi or Veerasenan can no longer be revived by our tears. Yet I have no heart to sing, in Kalki's style, an auspicious refrain: ‘May their souls attain peace!’ We must not forget that Ganapathi is a lesson to us—Kamaraj included! What is happening in Malaya is a tragic serial in Tamil life. This story has crossed the ‘chapter of going away to make a living’ and reached the ‘chapter where the Tamil is unjustly killed.’ What will the next chapter be? How terrible will it be?

If Dravidam had stood separate—had been a sovereign state—would Ganapathi have gone to Malaya as a coolie? Would he not have toured there as a representative of Dravidam? The Dravidians would not have suffered the wretched sight of that worker-comrade hanging from the gallows. Why... even now... if Dravidam gains self-rule, one order!...... Dravidam's sacred children who went to Malaya to make a living and are being driven into mortal peril will be called back. Tirupati Venkatesan and Thillai Nataraja will pool their capital and build factories for the refugees returning from there. Madurai Meenakshi, the goddess, will convene a women's divine

<!-- Tamil source: scan 15 / printed 14 -->

conference and pass a resolution, ‘We do not need jewels,’ take off pearl and emerald necklaces and ruby nose-rings, and provide beautiful places for poor Tamils to live. That is the good work that will happen in a separated Dravidam. If Dravidam stands separate, the sense of self-respect will bloom luxuriantly. The bond of blood will not leave the Dravidian's heart. Those descended from kings will have no need to go to Malaya and Ceylon and bend the knee. Dravidians will live in their own sacred land. They can live. They are going to live. May Ganapathi's murder give the Dravidians this resolve /
